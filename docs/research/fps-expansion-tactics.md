# Additional tactical evidence for FPS map design

Research date: 2026-11-08. Eighteen distinct original articles, absent from the initial research guide when this assignment began. All were opened and their relevant text inspected. These are practitioner analyses and player interviews, not controlled experiments. Historical lineups, fixed timings, networking explanations, and categorical claims of safety are not transferred to current CS2.

## Eighteen additional sources

1. **[T-Side Isolation: How It Applies to Clutches and Entry-Fragging](https://dignitas.gg/articles/t-side-isolation-how-it-applies-to-clutches-and-entry-fragging)** — PeterHusisian / Dignitas, 2016. **Evidence:** Distinguishes individually isolating angles with movement from using utility to suppress mutually supporting defenders. Some site checks necessarily expose the player elsewhere. **Design inference:** Local geometry should let players change the set of active threats, through movement or coordinated resources. **Limit:** Proposed tactics on historical maps; not proof that every entrance must permit unaided sequential clearing.

2. **[Positioning and Movement 101: The Dos and Don'ts of Peeking and Holding Angles](https://dignitas.gg/articles/positioning-and-movement-101-the-dos-and-don-ts-of-peeking-and-holding-angles)** — Haam / Dignitas, 2021. **Evidence:** Contrasts exposed versus retreat-capable holds around Generator, Icebox Tube, and Breeze mid; describes lateral movement through Split to clear threats sequentially. **Inference:** A cover pocket is less useful if leaving it offers no second position. **Limit:** VALORANT agent movement and old geometry; its simplified networking and safety claims are not adopted.

3. **[CS2: How To Hold Angles Like Pros](https://dignitas.gg/articles/cs2-how-to-hold-angles-like-pros)** — Burair / Dignitas, 2026. **Evidence:** Discusses varying positions, reacting to different peeks, retreating after discovery, and using utility to delay or narrow threats. **Inference:** Position value changes when opponents learn it; spaces permitting relocation support adaptation. **Limit:** General coaching advice rather than documented professional match analysis, despite the title.

4. **[What is a Crossfire? — CS2 Teamplay Fundamentals](https://refrag.gg/blog/what-is-a-crossfire-cs2-teamplay-fundamentals/)** — simba / Refrag, 2025. **Evidence:** Separated teammates cover one approach, making simultaneous checking difficult; utility can disrupt this arrangement. **Inference:** Multiple defender positions are strategically productive when attackers can contest their mutual support. **Limit:** Introductory commercial coaching article; its strong claims about difficulty are not measured.

5. **[Tips on Defending Overpass's A Bombsite with f0rest](https://dignitas.gg/articles/tips-on-defending-overpass-s-a-bombsite-with-f0rest)** — ghazz interviewing f0rest / Dignitas, 2021. **Evidence:** Forward A positions provide information before an execute; Bathrooms permits peeks and retreat, while nearby players can protect routes and flash for each other. **Inference:** Corridor depth and intermediate fallback cover convert information into reinforcement time. **Limit:** Historical layouts and professional skill assumptions.

6. **[Unraveling de_mirage #2 — Multiple Ways to Execute A](https://dignitas.gg/articles/unraveling-de_mirage-2-multiple-ways-to-execute-a)** — raveNf0 / Dignitas, 2018. **Evidence:** Different smoke partitions enable different site territory and planting/post-plant choices; entry partners clear distinct threats. **Inference:** A useful site supports multiple ways of dividing its threat network rather than just different approaches to the same mandatory fight. **Limit:** Old lineups and rules; article overstates some smoke effects.

7. **[The Art of Entry Fragging: Improving](https://dignitas.gg/articles/blogs/Unknown/8741/the-art-of-entry-fragging-improving)** — gMp / Dignitas, 2016. **Evidence:** Inferno B example uses a wall to clear positions in order while limiting exposure; the first entry also distracts and provides information for following teammates. **Inference:** Entry paths should be evaluated for the sequence of exposed threats and the follow-up they enable. **Limit:** Ignore its fixed clearance-time recommendation as a universal target.

8. **[Lining Yourself Up to Win — An Aiming and Positioning Guide for VALORANT](https://dignitas.gg/articles/lining-yourself-up-to-win-an-aiming-and-positioning-guide-for-valorant)** — Resoca / Dignitas, 2022. **Evidence:** Compares corner perspective and protected versus unprotected off-angles; repositioning changes enemy expectations. **Inference:** Lateral space, distance from occluders, and escape cover create different kinds of positional advantage. **Limit:** Some geometric/peeking claims are overgeneralized; validate actual camera/body visibility and agent escape mechanics.

9. **[Improve Your Entry Fragging with Tips from HEAP](https://dignitas.gg/articles/news/CSGO/14922/-improve-your-entry-fragging-with-tips-from-heap)** — ghazz interviewing HEAP / Dignitas, 2020. **Evidence:** HEAP emphasizes making space, conveying precise information, and having a second player follow for trades. **Inference:** An entrance's capacity includes timely follow-up visibility and identifiable defender locations, not mere body clearance. **Limit:** Role advice, not a measured map-design study; utility prescriptions are situation-dependent.

10. **[How to Make the Most Out of Your Utility](https://dignitas.gg/articles/blogs/CSGO/9388/how-to-make-the-most-out-of-your-utility)** — candyman / Dignitas, 2016. **Evidence:** Smokes can create retreat paths and new site angles; convincing fakes deny the observer who could verify an empty attack. **Inference:** Movement, vision, and information routes are different networks; modifying one can change how the others are used. **Limit:** Historical smoke behavior and fixed values are excluded.

11. **[How to Retake the B Site on Dust2 With Tips From friberg](https://dignitas.gg/articles/how-to-retake-the-b-site-on-dust2-with-tips-from-dignitasvie-friberg)** — ghazz interviewing friberg / Dignitas, 2021. **Evidence:** Narrow retake entrances constrain simultaneous entry and are susceptible to utility; friberg discusses multi-directional pressure and selective smoke cover. **Inference:** Doorway throughput and the relationship between approaches determine whether numbers can become effective combat support. **Limit:** Historical strategy; no universal optimal retake established.

12. **[A Painless Overpass Smoke Guide](https://dignitas.gg/articles/blogs/CSGO/14108/a-painless-overpass-smoke-guide)** — Valkyrie / Dignitas, 2019. **Evidence:** Explicit pros/cons compare safe remote throws with the thrower's delay reaching the fight, and early utility with later fakes. **Inference:** A grenade-support position has a participation cost; throw distance and onward circulation jointly matter. **Limit:** CS:GO 128-tick lineups and exact timings are obsolete instructions.

13. **[The Weekly Team Dignitas Map Guide: Inferno](https://dignitas.gg/articles/the-weekly-team-dignitas-map-guide-inferno)** — Xela / Dignitas, 2017. **Evidence:** Describes adjacent Apartments peeks, fallback into Pit/Balcony, and two executes partitioning Pit/site threats differently. **Inference:** The same cover/route relationships can support low-resource versus utility-heavy plans. **Limit:** Historical team guide; its assertions about map preference and strength are not treated as current facts.

14. **[The Weekly Team Dignitas Map Guide: Overpass](https://dignitas.gg/articles/blogs/CSGO/11224/the-weekly-team-dignitas-map-guide-overpass)** — Xela / Dignitas, 2017. **Evidence:** Connector/Toilets/B Short control limits information and permits rotations. A pillar blocks a supporting flash from teammates while it affects opponents. **Inference:** A small occluder can enable support across spaces without harming the entry team. **Limit:** Historical examples, not engine-independent flash guarantees.

15. **[Analyzing the Major Champions: Luminosity Gaming's de_overpass](https://dignitas.gg/articles/analyzing-the-major-champions-luminosity-gamings-de_overpass)** — MnR / Dignitas, 2016. **Evidence:** Original match analysis contrasts safe CT rear rotations with the contested Connector shortcut, and shows staffing changes leaving Long uncovered. **Inference:** Route value depends on alternatives and the territory conceded to secure it. **Limit:** Small professional match sample; old statistics and inferred team motives are not generalized.

16. **[Setting up a Terrorist Default](https://dignitas.gg/articles/setting-up-a-terrorist-default)** — TheGenuineBuzz / Dignitas, 2016. **Evidence:** Distinguishes staging territory from sites, preserving flank coverage while taking a contested area and later splitting an objective. **Inference:** Maps benefit from intermediate positions that support preparation without forcing immediate objective commitment. **Limit:** Avoid its categorical claim that defaulting is always the strongest strategy.

17. **[Clutching a round in CSGO](https://dignitas.gg/articles/clutching-a-round-in-csgo)** — JKC / Dignitas, 2015. **Evidence:** Objective sound/fake interactions can force exposure; smokes break crossfires; retake entry geometry changes feasibility. **Inference:** Objective placement can make information, time, and position substitute for raw duel advantage. **Limit:** Old economy values and absolute rankings of difficult sites are not transferred.

18. **[Top 3 Tips for Support Players](https://dignitas.gg/articles/top-3-tips-for-support-players)** — Xela / Dignitas, 2018. **Evidence:** Describes replacing a lost teammate's role, angle isolation, and changing the expected position after a defuse tap. **Inference:** Strategic spaces need to remain usable after casualties and role changes; alternative holds gain value from what opponents expect. **Limit:** Strongly worded claims of guaranteed rounds are rejected; tactics depend on information and timing.

## Candidate principles and counterexamples

### Conditional positional value

**Synthesis:** A compelling combat position offers a legible advantage whose value changes with information, support, territory, resources, or remaining time. Local geometry should let players exploit, contest, or abandon that advantage.

An off-angle benefits from surprise but degrades after discovery; a forward corridor hold yields information but needs a retreat or support; a crossfire rewards staffing but can be disrupted by a split or utility. These are different manifestations of context-dependent advantage.

**Counterexample to an overly broad rule:** An objectively inferior position can still work as a one-off surprise. Therefore, every position does not need equal value, repeated use, or a safe retreat. Conversely, giving every strong position a fully safe fallback can remove the commitment that makes counterplay meaningful.

### Players should be able to change the fight they are about to take

**Synthesis:** Useful geometry creates opportunities to change exposure, timing, support, or information before a duel: clear sequentially, divide with smoke, reposition around cover, bring a teammate through another entrance, or force objective interaction.

**Counterexample:** If every fight can be freely avoided or reduced to a safe isolated duel, defenders cannot establish meaningful resistance and attackers need little coordination. Some simultaneous threats are productive precisely because they require spending utility or gaining a second approach. The design target is contestable difficulty rather than universally easy clearing.

### Options need consequences beyond the immediate corner

**Synthesis:** An additional corridor or cover pocket contributes more when using it changes future territory, escape, reinforcement timing, or objective defense. Mere route count misses those consequences.

**Counterexample:** A small movement option can be worthwhile purely for moment-to-moment expression even if it does not change the whole round. Macro significance is not mandatory for every prop; the contribution should be proportional to its spatial and cognitive cost.
