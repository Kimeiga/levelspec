## Macro assessment

The raised north front and deep southern interior create distinct commitments around the scoring court. The center kiosk lets a scoring group break a single upper sightline without abandoning the point, while its corners can also cut support healing. The terrace does not score and cannot reach the court directly under the stated ground-only mechanics; occupying both bays costs players and stair-return time. The repair/hall loop can pressure the opposite mouth, but the court force must survive that rotation. Those are useful 5-player decisions rather than an assumption that every route can be occupied at once.

The asymmetric spawn locations are a material round property. East can reach its tea stair toe in about 9–12m and the terrace in another 12m (roughly 4–5s total at 5.5m/s, before clearing). West's route through the north garage exit to the west stair top is roughly 40–45m (7–8s). East also has the shorter first route to the northeast scoring edge; west has the shorter safe hall access. These are estimates from the drawing, not measured paths. A 25s unlock permits staging but does not prevent east from taking early contact or spending utility before west arrives. Side swapping makes this an intentional match structure; it does not establish round fairness.

## Scenarios

- **Initial 5v5:** East can send two up ST-E with three toward books/court; west can meet them with two through clothes/ST-W while three stage at the west/south mouths. West reaches its own stair top before east can cross the entire terrace, so the drawing does not imply an uncontested whole-terrace sweep. First contacts are likely to be separated between the tea front and offset court mouths, with time to show presence and change commitment before unlock. Verify those first-contact estimates on the compiled paths.
- **3 court / 2 tea hold:** Two players can occupy separate bays but cannot see through TEA-SCREEN. Three remaining players must cover the scoring floor and southern threshold. A hall retake can force the court players to turn while tea has to move bay or descend to restore a healing angle. The central kiosk offers the retakers a local sight break; it does not make the retake safe from every angle. A five-player team cannot be credited with both full terrace control and three independent mouth holds.
- **4 court / 1 hall pressure:** One hall player is a warning/angle threat, not a guaranteed winning flank. The four court players must use the kiosk when tea is conceded. If two opponents rotate to clear hall, the fourth court player can contest scoring instead of automatically following. This creates a credible response without demanding a solo clear of the terrace.
- **Five-player retake:** Two can stage toward tea or hall while three wait at the direct mouth. The long route is a real time cost: the upper hall aisle provides a roughly 40–50m court-mouth-to-south-threshold rotation (about 8–10s before fighting), while the deeper aisle around REPAIR-ISLAND is longer. The defenders can notice loss of direct pressure, push one mouth, or retreat around the kiosk; coordinated timing remains a playtest hypothesis. Do not describe 10–15s as the minimum for all hall rotations.
- **3v5 casualty state:** Three survivors cannot cover both tea bays and score. Falling back behind the arrival screens until the next wave is reasonable when enough score remains. At 1%/s and nominal 10s respawn waves, a fresh team can lose roughly 10–20% while waiting and returning, so regrouping has a meaningful cost. A last-second touch or stagger may still be rational near 100%; the design packet does not yet specify overtime.
- **Spawn pressure:** Taking a garage exit costs attackers point attendance, but a single player may remain to score under the proposed rules. That trade is not sufficient protection by itself. Enemy exclusion and team-filtered protection are explicitly external requirements. East's west and south exits feed the same books-side area quite quickly; EAST-RETURN forces the south exit westward before it reaches repair. This merits a focused compiled view/retreat check rather than assuming two apertures guarantee independent recovery routes.
