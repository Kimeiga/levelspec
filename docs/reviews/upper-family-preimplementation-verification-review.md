## Discriminating negative controls

Each control changes the actual saved subject or physical surface contract and must fail the intended geometric/semantic assertion, not merely a hash check. First show that the positive subject passes that assertion. Group controls by distinct failure mode; do not increase counts with redundant point probes.

- Restore the actual old upper glazing triangles, including under an unrelated alias; detect both residual extent and wrong/duplicate infill at the new arch. A lower-arch preservation control guards against overbroad retirement.
- Restore the stale N-MID 7.745 family, or leave metadata at 7.225 while changing actual cells. Detect the physical mismatch without consulting owner-name expectations alone.
- Remove one actual N12/FE2 tread or receiving-floor patch while leaving native/navigation metadata intact; duplicate a shared floor/contact slab; shift a fractional contact away from its encoded cell. These distinguish absent support, wrong counts and positive-volume overlap.
- Refill part of an aperture in collision only, change a closed leaf/pane to masonry at the same bounds, or omit a pier/end return. Exercise full-depth and per-face semantic checks.
- Remove the finite canopy or HOME roof continuation, including a remote edge away from illustrated section lines. Detect missing overhead enclosure without requiring an invented hidden interior.
- Insert a narrow shoulder/head intrusion at the edge of the normal body's swept footprint, outside its centre line; alter support under a turn. Detect both body and footing failures.
- Swap profile axes, reverse the run direction or move one finite endpoint while preserving aggregate dimensions. Catch a Z-up/export-frame error and a wrong overlap plane.

Include a harmless rename/retessellation/signed-zero positive control where the physical contract permits it. Reuse package-level missing/changed asset and reload controls, binding the actual XML-declared read set; the R12 package contains provenance assets that are not all runtime dependencies. Preserve historical checker revisions; use a fresh output directory for every new checker revision and subject.
