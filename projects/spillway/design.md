# Spillway — tactical hypotheses and playtests

Start with the [named-space graph](audits/node-graph.md), [layout](layout.svg), [features](inventory.md) and [principles record](reviews/principles-record.md). The following relationships are design hypotheses supported by geometry studies; they are not observed human behavior.

## Territory and routes

Departure Court branches toward west preparation, the offset mid vestibule and the east default. Spawn areas are checked from multiple positions and elevations. Attached building wings shape the west passage, Elbow and individual site rooms. The Splitter's diagonal industrial corner changes clearing order. Central A01/B01 and purposeful low obstacles remain: architectural structure does not prohibit freestanding cover.

Mid control opens B-short and the A rear approach, while upper control requires its own access. Filled S02 prevents a direct ground crossfire through the stair side. The gallery floor itself bends, trading concealed travel and destination flexibility against access cost, corner clearing and opposite-end attention. Its low covers protect selected postures/directions; end and rear approaches remain. D01 can commit upper control into Mid, with landing exposure and a return cost. Actual falling mechanics remain an engine test.

A's +.60m platform changes eye height and entry sequence without overlooking A01's2.60m top. The east crest changes ascending/descending exposure and the relative height of E-L01. It does not automatically block a standing ground-level eye. A flat layout or a gradual section-wide grade remains a valid alternative for future designs; elevation features have no quota.

## Interpret the warnings

The35m and100° values are configurable review thresholds. The original spawn exposure and the65m cross-region Elbow bundle are repaired through route/architectural changes. The remaining59.91m A-stair-side/north-Mid→B-short/East-entry view is retained for prototype testing: neither endpoint watches its own plant center, and nearby rear/side threats require looking away or teammate coverage. This is not a pixel-only line; lowering the eye alone does not break it. The [macro tactical review](reviews/los-blockout-r08-tactical.md) records the actual views, costs and limits.

Six A rear-position candidates contain partly occluded gate samples. Local review checks plant visibility and actual S02 arrival heights. They are neither automatic camping proof nor certified safe holds. Hidden and unsampled approach portions remain unknown. Warning counts are not a quality score.

## Target-engine playtests

1. Matched5v5 defaults: record first reliable information, defender allocation, utility spent and whether upper control supports a timely destination choice. Compare contesting the gallery with holding its exits.
2. A takes: coordinate gate pressure with rear/upper arrival. Inspect stair body-before-eye exposure, A01 side peeks, follower trades and A02 against raised plant eyes.
3. B takes: combine East and B-short pressure. Check machinery-bay corners, ramp arrival and the broad rear-west threat.
4. Elbow: traverse both ways with pairs following, passing and retreating. Test utility denial and the direct East alternative, including clearing time rather than only path length.
5. The long transit lane: test sniper denial, utility and rotations, comparing contest, concession and crossing. In late2v2 situations, verify that proposed counters arrive before the objective resolves.
6. Postplants at each site: test2v2 and1v2 information, cover ownership, reverse approaches and repositioning without assuming equal solo winning chances.
7. Engine movement/body/camera, crouching, weapon protrusion, penetration, audio, smoke, grenades and roof boosts. Verify D01's landing, falling damage/recovery and whether return really requires another route.

No actual multiplayer playtest was performed. Capsule placements, constrained route replay and low cameras remain physical/optical evidence rather than recorded combat.
