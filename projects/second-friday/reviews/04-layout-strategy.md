Verdict: **accepted for blockout production**, with the compiled and target-engine checks below still open. The compact L-series reviews do not apply to this replacement district. This is layout acceptance, not competitive balance, measured traversal or observed play.

Evidence order: visually inspected `evidence/layout-B02.png` and read the B02 `layout-data.json` before reading `design.md` and the revised brief. Applied fps-strategy-design, its size/substance guidance, and fps-map-review. No other reviewer verdict was used to form this assessment.

## Strategic structure

This direction develops the requested outdoor building-first map. Carpet Bend is shaped by the offset B07/B06 faces; Copper's approach turns between B03/B06/B05; the eastern yard is bounded by B04/B05/B09; B10 and B08 make a separate north service choice. Their junctions, retreat corners, reconnections and different approach commitments give the district substance beyond its 136×108m rectangle. There is no need to populate every peripheral meter with fighting or to create a second objective solely to justify the bigger extent.

Auction Square is scoring territory. Roof control supplies information and selected support angles, while Book Walk and the two real stairs determine whether it can be retained. Deep outer streets concede contact time to change the next approach. With only five players a team can pressure two fronts and keep a scorer; it cannot credibly cover the whole outer district, both roof stairs and all point entrances. That limitation is a useful source of decisions.

## Section assessment and scenarios

### S1 — West yard, B10 and its two departures

B10 screens direct routes from scoring territory into the protected marker pocket, while its south and north ends lead to different street journeys. The north exit is a considered service wrap; it is not a second equally fast scoring route. A west team pressured on Clothes Street can use that route to approach B08 from another side, at the cost of losing immediate court influence. A team pushing all the way to this yard has committed well away from point and needs a surviving scorer elsewhere.

Neither the drawing nor the external protection assumption establishes safe egress against an opponent already beside the yard. Both ends of the protected yard can be optically visible from some close positions west of B10; inspect that pressure rather than treating two exits as automatic immunity. A lone watcher having to turn between exits is a different claim from one angle providing a lock.

### S2 — North clothes, carpet sellers and Carpet Bend

The B11/B07 gap admits a southward retreat into Tailor Street instead of trapping a lost west approach in its starting alley. The B10 south street also joins the eastern side of the clothes area before the B08 stair commitment. These are meaningful early plan changes. Low shelves supply staging but cannot be credited as continuous protection along the entire street. A west opening can show three toward Carpet Bend and two toward ST-W; if roof pressure fails, those two must descend or use the roof's other stair, not be treated as immediately available court players.

### S3 — Tailor / Copper network and the south approach

The southern route changes exposure as it passes B02/B03 and turns into the B06/B05 gap. RADIO-RACK can screen part of that commitment, but its support position and onward clearance need compilation. The deep southwest route reconnects around B01/B02; its likely purpose is retreat, recovery or a deliberate full reset, rather than an efficient first-contact lane. It is acceptable for this street to be quiet in a particular round. Playtesting should determine whether it ever enables a different decision; its footprint alone is not evidence of strategic value.

### S4 — East yard, B03/B04 and service street

East chooses a western Copper approach or the long road beyond B04. B04 makes those routes diverge substantially after departure, allowing a team denied Copper to reach the appliance side without crossing the same B05 corner. The six-meter service road is an intentional commitment: a northern hold can see down much of it, so its value is a different approach, not an assumed safe flank.

The close spawn-pressure case is important here. In plan an enemy around (131,20,0), just below B04's southeast corner, has possible same-height views toward the western yard departure near (116,10,0) and the northern departure near (133,20,0); B04 begins at y22 and does not block those short rays. The two directions require a large attention turn, so this is not proof that one player can win both fights. It does show that the statement “a close hold cannot cover both exits without moving” is not established by building placement. Inspect actual spawn offsets, protection boundary, exit counterfire and alternative movement before blockout acceptance. Enemy exclusion/protection rules remain external requirements.

### S5 — Repair wedge / appliance yard

B05 makes the eastern route wrap before reaching scoring territory; B09 and the furniture banks permit preparation and a choice between the point and Book Walk. A two-player appliance group can threaten the east front while three allies contest from Copper or Carpet. If the hold answers with two players, their departure should cost point or roof coverage; do not assume defenders keep all five original angles. FRIDGES and APPLIANCES are plausible sight breaks, with no ballistic or splash guarantee.

### S6 — Auction Square and four approaches

The off-center cupboard leaves a broad eastern scoring passage and interrupts a single uninterrupted view through the entire point. A 4-ground / 1-flank formation can keep scoring attendance and use the cupboard to separate roof from one ground threat. The lone flank supplies information or a conditional angle; it is not assumed to clear a pair unaided. A 3-ground / 2-roof formation gains an upper front but must accept thinner ground coverage and possible healing breaks at the cupboard/B06. Neither formation can cover four entrances independently with full trading support.

For a retake, three can stage at one street front while two move through Copper or Book Walk. The holding team can notice the lost pressure, push the main staging corner, or reposition across the cupboard. Synchronization therefore matters; an undetected deep wrap is not the default outcome. Verify actual approach-to-approach times and support views before describing this as a dependable retake.

### S7 — Book Walk, tea roof and both stairs

The roof is useful but does not score. The screen divides a broad west bay and a much narrower east bay; the western bay includes the ST-W landing, so occupation and access can interfere. Two roof players cannot be credited with seeing through the screen or supporting every point quadrant. Ground Book Walk can threaten ST-E's toe and offer reverse fire toward the east roof edge; Carpet Bend can contest ST-W. Those are credible plan-level responses, pending height-aware opposing views and full ascents. A ground team which loses the roof can choose cupboard/B06 sight breaks and score rather than being forced to clear the high position immediately.

### S8 — Peripheral reconnections and match pacing

The north service street reconnects into the alley west of B08 and can continue around its rear toward the eastern stair territory. The east service road reaches Appliance Yard / Book Walk. The southern loop returns through Tailor / Copper. These routes have identifiable endpoints and different denial costs. They should be evaluated as expensive resets and counter-approaches, not three additional fronts that five players continuously occupy.

The author's ~17–19s primary and ~20–26s perimeter travel are distance estimates, not measurements. They are plausible in order of magnitude from the drawn journeys but must be replaced by replay lengths. The 25s point unlock leaves room for preparation; it does not prevent early shots or an early roof fight.

## Casualty, wipe and clock implications

In a 3v5, survivors generally cannot split into separate roof/point/flank contests. They can cede outer blocks and wait behind a mass for reinforcements, but retreating all the way to the protected yard adds travel again. At 1%/s, a full wipe followed by an estimated 18–23s return plus up to 10s for the next wave can concede approximately 18–33% before the next full contest, without any further delay from enemy pressure. A deep alternate approach can cost more. That is a consequential proposed mode setting: a few lost fights may decide a round. It is not evidence that the map must be shrunk after the user's request for a larger district. Test capture rate, wave timing and recovery together in the intended game.

Near 100%, a surviving touch or stagger may be rational rather than waiting for five. Overtime, touch radius, contest conditions and delayed scoring are unspecified. No confident late-round recommendation or stall claim can be made without those rules.

4. Exercise paired/follower circulation through Carpet Bend, the B06/B05 gap, Book Walk and both stairs. One centerline route does not establish a two-player retake.
5. In target-engine 5v5 play, first test repeated full-wipe recovery, a 2-roof/3-ground hold, a synchronized 3+2 retake, and whether the expensive outer approaches actually change a denied fight. Then tune mechanics or geometry from observed failures.
