# Ninefold L01 — independent strategy / player-behavior review

## Initial artifact observations (recorded before review-packet.md)

Inspected brief, the three actual plan PNGs, sections C1–C9, exact layout-data.json and freeze.json before author rationale or earlier reviews. The raw data contains authored roles/predictions; those were treated as hypotheses. No sibling verdicts read.

- The useful strategic center is not a single room: ground Dye gives A-west and Water access; upper factory north receiving gives west-aisle access to B and the separate observation pocket. Their overlapping footprints do not make them mutually supporting movement territory.
- B is a bent factory volume around an actual lightwell, with three outer entries but only two immediate bomb-floor fronts: School west, East east; Boiler north must first clear Receiving and the west aisle. The core prevents the northern observer from directly covering the bomb floor. This is a consequential organization, not three equivalent doors.
- A has a south attacking front and two rear approaches, separated from the southeast plant by the kiln. Sign watches the western shoulder from above; it is not shown covering the plant. The A/B relationship therefore changes with control of East/Sign and Dye rather than merely with distance between bomb icons.
- The southern Tailor journey is materially longer than the School journey. Its S2 branch preserves the option of B-east, but a team split at spawn cannot assume simultaneous School/Tailor contact. CT can send an east B player through Sign and a north player through Boiler, at the cost of splitting A/rear attention.
- The roof is a committed School→Boiler bypass: J07 alone is 83.73 m / 24.63 s at the inspection speed before any clearing. It could matter as an early plan or held territory, but should not be described as a late post-contact/plant rescue flank without a clock budget.
- The lower routes provide a loop and a choice at Butcher, not just stairs chained to objectives. The factory underpass/well and northern Boiler court approach converge at Dye, so several drawn paths do not equal several independently supported attacks. A paired lower probe seems more credible than one lone lower player being asked to monitor both the well and Dye's multiple exits.

Further scenario and gate findings follow after consulting the authored packet. All statements above are drawing hypotheses; no compiled, timed or play evidence exists in this review.

## Verdict — accepted for the L01 paper strategy scope

The two objectives have distinct fronts, consequential intermediate territory and feasible five-player plans. The lower urban loop, inhabited factory over a through-street, separate north receiving volume and committed roof bypass substantively reinterpret the graph. No supported strategy defect requires moving an objective or adding another connection at this stage. This accepts this paper scope only; the separate visibility/construction gate and compiled checks remain open. It does not establish first-contact fairness, live reachability or balance.

### Scenario assessment

Numbers below are cumulative **polyline estimates at 3.4 m/s**, without clearing, stance, spawn spread or engine mechanics; they are not measured travel. In particular, times to bomb anchors obscure where first contact happens.

|---|---|
| Opening 5v5, B | A credible T allocation is two School, two lower (Paper/Well **or** Drain/Boiler), one delayed Tailor probe. CT can allocate one North, one East, one Dye, one A anchor and one Sign/A flex. T reaches D-B-W at about **18.4 s**, CT D-B-E at **19.3 s**, CT North Receiving at **15.5 s**, and its west-aisle position (44,49) at **19.6 s**. This predicts an early disputed B-west/aisle relationship, not an already-set CT crossfire or equal first contact. The School pair can hold the terrace while lower teammates take information; a five-player School rush compresses utility/trading through the same entry and concedes Dye/Tailor information. |

Tailor reaches the interior south-front waypoint at about **31.3 s**; the Drain route reaches A-west about **33.7 s from the T anchor** using the Butcher approach plus J04. The fronts can plausibly synchronize after deliberate lower clearing, but “Dye support” must be earned against the Dye defender; the roof is not another simultaneous support player. Choosing S4/Sign purchases bounded shoulder information and temporarily removes one ground body; it does not force a plant-side T to react. The T pair should not automatically send one to the western shoulder to duel Sign: it can concede that strip and keep the plant while CT still has to descend/advance. Sign becomes valuable when its observation supports a ground teammate's timed entry. |

### Proactive proposals and dispositions

These are **optional design opportunities**, not supported defects or selected experiments. Retaining L01 is coherent. Their consequences require another reviewer if adopted.

- **NS-01 — Give School a bounded work-aisle support option.** Compare the retained opaque x42 wall with a standing-height observation aperture at approximately x42/y44–46 into the west aisle (no movement link and no lower-well view). A School partner could contest a CT North advance while the entrant turns around SCH-RETURN toward D-B-W; CT could reciprocally pressure School and force the supporting player off the opening. This gives the northern terrace a local role beyond the roof commitment. Cost: North loses some isolation and the opening may expose the S1 crest or strengthen a B post-plant too much. Test both ownership directions, S1 mid/follower positions, and the two-person North retake before selection. **Deferred; do not silently add it to L01.**
- **NS-02 — Compare one developed Tailor service court against the repeated southern bends.** Around x54–67/y17–24, replace one small return with a loading court and a tall return screening Awning, retaining the unobstructed S2 foot and the ground A branch. This could give the Tailor pair room to gather and choose upper pressure versus A while removing roughly 6–10 m of low-information backtracking. CT East could contest the ascent, while a ground partner threatens the onward A approach. Cost: faster T B-east arrival changes the opening, and a larger court can leak long sightlines; compare staging, contact timing and return travel against L01 before selection. **Deferred; optional pacing/spatial enrichment, not a demand to widen all routes.**
- **NS-03 — Retain the roof's strong commitment, test its actual reason to exist.** Use an early School-held state with direct B contested, two teammates sustaining lower pressure, and one roof pair. Ask whether that pair reaches Boiler in time to change the opponent's next decision, and whether a CT can punish its departure by retaking School. Do not shorten it merely to equalize route usage. **Later play hypothesis; no layout change required.**

### Checks carried forward

Independent spatial review must resolve V-WELL offset visibility (the packet's 0.08 m center-ray miss is not exclusion), Sign's actual shoulder influence, crest/follower support and B's three-front exposure before construction. Compiled review must walk all ordinary routes and record contact/rotation times from spawn offsets; low-cover optical evidence cannot certify bullet protection, smoke coverage, audio cues or trading. Target-engine play must test North concession, the lower paired probe, A fake recovery and early roof investment under the provisional 115/40 s clocks. No missing engine capability is treated as a failed paper design.
