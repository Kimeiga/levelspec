## Verdict

**Promising, with a required circulation clarification before the developed layout is handed to modeling.** The dock and hall create two different forms of commitment. A is a lower destination behind a hull; B occupies a shared industrial volume beneath a partial gallery. The rounded dock head and side stair cuts are spatially substantive, rather than decorated square rooms. The no-crossing caisson is useful: adding a bridge there automatically would weaken the difference between the outer yard and lower dock.

The central D2 / west-hall interface is the most consequential unresolved strategic relationship. It may let one small holding group influence both objectives too efficiently. This is a **design risk and proposed experiment**, not a demonstrated dominant strategy. Height-aware compiled views are necessary before deciding whether it is a defect.

## Proactive proposal — STR-R01-02: separate the dock exit from the immediate B response

Current R01 relationship: D2 rises to approximately `(10,8,0)`, immediately beside the west-hall opening at `x=12, y=6..14`. B is near `(28,17,0)`. The west gallery wing at `x=14..19, y=8..21, z=4` sits over this same interface. The drawing therefore concentrates the lower-dock exit, B reinforcement and a gallery support position in one small sector.

Before: after discovering opponents near A, a B holding group can potentially keep a player on the west gallery wing while a teammate moves through the nearby opening and down D2. The upper player may influence both the dock emergence and the B approach. An opponent below may have to win the same interface merely to leave A and create pressure elsewhere.

**Experiment M2a:** move the D2 notch and ascent south to approximately `y=-4`, keeping its width and ordinary movement requirement. Retain the existing hall openings rather than adding a new opening directly opposite the moved stair. The emerging player then turns within the exterior work strip and chooses a north or south hall threshold. H2 and the intervening west wall can interrupt immediate information between the lower exit and B.

After: a lower team can show at D2 and choose which hall threshold to threaten; its teammate at A can cover the lower departure but cannot provide all the upper information. A B defender can contest the north threshold or leave the site to check the southward route. Gallery support should become more local to B, while checking the dock exit consumes a separate position or movement.

Sacrifice/risk: A-to-B switching becomes longer; the strip at the stair top may become an uncomfortable forced turn. Preserve a real landing and follower space rather than relying on the nominal route width. This change also changes which retaining-wall sector protects the lower stair and must be reviewed locally.

**Competing experiment M2b:** retain D2 but shorten the west gallery wing so it ends farther north, around `y=16`, or shape its west edge to prevent the same occupied position from supporting both D2 and B. This preserves the quicker lower-to-hall response but gives up the gallery's direct dock-exit influence.

Choose one experiment first; do not automatically apply both. Compare the same occupied A/D2, B bow and gallery positions. The author should retain R01 if the compiled hull/wall geometry already separates these responsibilities sufficiently.

## Scenario checks

### Opening allocation

S1 can allocate players between D1, the west service road and the south repair apron. S2 can allocate players toward E1/G2 or the external dock-head/G1 route. These choices are spatially different; they should not be made equal simply to produce symmetric path times.

Approximate polylines read from the drawing give S1→A via D1 about 65 m / 19 s, S2→B via E1 about 67 m / 20 s, and S1→B via the southern apron about 114 m / 34 s at 3.4 m/s. These are provisional centerline estimates without clearing, body routing or combat; they are not exact navigation measurements. They suggest two initially separated fronts, not proof of a balanced mode. The external northern journey must be corrected before using its timing.

### A occupied, pressure changes toward B

The hull allows a player arriving from D1 to advance around its shoulder without immediately sharing the other side's view. However, the movement ultimately converges on the open rounded head and A. A team leaving A through D2 gives up some lower exit coverage. R01's approximate A→D2→B route is about 50 m / 15 s before clearing, so the cost is consequential but not automatically prohibitive. The proposed D2 experiment should be judged against this baseline, not assumed better because it lengthens the rotation.

The high rim remains a distinct opponent response: contest support above or threaten the far stair rather than giving every lower hold another rear entrance. Verify that the lower objective is not exposed simultaneously from too much of the rim; this is delegated to the independent spatial/visibility scope and later compiled views.

### B occupied, gallery held

A B group can use the hull bow, the north floor pocket and the upper gallery as different positions. Opponents can pressure the open southern slip or contest a gallery access from outside. G1 must actually exist for the latter plan to remain credible. A gallery occupant should have a useful support task but should not retain automatic control of the dock exit, both B approaches and both ascents from one position.

### Casualty state: 3 versus 2

With fewer players, the differences between a lower commitment and a high support position should become sharper. Two players holding A cannot simultaneously inspect both stair tops and the dock-head road. Two players at B should likewise have to relinquish some information when one checks D2 or the gallery. STR-R01-02 is intended to preserve that attention cost; it is not a demand for equal defensive strength or a mandatory extra flank.
