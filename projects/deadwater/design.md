# Gameplay hypotheses for L05

The rig is a folded loop of connected working platforms, with A high on the northwest drilling deck and B low on the east process deck. T arrives at the southwest cargo dock. CT musters behind a screen on the north service deck. Sea voids deny arbitrary lateral shortcuts. It is not a sightline across the full rig: machinery, bulkheads and turns divide information.

T can choose Sea Walk to the exposed West stair, or Freight to Compressors and B. Taking Valve through O03 or O04 permits a second A ascent via SA or a B split through Pipe Bend. Freight/Pipe link O06 lets a stalled B team regroup without retreating all the way to spawn. Holding Valve does not look into either plant zone: it buys route access and uncertainty, while leaving the front at the stair or Pipe doorway. The first player clears the threshold; a follower has room to trade and stage. Four-meter O06 intentionally compresses movement and can be utility-denied; O07 and O05 remain alternatives at a territory/time cost.

A's two T entries terminate on opposite sides of the derrick plinth. The plinth divides the immediate entry sectors; eastern A still permits an exposed view of both attack doors. CT can hold the toolhouse side, fall behind it, or concede A and retake from O14. A west-entry player has a committed final ascent with a landing to pause; a Valve teammate pressures the other flank. SA is broad enough for a follower and resets at its head, but has no protected position halfway up against an occupied A entry. Utility is a hypothesis for that exposure, to be tested, not proof of fairness. Low reels/boxes are brief crouched refuges, not standing head cover. CT gets established before expected T contact by shorter path; measure actual paths after compilation.

B's south Compressor entry and west Pipe entry are split by separator pedestals and opaque equipment. A defender cannot hold both solely by looking down one corridor. Ts can pressure the west pump plant pocket while another pair rounds the south tank; dividing a team costs space and timing. The tanks provide a clear large-scale silhouette, not small foot/head gaps. The rear process stair is a commitment visible from within B; CT can stage behind Q-lockers and enter in pairs, but taking B's rear threatens CT's own rear circuit.

A–B has two rotation families: rear A→Muster→Control→SB→B, and contested A→SA→Valve→Pipe→B. Rear rotation is initially CT-owned and costs distance/footsteps; attackers can earn it by taking a site and pushing through its rear. Valve is initially T-accessible. CT must advance through A or B to earn the contested central rotation; the rear flex player initially rotates through Muster and Control. In 3v3 or 2v3 states, abandoned entrances become real choices: defenders cannot staff every doorway. Afterplant players must choose between guarding their entry-side plant and contesting the rear, rather than occupying an all-seeing tower. The derrick lattice and control roof are scenic and inaccessible.

First playtest questions: arrival margins under actual equipment speed; whether SA needs utility too often; B west pump plant sight from south; sound through bulkheads and sea gap; rear rotation speed versus 40 s fuse; utility-bounce and penetration behavior; whether 2v3 retakes have credible trade paths. Adjust structure if measured results support it. No balance claim is made without live 5v5 evidence.

L03 spatial return: extended CT-screen to close diagonal doorway views to the north CT offsets. South-facing weather baffles on A and Muster deliberately remove cross-sea shots into Valve/Pipe Bend; front doors remain the meaningful engagement sectors. A-reel serves the west-entry alcove. An exposed player on A east can potentially see both attack entries and the plant inside a narrow fan; this is a compiled review priority, not a claim that the derrick blocks both from every position.
