# Fusion S01 — organic layout and architectural composition

Verdict: **promising — develop A, Port-hall weave**.

Scope: sketch-scale spatial order and architectural composition. Inspected
`planning/brief.md`, `planning/candidates-s01.svg` and its PNG, plus the original
`references/vessel-0.webp`, `assembly-0.webp` and `assembly-2.webp`. No sibling
verdict or later rationale file was consulted. The task inherited the parent
conversation, including preliminary author ideas, so this is an independently
reasoned artifact review rather than a blind review.

## Comparison

| Candidate | Assessment of its actual organization |

The west Cleanlock → Cask lane → Hot Cell journey differs from the east Cooling → Bus trench → Control journey, while the vessel passage can redirect players between them. The west elevated route stays associated with one service face instead of becoming a symmetric bridge across every approach. These differences have an industrial explanation. |

A is not accepted because its silhouette is varied. Its useful distinction is the
junction between circular machine access and two unequal servicing programs. The
plan still has roughly west/center/east progress from the southern receiving area,
but the ring's radial exits and separate service interfaces can make the journey
more than three parallel lanes. Preserve those interactions when adding walls.

## Supported order

The source pit photograph shows a circular concrete enclosure, radial port cells,
service ring and overhead lifting clearance. The assembly hall photograph shows
large straight crane axes, regular structural bays and local working galleries.
Thus a circular core inside a regular hall is a coherent choice. No decorative
rotation of the building grid is needed. Repeated sector divisions are authentic;
repeating identical room/choke sequences around them would be an unnecessary
second repetition.

The photograph's huge machinery and small work decks support keeping the 19.4 m
vessel at published scale. The spatial distinction between vessel, magnets,
cryostat and concrete pit should remain visible in a section. An accessible
annulus outside the machine must not silently become the vessel interior.

## Concrete opportunities for A

**O1 — Make the unfinished side a shared working volume.** In the southern face of
the reactor, identify an omitted shell arc and the temporary deck spanning that
work area. Let the receiving/lifting aisle terminate in this opened sector. The
north arc remains a contained curved passage, so walking around the vessel changes
from enclosure to a view into the working hall. This uses the requested partial
assembly as a spatial relationship, not an aesthetic cutaway. Preserve a clearly
bounded walkable edge and indicate which missing parts are fictional. Cost: the
open arc loses concealment and may need a local screen; avoid opening the entire
ring to the same long sightline.

**O2 — Resolve the west gallery as an equipment-access gallery.** Keep its run near
the west pit/service face, but give its Hot Cell end a real landing and a deliberate
relationship to the cask doorway below. The same gallery should not overlook both
objectives. A recessed inspection/work position can observe a bounded port exit,
while the adjacent machine mass prevents a panoramic hold. Its return or commitment
should be explicit in the developed plan. Cost: extra enclosure at the landing may
reduce the large-volume view; compare that cost in a section.

**O3 — Preserve the different service journeys.** Keep a relatively straight,
wide cask-transfer axis on the west, broken by authentic work bays or shield-door
thresholds. Let the east bus/utility route wrap fixed equipment and widen at its
junction with Control. Do not insert the same alternating crate/slalom sequence
into both. Test whether the vessel's east port creates a meaningful side entry
into this utility space rather than merely another doorway into the same firing
corridor. Cost: the western journey may expose a longer line and require a specific
bounded crossing/support relationship.

These are development proposals, not compulsory independent features. O1 carries
the user's central premise most directly; O2 and O3 refine already drawn relations.
No whole-sketch rework is required before development.

## Next-stage drawing needs

Mark installed versus absent reactor sectors, exact vessel versus service-ring
walk surfaces, port thresholds and the west ascent/landing. Show the reactor
section and one hall section with real overhead structure. This is not a demand
for engineering CAD or final combat verification; it prevents a credible plan from
being implemented as several concentric floor rings with unexplained shell gaps.

No claim is made about physical reachability, balance, full source fidelity,
competitive timings or completed architectural enclosure.
