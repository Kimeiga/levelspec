## Spatial organization and route development

The developed plan preserves the relationships that made S01 promising. Its principal sequences differ in function and enclosure. The lower freight/pump wing shares successive walls and side thresholds; the middle ascent opens onto an atrium that can transfer into the exchanger wing, turn onto an overhead gallery, or continue through Cleanroom; the upper objective has a main stair, an offset western receiving landing and a rear command entrance. The rear bridge/manifold/switchgear route folds around B at a different elevation and reaches its rear stair. The galleries and landings are part of larger building wings rather than detached room boxes linked by narrow tubes.

The choices occur during advancement: Freight's P/L split can change through P-L or K-PC; M-H changes which upper ascent is reached; M-Q-S5 reaches B from a different side, while D-W reaches the rear network. Q-over-L is correctly drawn as an overlap without a level transfer. There is enough intervening room and preparation space for this map extent; the 24/32 count is consistent with the declared coarse room/circulation granularity, though small V and AW remain landing spaces rather than additional large encounter rooms.

The 70m occupied X span and 96m Y span are substantial for the brief. Large voids are concentrated in the cliff/rock separation and between stair bays, where the mountain installation can explain them. The rectilinear shared-wall equipment bays suit an engineered facility. The valley-facing lower vault, upper rock-side reactor, major stair bays and cantilever supports can communicate the mountain setting without distorting all rooms. Preserve scenic rock outside the playable volume, as the brief requires. The main occupied floors themselves rise only 12m; the scenic section and actual hanging supports must make the much steeper host slope evident in the model.
