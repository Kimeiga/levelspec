# S01 independent spatial-interest review

Verdict: **promising**.

Reviewed the user brief and `reviews/evidence/sketch-s01.png` visually before author rationale or other reviews. Scope is the first spatial proposal; this is not a gameplay, balance or physical-reachability acceptance.

The town has an intelligible terrain relationship: the Ferry Square opens toward the lower shore, while the chapel and CT town occupy the upper terrace. A westward journey follows the seafront before committing up the Cliff Stair into A; a central journey reaches the village square and can turn toward A Short, Fish Market/B, or the Town Gate. The harbor journey reaches B from below, while CT can descend from the upper lane or contest the square. Those choices reconnect at meaningful places, giving the map enough spatial substance beyond two named sites. The broad courts, sheltered square and sea edge fit a calm coastal settlement.

Develop these consequential interfaces:

- Make the B Return Stair top landing explicit. Its six-meter top edge meets the chamfered east end of Upper Lane only partly in plan; the developed drawing should show a supported, full-width onward landing rather than leave that join implied.
- Distinguish the tall town/building boundaries from scenic open ground. The current beige negative spaces could read as traversable terrain. The next drawing needs the actual enclosing masses or walls because they determine whether Upper Lane can see and pressure lower routes.
- Keep the long shoreline journeys readable while adding local encounter definition. In particular, establish the opposing views at the Cliff Stair and Harbor Walk/Boat Court threshold; their large length contrasts with the compact central square and will strongly affect the experience.

No need to replace the overall composition at this stage. Height-dependent views and construction remain open for developed-layout and compiled review.
