## Territorial and information consequences

The occupied exchange at+3.6 makes objective access a real ascent. G1 beneath it neither scores nor directly climbs to the circle. Taking G1 can change sides or threaten an opponent's lower territory, but still requires another ascent and abandons immediate objective influence. Closing the exterior ground belts therefore concentrates a meaningful cross-map commitment while preserving each home team's two ascents.

Public and service entries reach different upper spaces. U1/U4 offer preparation near west/east scoring thresholds; north traverses a longer gallery and kitchen band around the tall island, while south reaches a broader re-entry area. Four objective mouths do not mean four identical final attacks or four permanent defenders. The tall concession masses create places to divide a team's attention and also interrupt its support. The owner can hold one coherent scoring arc, concede another and turn to information, rather than promise omnidirectional coverage.

Actual public early/mid captures show the direct crest while the selected lateral opponent stays hidden. Late leaders and3 m followers acquire the side relation, with stance-sensitive exceptions: west crouched follower retains3/9 target samples while east loses9/9. Service climbs likewise see the top before the lateral receiving threat. A tank on the last steps and a support at the first step are not automatically a combat pair; the support must advance to the same useful angle. A crouched eye losing the target cannot be counted as uninterrupted healing or fire.

Standing aperture pairs provide bounded lower/upper information; paired crouched eyes close it. G1's offset returns screen aligned pressure while leaving its nominated diagonal. Neither a clear window nor a clear corridor endpoint proves the whole upper area empty. The lower operator can be informative without being a guaranteed flank.

At the home fronts, compiled samples preserve the intended separation of W-REG/E-REG visibility. Deeper near-door pressure remains. A pursuer cannot cover both shallow departures from the repaired cheap station, but moving close to a door may still pressure them. The friendly counterpositions challenge through the same local opening, not two independent flanks. Stock returns break follower support, especially until the east pair reaches its southern corner. These are real recovery costs; the source provides no invulnerable spawn barrier.

## Proposals, disposition and remaining limits

**LL-ST-01 — retain full regroup and closed perimeter.** The most useful test is full rush versus timed3+2, followed by a lost-point five-player recovery with the same starting waves. Record the moment the second front can actually support a scoring action, and whether the public trio can choose to stage. No new outer loop is justified merely because a pair loses G1. Status: retain geometry; target-engine test queue.

**LL-ST-02 — north support pause remains optional.** The earlier prep-bench idea nearx64–66/y68–69.2 could help a service support advance with its damage teammate, but would reduce passing space and strengthen an already distinct north foothold. Current compiled views do not demonstrate a failure that requires it. Status: deferred, not a selected experiment and not a gate blocker.

**LL-ST-03 — shared atrium clarity.** C01 has actual over/under occupation, but the complete G1 mouth is not revealed in a single broad atrium view. That may make the lower choice slower to learn; it does not make it absent or demand moving the source to fit an overview. Retain C01 while the purposeful curved X1 comparison tests changed arrival and parallel occupation. Status: P01 experiment-readiness reviewed separately; no automatic replacement.
