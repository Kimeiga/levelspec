# Design hypotheses / L05

Two arrangements were considered after the randomized seed: a summit objective directly above a lower objective, and separated reactor/data wings joined at a middle contour. The separated wings preserve clearer site identity and avoid mandatory stacked-site sound and defuse ambiguity. A fully exterior zigzag road was rejected in favor of inhabited contour wings with two interior ascent choices; the exterior silhouette still exposes the mountainside and cantilever structures.

The freestanding masses are a reactor cut into the upper rock shelf, the suspended lower data vault, and the arrival/pump wing. Shared walls and repeated equipment bays establish an industrial order. Q is a service gallery crossing above L/PC, not a magical connection; it shares a Mid threshold and descends to B short. R/N/U/V are one rear service wing with actual doors, not four detached pods. The scenic mountain is inaccessible and physically outside playable floor volumes.

T can prepare in Freight and choose Cooling or the lower gallery, or use Concourse/Cable to climb into Mid and Heat. H↔M allows a change before commitment to A main. Mid possession opens B short, A short, or a costlier push into the rear service spine. That value is a hypothesis; a CT push can contest it through D/W. A late T flank costs several rooms and climbing instead of instantly opening behind the CT spawn.

A is a taller reactor room, at+12, with three arrival directions and a circular central occluder. Main and short ascents demand different clears; low refuges at the mouths permit preparation/repositioning but do not protect against every elevated or side angle. B at0 is a lower, wider room divided by a data core. K and PC can support the southern arrival, Q/J creates a different east angle, and CT arrives at the north stair. B's western plant forces retakers to move around the core. Neither site is assigned a solved utility execute or a guaranteed safe plant.

CT deployment in the mid-height R service wing follows the tactical timing repair: the original summit spawn arrived after T at B. R offers earlier B access through N/U/V and an ascent through S7 toward A. Test whether the B setup margin and rear rotation are suitable for the intended round clock. D/W/R support a mid-height flex rather than an all-seeing summit perch. The D west-wall baffle closes the Mid-to-R standing eye ray while preserving a4m east passing gap. Tall walls, opaque spawn roofs and grade-following stair screens are the primary global-LOS controls. Distributed low fixtures provide local refuge; they do not replace architecture.

Playtest priorities: (1) both CT B defenders can reach useful positions before a fastest five-player B commit; (2) a Mid defender cannot cheaply cover H, Q and D simultaneously; (3) A main defenders cannot repeatedly isolate an unsupported first attacker on a12m stair; (4) B rear descent offers a feasible two-player retake after loss of short; (5) a1v2 postplant cannot watch every live entry from a single safe spot. Record weapon, utility, clock, populations and failures as well as successes. No ten-player playtest is claimed.

Final compiled repairs retain that strategy: Q-low joins its north wall to remove the narrow backslot, and S1-west reaches5.2m above grade to close the observed crest-to-T-spawn rays. The Q west niche is not a verified route or refuge; use the admitted south-side preparation position.
