## Population and objective scenarios

- **5v5 default:** 2A/1Mid/2B CT remains a plausible starting allocation. A defenders gain early arrival, while B defenders spend longer deploying. Ts can split West/Mid for A or Laundry/Mid for B. Mid loss changes both A-short pressure and access to Service/gallery, so the lone Mid player cannot treat it as a free-information post. Actual first-contact and support timing remain unobserved.
- **5T rush:** West's bends and Laundry's two climbs are deliberately committal. Their pockets/staging offer reset points, but smoke/incendiary duration and follower sight on the last corner or stair crest determine whether a rush can be traded. Test with real players and utility; no screenshot establishes this.
- **2v2 A postplant:** CT rear+east is a coherent retake proposal. A-press gives the site player a position whose protection changes by entry, while a prepositioned gallery player adds a vertically separated threat and a costly withdrawal. Clear the window as a named threat; do not assume lower low-cover pieces protect against it.
- **1v2 A retake:** Rear and east provide sequential route choices but do not guarantee a safe duel. Window/press crossfire should be tested with planted bomb locations and limited remaining utility. The gallery's committed access is a balancing cost to evaluate, not proof of fairness.
- **2v2 B postplant:** Treat Service/west as the dependable CT front. Laundry is conditional on prior control. The tank splits observation as the retakers advance, and the low crates create an interior refuge after the mouth. Test whether two CTs can support the first tank-edge clear before a planter can reset to the other side.
- **1v2 B retake:** A known west entry plus the rotation clock is deliberately difficult. The current geometry supports a testable challenge; it does not support a claim of feasible utility counterplay or reliable retake success. Record north/east holder positions and remaining time in the first tests.
- **Late 1v1:** A/B separation and climb costs make information valuable and late fake rotations expensive. Instrument actual remaining-time decisions rather than infer strategic diversity from route counts.
