# Fusion complexity expansion — independent organic and section proposals

New brief received after B04: make the map substantially harder and richer, with
many more layout choices, shortcuts and vertical relationships. Keep the ITER
reactor envelopes and 5v5 / two objectives. This is a fresh spatial-development
proposal, not acceptance of added complexity merely because B04 passed review.

Basis: the raw B04 source and its 32 section/support captures, original ITER vessel
and assembly images, the existing brief, and the new user direction. No sibling
complexity proposal or verdict was consulted. The author supplied an inner-vessel
section feasibility calculation after these alternatives were outlined; it is
treated as a geometric constraint, not a selected design.

## What currently limits the journey

The vessel gives B04 a strong circular central encounter, but most of the remaining
journeys are consecutive rooms: Cleanlock → Cask → A and Cooling → equipment/trench
→ B. The west gallery changes height and provides a local support relationship,
then returns to essentially the same A approach. The east trench changes the eye
height of the same forward journey. The rear service strip mainly joins the two
sites. These are legible, but the user now asks for substantially more overlap,
re-entry and changes in tactical level.

The industrial order remains useful. Crane bays justify straight transfer axes;
radial servicing justifies curved routes; shielded cells justify thick offsets and
concealed preparation. Complexity should emerge by making those systems overlap.
Rotating the outer footprint or inserting more alternating crates would leave the
same journey underneath.

These two alternatives revisit S01 B's wrapping ascent and C's assembly/service
volumes. Their earlier weakness was undeveloped occupied levels and equipment
adjacency. The proposals below give those ideas specific destinations and sections.
Coordinates are provisional sketch constraints, not authored collision geometry.

## Alternative 1 — lower pit crescent and split-level transfer rooms

**Promising.** Put most of the new complexity below the present ground-level ring
and within the work volumes on either side. The upper west gallery remains local
to A. The reactor continues to organize the plan through concentric construction,
while its lower maintenance access creates new ways to arrive beneath existing
crossings.

1. **Add a southern outer maintenance crescent at approximately Z=-2.55.** Start
   with radial band R=12.8..16.1 around reactor center (0,4), from the W radial cell
   through the south pit to the E radial cell. This lies outside the vessel and
   TF envelopes, inside the bioshield, and above the unfinished cryostat's -2.8 top.
   It is explicitly a service platform outside the reactor, not a second plasma
   chamber. Break its view with two radial service-cell returns so it does not
   become a single long curved firing gallery. Preserve one wider passing/preparation
   bay at a port, rather than identical bays around the circumference.
2. **Make the west transfer zone a layered room.** Open a lower access bay under
   part of Cask, approximately x=-32..-21,y=-9..3. A short dogleg descent connects
   the ground cask route to the west maintenance mouth; the original ground lane
   crosses above it on an actual transfer bridge. One doorway returns to the
   ground W-port junction and another proceeds under it. A player can feint through
   the familiar upper door and emerge at the lower cell, but must pay descent and
   re-ascent distance. Tall cask/bridge masses separate the levels' initial views.
3. **Connect the lower east mouth to the existing bus level.** Use a short rising
   connector from -2.55 to the -1.2 trench, ending on the transformer's south/east
   working face. The narrow ground bypass stays at 0. This produces three different
   arrival elevations around fixed equipment without creating three identical
   parallel corridors. The connector should reveal the trench obliquely, not aim
   straight down its full length.
4. **Turn B into a split-level work bay.** Descend from the trench's -1.2 service
   floor into a limited eastern portion of B at -2.55, with a ground bridge leading to the plant
   and a separate rear stair returning near Control. Keep the objective on the
   existing ground level. The plinth and bridge should hide one ramp's lower feet
   while leaving a deliberate view from a different position into the bay. B gains
   a local loop for entry, post-plant repositioning and retake, instead of simply
   adding another doorway on the same wall.
5. **Give A's upper landing a rear destination.** Extend a bounded +3.2 gallery
   toward the A rear-link and descend into the rear-service strip. Its work-room
   threshold should separate the site-facing perch from the rotation, so one
   upper coordinate does not own both functions. The existing A stair remains;
   the new exit makes taking the upper floor a circulation choice as well as a hold.

Representative journey: a pair enters Cask, splits across the ground transfer
bridge and lower work bay, then reunites at different heights of W-port. The lower
player can continue around the pit toward the bus trench; the upper player can
take A's gallery and rear return. An opponent controlling the normal vessel deck
does not automatically control the lower service crescent. Vertical decisions
are concentrated at transfer cells and sites, not present at every corridor turn.

**Section checks and costs.** A 0.25 m lower slab with top -2.55 has underside
-2.8, meeting the current incomplete cryostat top. Existing ground bridges have
undersides near -0.3, leaving roughly 2.25 m above this proposed lower walking
surface. This is a plausible standing underpass envelope, subject to beams,
stairs, rail toes and the actual compiled Boolean result. The outer PF ring reaches
R≈12.35 at a lower elevation, so the proposed R=12.8 inner edge has a finite gap;
do not quietly widen inward into that equipment. Below the cryostat top, its outer
wall and the bioshield leave only about 1.8 m radial width: that deeper annulus is
not a ready-made broad combat corridor.

The section drawing deepens B's local bay to -2.55: the earlier -1.2 suggestion
would leave only 0.9 m beneath a 0.3 m ground slab and cannot form a standing
underpass. The new lower bay preserves the ground objective and provides the same
approximately 2.25 m bridge clearance as the pit cell. Its rear stair and deeper
arrival ramp are explicit additional travel, not a free speed advantage.

The maintenance arc alone is not necessarily a shortcut: descent, curved travel
and ascent can make it slower. Its reward is bypassing an occupied ground interface
and enabling re-entry. Compare full journeys before calling it faster. B's lower
bay requires real openings in the ground slab and retaining faces; no concealed
walkable floor should remain over the opening. A's existing west roof accommodates
+3.2, but its new rear return must remain under the higher roof or explicitly step
down before the lower rear-service roof.

## Alternative 2 — upper partial torus spine and unequal wing mezzanines

**Promising, with the higher visibility burden.** Concentrate the new map-wide
connection above the existing ground ring. The open southern reactor becomes a
two-level assembly volume, while the west and east wings attach to it through
different work rooms. Keep the lower pit mostly environmental; use the trench for
local complexity rather than adding the full lower crescent from Alternative 1.

1. **Add a partial temporary deck in the unfinished southern torus at +3.2.** A
   starting sketch is R=4.4..7.8 over angles roughly 220°..340°. Its curve comes
   from the machine, not a cosmetic bend. It ends before the retained north shell
   and remains distinct from the true vessel steel. The ground annulus continues
   below it, so the new route crosses, overlooks and bypasses parts of the old
   route. It must not become a complete second ring with identical ports.
2. **Connect the west gallery through an asymmetric assembly-room threshold.**
   Add an upper bridge from the west gallery toward the southwest deck end, with
   a work booth or equipment return at its wing end. The booth makes a player
   choose between inspecting the lower W-port and crossing to the upper reactor
   route. Preserve the existing ground W-port. Locate the new bridge away from
   the installed coil legs and show its actual relation to the bioshield top.
3. **Attach the southeast deck end to an east service mezzanine.** A short raised
   service aisle over part of the equipment-side bypass turns north into B's west
   work face. It should widen into a room near B, rather than copy the west narrow
   gallery. Raise the affected east roof to support this occupied level; the
   current 4.8 m roof cannot cover a +3.2 floor with an ordinary standing body.
4. **Give the two wing mezzanines different exits.** On A, preserve the direct
   descent beside the cask plus the ground approach; the upper room can continue
   to a small rear stair. On B, put one stair at the south equipment face and a
   second behind the site-side work room toward Control. Screens and the large
   plinth should separate those upper stair arrivals optically. This yields an
   upper A↔reactor↔B route, with local down/up alternatives and site re-entry.
5. **Create a short lower bypass at the transformer rather than a new full floor.**
   Connect the -1.2 trench through one shielded transverse recess to the ground
   bypass's north end. Players can switch sides of the transformer under the new
   mezzanine without committing to all of the trench or re-entering cooling. This
   local loop can contest the upper stair's base, giving the high route an
   immediate lower response as well as its distant exits.

Representative journey: an A group obtains gallery control, enters the upper
assembly room and can either descend onto A, cross the unfinished torus toward B,
or return through the rear stair. A ground-ring opponent sees a selected part of
the crossing and can contest its exit; the higher player can support a selected
lower transition while exposing a different side. At B the upper route enters a
larger work room and has to turn before choosing its stair, preventing the central
bridge from simply ending in a full-site firing platform.

**Section checks and costs.** The current missing upper vessel sectors begin at
200° and the uninstalled southern TF stations create a plausible fictional work
zone. Preserve all nine sector slots and eighteen coil stations, installed/missing
state and actual published envelopes. A platform slab at +3.2 with thickness 0.25
leaves about 2.95 m above the ground route; no downward beam may erase its headroom.
The supplied inner-profile calculation admits approximately R=3.745..8.120 over a
1.8 m standing envelope at this elevation, but excludes floor supports, ports and
other machinery. It is supporting feasibility evidence, not a body-fit certificate.

The southern upper deck can overlook Receiving's staging bridge, W-port and
E-port from different places. Draw exact apertures and solid work modules to select
which lower crossing each position supports; do not permit the whole perimeter to
watch all three. Its baseline +3.2 height stays below the crane/lifting hardware,
but the source's exact cables and beam positions still need collision inspection.
Where an upper bridge overlaps a ground port, resolve the shared slab/header
explicitly; adding a doorway above an existing doorway without checking heights
would repeat the original gallery-clearance mistake.

## Comparison to develop

| Question | Lower crescent / transfer rooms | Upper torus / mezzanines |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main spatial identity | Radial service cells beneath heavy transfer crossings | Partial curved upper spine joining unequal wing work rooms |
| New decision | Bypass the contested ground port, then re-enter at a chosen work cell | Commit to a visible upper crossing, then change site or level |
| Principal risk | Too much concealed rotation; long lower arc mistaken for a shortcut | Excessive cross-level attention and one upper route controlling both sites |
| Section emphasis | Ground bridge undersides, lower radial clearances, B ramp exits | Upper/ground body exposure, roof heights, opposite stair/follower views |
| Source relationship | Clear distinction between outside-machine servicing and vessel interior | Temporary occupied structure within the intentionally unfinished sectors |

Develop either into actual occupied volumes and sections before selecting a
combination. A useful hybrid might borrow one local site loop from the other
alternative; adopting every connection would need its own new macro proposal,
because it changes who can arrive behind whom and how often teams must check above
and below. More difficulty should come from consequential route and elevation
choices, with recognizable industrial landmarks, rather than unreadable duplicate
entrances.

For the next drawings, include a radial reactor section at each proposed upper or
lower bridge, a longitudinal transfer-room section, and standing/follower views at
all new stair crests. Show the whole 1.8 m body and 1.1 m crouch envelope, roof/slab
thicknesses and plausible supports. Retain B04's exact near-W-mouth / A landing
qualification as a regression case; its original separated inner-bridge rays do
not approve any new upper route. Then compare objective ownership reversal and
the same journeys in compiled geometry before assigning a gameplay verdict.
