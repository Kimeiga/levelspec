# Santa Bruma: real-image study

This is **theme study for an original place**, not reconstruction. I searched before selecting the organization, downloaded the photographs below, and inspected the actual image pixels with `view_image`. The retained files are unmodified research copies. The design drawings do not trace a source plan and use none of these photographs as textures.

The image evidence establishes visible architectural relationships, not dimensions, hidden circulation or FPS performance. All Santa Bruma room positions, elevations, stairs, objectives, playable boundaries and roof heights are invented.

## R01 — São Bento, Porto: platforms toward the tunnel

- [Source and attribution](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Inside_Porto_Sao_Bento_station.jpg)
- [Image URL](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b1/Inside_Porto_Sao_Bento_station.jpg)
- Photograph: Dicklyon, 6 June 2019; [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). Local copy unchanged.

**Image observation:** Slender repeated iron columns support an unusually large continuous canopy. The platforms run longitudinally through that shared volume; rails sit below their edges. Beyond the roof is a stone tunnel portal, with steep retaining walls and buildings rising above the railway. The train curve and roof rhythm give depth and orientation without needing a sequence of room doors.

**Translation into the invention:** Keep the rail shed a large enclosing volume, not several platform rooms. Give the scenic line a reason to terminate at a cutting/tunnel. Preserve the difference between platform and rail heights. The photo does not establish Santa Bruma's +4.80 m datum, its two-track choice or its lower subway.

## R02 — Rossio, Lisbon: the shed interior

- [Source and attribution](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Esta%C3%A7%C3%A3o_do_Rossio,_Lisboa,_2016_(16).jpg)
- [Image URL](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/37/Esta%C3%A7%C3%A3o_do_Rossio%2C_Lisboa%2C_2016_%2816%29.jpg)
- Photograph: Draceane, 29 October 2016; [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). Local copy unchanged.

**Image observation:** Long rows of thin columns and a high iron-and-glass roof lead toward a tall masonry end wall with a large bright arch. Lower solid side walls, signs, roof ribs and train bodies operate at very different scales within the same hall. The platforms have substantial length and breadth; the roof reads as enclosure rather than a decorative strip over an outdoor path.

**Translation:** Santa Bruma needs real roof height, support rhythm and a generous head-end volume. Its roof is included in the sections even though the plans remove roofs for inspection. The passenger headhouse and rail shed can be distinct structural systems. Exact roof shape, bay spacing, rolling stock and every dimension remain invented.

## R03 — Rossio: the city-facing facade

- [Source page](https://railwaystations.blogspot.com/2011/07/portugal-lisbon-rossio-railway-station.html)
- [Image URL](https://3.bp.blogspot.com/-V_mJpAfUIBs/ThtR6-AyJnI/AAAAAAAAAzI/tZRCERrIs_A/s1600/Portugal%2Btrain%2Bstation%2BEstacao%2Bde%2BRossio%2BLisboa%2BDSCN3761.JPG)
- Credit: railwaystations.blogspot.com source photograph; specific photographer/reuse license not established.

**Image observation:** A substantial stone street facade with large entrances, upper windows, a clock and a parapet addresses a busy pedestrian street. It reads as a civic building, quite unlike the exposed iron shed in R02. This view alone does not show the interior stair or the rail-to-street height difference.

**Translation:** Give Santa Bruma a city-facing masonry headhouse with a genuine tall interior, rather than exposing the rail canopy at every edge. I am not copying Rossio's carved facade.

**Separate textual corroboration:** [Open House Lisboa's Rossio description](https://2015.openhouselisboa.com/en/places/estacao-ferroviaria-do-rossio/index.html) describes platforms above the main entrance and historic access ramps. That supports exploring a station that negotiates urban levels. Santa Bruma's modest 4.8 m difference is my invention; neither this facade photo nor the text establishes its layout.

## R04 — Wemyss Bay: ticket hall / concourse

- [Architect source page](https://www.reiachandhall.co.uk/public/wemyss-bay)
- [Image URL](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/64ccc14e39bcdb46fbacd29e/c83265be-4ed8-4039-b2f3-74c258527fb6/3135_N2_website.jpg)
- Credit: Reiach and Hall Architects project page; specific photo credit/reuse license not established.

**Image observation:** A substantial central booking kiosk divides circulation under a continuous curving glazed roof. Separate roofed directions fan away around it. The kiosk and roof meet as a piece of architecture; the floor around them widens into a distributing space rather than staying the width of a passage.

**Translation:** Allow a circulation element to become the principal public room and let a functional island shape movement. Santa Bruma uses a tall angular hall and an occupied gallery, not Wemyss's circular plan. Its small ticket island is subordinate to the volume and is not a prescribed combat obstacle.

## R05 — Wemyss Bay: aerial of station, pier and coast

- [Network Rail image page](https://www.networkrailmediacentre.co.uk/resources/53wzt-tx72y-4ub06-4axof-ifb9u)
- [Image URL](https://cdn.prgloo.com/media/d2bbc9a74afd425aa968f3f2f354ff60.jpg?height=960&width=1135)
- Credit: Network Rail Media Centre; specific photo credit/reuse license not established.

**Image observation:** The railway follows the narrow strip between shore and higher land. Curving platforms feed the headhouse, and a covered route bends away onto the ferry pier. The road occupies the landward side. Pier, railway, headhouse and street have different alignments and dimensions but form a continuous transport interchange.

**Translation:** This changed the preferred organization: a waterfront transfer journey can be a major part of the station instead of an unrelated approach corridor. Santa Bruma puts rail above the quay and makes the town and railway grids collide at the headhouse, both invented departures.

[Historic Environment Scotland's listing](https://portal.historicenvironment.scot/apex/f?p=1505%3A300%3A%3A%3A%3A%3AVIEWTYPE%2CVIEWREF%3Adesignation%2CLB12473) independently describes the concourse, curved pier ramp and platform canopies. No portion of Santa Bruma claims fidelity to that building.

## R06 — Bricklayer's Arms: goods loading-bank interior

- [Source and attribution](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bricklayers_arms_goods_shed_1895307_0cf1e484.jpg)
- [Image URL](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/20/Bricklayers_arms_goods_shed_1895307_0cf1e484.jpg)
- Photograph: Ben Brooksbank, 30 April 1959, via Geograph; [CC BY-SA 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0/). Local copy unchanged.

**Image observation:** Repeated structural columns and overhead working equipment coexist with clear longitudinal work space. Stacked goods and loading-bank edges shape useful occupied zones rather than occupying the floor uniformly. Industrial roof structure and work circulation remain legible despite the cargo.

**Translation:** The parcel hall should have purposeful sorting fixtures and a work aisle tied to its interfaces, not scattered crates. Santa Bruma's weigh office, sorting bench, cage and mail rack are invented pieces of that process. The reference's much larger depot is not the proposed station's floor plan or scale.

## R07 — Coventry: goods shed road-loading interface

- [Source page](https://warwickshirerailways.com/lms/lnwrcov569.htm)
- [Image URL](https://warwickshirerailways.com/lms/lnwr/coventry/station/lnwrcov569.jpg)
- Credit: Coventry Evening Telegraph photograph, November 1960, reproduced by Warwickshire Railways; reuse license not established.

**Image observation:** Road vehicles, a rail wagon and loading structures share one working yard. A broad roof projects over the vehicle-handling area, while the masonry building and open-sided structure give different edges to the same operation. The vehicle approach remains a real space with turning/depth rather than a door that immediately meets another room.

**Translation / targeted return lookup:** Inspected during r02 to develop A's interface. Two drawn cart doors now meet marked apron bays, and the sorting aisle reconnects customs, dispatch and the apron. The map uses a smaller flush cart-loading operation; the photo does not prove those threshold heights, door widths or handling technology.

## What remains deliberately invented

The fictional Portuguese estuary site, diagonal rail podium, two occupied levels, tall booking hall/gallery, all four stair connections, under-rail luggage subway, mail bay over rivet court, parcel-hoist relationship, two bomb anchors and team starts are design choices. The photographs informed the building's spatial grammar and processes. They neither establish its exact construction nor validate a competitive layout.
