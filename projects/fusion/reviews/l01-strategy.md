## Approach and response

Receiving offers three different commitments. Cleanlock and Cask lead toward A;
Cooling leads toward B; S Bridge commits to the reactor and preserves a later choice
between ports. The apron connects the southern staging space without making the
machine pit an alternate lane. The ring therefore remains the principal central
transfer rather than one of two interchangeable concentric circuits.

For a concrete 5v5 test, assign two attackers to the western journey, one to a vessel
probe and two to the eastern journey. This is a test allocation, not a prescribed
execute. One western player can acquire the gallery while the other stages at the
Cask approach; the vessel player can join W port, wait for support, or change the
pressure toward B. If the probe meets a pair in the ring, attacking an outer site
with fewer defenders is a plausible response. Defender forward control therefore
has a coverage cost elsewhere.

A defender allocation with two near each site and one rear flex player can respond
to these pressures, but it must choose which information to trust. The rear band
links both sites without granting a drawn central view of both plant anchors.
Its exits require local entry contests. A defender advancing into the reactor is
giving up that protected response position in exchange for earlier information
and pressure on S Bridge. First-contact timing still needs actual movement checks;
the drawing does not establish who safely reaches each port first.

## Site-entry diversity and local holds

**A:** Cask's southern gap and the W-port arrival at the southeast work bay are
distinct before the a-cask mass. The rear gap adds a different approach. The gallery
can connect western movement and bounded support while its screen separates the
port-facing and A-facing jobs. A defender using the cask can change side as the
south or port entry becomes urgent; a coordinated attacker can challenge the hold
from different directions. Exact mutual support is an explicit compiled check,
particularly at the W-port's narrow overlap with the site floor.

**B:** the port bay and trench arrival both approach the southern part of Power
Hall, but they enter on opposite sides of the b-plinth's influence. They are not
yet shown to be equivalent final-door funnels. The ground equipment-side bypass
gives a player a way to avoid the trench's lower stance and ramp commitment, while
remaining exposed to port control. This is a useful difference to retain. Inspect
whether the plinth actually separates their threats and whether a follower can
support a ramping player; no extra entrance is justified by this drawing alone.

The rear screens, casks and plinth must be inspected from both standing and crouched
opponents. A nominal screen height or a blocked anchor ray is not proof that the
whole objective interaction is protected. The rear-circulation/no-plant-view claim
is accepted as a drawing intention and remains a specific blockout test.

## Ring transfer versus rear rotation

The northern and southern vessel arcs have different access relationships despite
their common radius. The southern arc contains S Bridge's arrival; taking the north
arc can bypass that junction while changing port pressure. The thick inner machine
mass prevents assuming a direct shot across the central hole. Curved views along
the annulus still need inspection, and the open shell sector is not automatically
safe merely because its plan is curved.

Rear Services is a route for response from retained territory. The reactor route
requires a usable port and exposes the team to contested forward territory; after
planting it can become an attacker transfer or an alternative retake approach.
These are meaningful differences. Do not tune both toward equal travel time or
equal usage. Compare a genuine hit, a fake and a post-plant response, including the
time to acquire information and prepare at the destination rather than running
distance alone.

## Information age and disrupted support

The gallery's W-port view can provide actionable arrival information to a teammate
already near the ground entry. The observer's own stair return is a different,
slower action. Preserve that distinction in the test: record the last observation,
the ground player's commitment and whether the target could change position unseen.
The dedicated upper aperture need only support its named local job; no view deep
into the vessel is assumed.

Remove or suppress the gallery player and the ground attacker loses that observation
and possible firing support. The remaining player can delay, recheck locally,
regroup with W-port support or switch destination through the ring if accessible.
The map need not let them execute the same clear unchanged. A lost vessel partner
similarly makes a solo ring traverse more uncertain; an outer route can become the
better plan. These are useful changed decisions rather than automatic failures.

## Two-versus-two after planting

Two separated retake arrivals are plausible when survivors already hold different
territories—for example, one at Rear Services and one near a captured port. Each
must relinquish coverage elsewhere to support the retake. Neither player can also
watch every ring mouth, and the north arc can represent a credible attacker return.

**Do not claim a fast two-front retake from every starting state.** If both defenders
begin in Rear Services, routing one through the other site, its port and a substantial
reactor arc costs much more than approaching the local rear entry together. It may
be too late under the eventual round clock. A paired rear clear, a deliberate wait
for information or accepting a disadvantage can be the rational response. Do not
add a fourth rear-to-reactor port merely to guarantee a second fast arrival.

The equivalent question applies to a planted B objective. Test survivors already
at different approaches separately from two survivors in the same rear position.
Actual interaction duration and remaining clock are required before calling any
of these responses feasible in a live round.

## Abandoning strong positions

The west gallery offers a support job but does not automatically control the opposite
site or both sides of A at once. A B commitment, loss of ground support, or pressure
on its access can make leaving preferable. Its stairs impose a return cost that
matters if it is later reacquired. The gallery can also be deliberately bypassed
in a late retake. Do not infer uselessness from infrequent occupancy.

The cask/plinth holds and vessel junction likewise lose value when the relevant
arrival is conceded or the objective shifts elsewhere. No safe escape is required
for every committed ambush. Compiled views should test whether any of these positions
actually retains nearly universal influence despite the intended divisions.
