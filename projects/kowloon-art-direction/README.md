# Kowloon art direction

A shared visual reference set for the building, storefront, wall and floor work.
Start with [the visual board](board.html) or [its saved image](board-v6.png).
All nineteen selected images are preserved here; the original project files remain
in place. The five material studies, five component sheets, four ground images and
three furnishing sheets were created with built-in ImageGen using the saved concepts
as style references. Earlier boards remain available as [board 01](board.png),
[board 02](board-v2.png), [board 03](board-v3.png), [board 04](board-v4.png) and
[board 05](board-v5.png).

**Current ground direction: hardcore cyberpunk Kowloon.** The user requested much
more tattered and physically broken paths. [Severe-damage studies](ground/13-broken-paths.png)
and the [alley ground concept](ground/14-cyberpunk-alley-ground.png) now lead the path
direction. Earlier restrained paving remains as a construction/material baseline.

The sheets pair whole assemblies with close construction studies. Use their
material changes and design variety as references: sign faces versus galvanized
edges, handled door hardware versus painted panels, older pipe coatings versus
replacement fittings. Wear belongs at joints, contact points and water paths.
Keep regular geometry, lettering and working mechanisms authored when adapting
them. Generated lettering, folding mechanisms and plumbing connections are
illustrative and need reconstruction checks before adoption.

Window studies emphasize recessed glass, layered tracks and frame profiles; a
shallow sill cage differs from a walkable balcony. Balcony studies pair daily-life
arrangements with access openings, floor depth, wall connections and continuous
guards. Preserve that distinction when adapting them. Opening sweeps, supports,
drainage and structural dimensions still need authored geometry checks; the
illustrations establish visual direction, not verified construction.

| [14 · Cyberpunk alley ground — current target](ground/14-cyberpunk-alley-ground.png) | Ground-focused alley composition with overlapping failed repairs, metal bridging plates, oily water, tattered debris and dense overhead infrastructure. | [Exact prompt](prompts/14-cyberpunk-alley-ground.txt) |

The earlier ground concepts use downward views to show construction joints,
contact between materials and local wear. Their concrete remains mostly calm
with fine gray variation; wetness gathers beside drainage. Tile families differ in unit size, pattern and
surface response; foot traffic softens the center while damage stays localized.
The gray tiled repair reads as replacement units, rather than one uninterrupted
cement pour. Threshold and drain heights are illustrative: fit those transitions
explicitly when implementing. These perspective sheets are art references, not
direct texture maps or measured drainage specifications.

The severe pass changes actual surface breakup: displaced slabs, missing chunks,
exposed sublayers, failed repairs and depressed drainage edges, alongside wetness
and debris. Its physical damage must read at scene distance. Density, layered
infrastructure and small warm/cool light reflections establish the cyberpunk mood.
Preserve a readable irregular walking line amid clustered failures. Avoid reducing
this direction to fine crack decals on an otherwise flat, tidy pavement. The
images are aspirational references; no production geometry was changed.

These extend the heavier Kowloon wear into small shop props: directional wood
grain and end grain, splintered corners, darker damp feet, hand-polished edges,
water rings, mismatched replacement boards, torn labels and localized rusty

adapting the concepts; boards should not share one generic repeating texture.

The cart sheet received a targeted image edit to remove the initial generation's
floor-anchor plates and bolts from parking legs. The chosen image has freely
resting legs with close-fitting protective shoes. The initial result is retained
under ignored `generated/kowloon-art-direction/` and recorded in provenance.

## Shared direction

- Begin with a freely composed scene concept to explore identity, silhouette,
  activity and material hierarchy; use a matched render study later for comparison.
- Use strong damage hierarchy. For the current ground direction, include substantial
  missing material and displaced edges; retain enough quieter surface to read the
  construction. Concentrate failures at water paths, utility cuts and worn thresholds,
  and distinguish overlapping repairs by age.
- Separate material layers: fine plaster over coarse concrete; ceramic over mortar.
  Surface loss should expose the right substrate, not merely change its color.
- Preserve neutral gray concrete, ivory plaster, dusty jade ceramic and faded red
  accents. Warm/cool scene lighting is separate from these material colors.
- Translate grain, small chips and glaze into texture/normal/roughness detail;
  reserve geometry for silhouette, cavities and materially visible depth.

The damaged tile study shifts some color near the fracture; use its layer breakup
as reference, not an exact registered variant. The plaster illustration emphasizes
flaking to explain the layers; set physical thickness from the intended construction.
