The meaningful distinction is where a journey changes its relation to an objective.
Spatial B makes ascent part of acquiring A and descent part of acquiring B. The
lower passage can change a western commitment into a lower B arrival; the crown
offers a long upper return with intermediate transfers. This is an asymmetric
industrial composition even though many supporting rooms remain rectilinear.
No irregular perimeter is needed to make it legitimate.

## What the L04 contract resolves

The contract's expanded hall x=-22..22,y=-20..27 is consistent with the proposed
crown at R18.6..21.4 over 20°..160°. Its northmost outer point is Y25.4; its east/west
end extrema are within approximately ±20.11. The occupied arc therefore fits the
hall's plan reservation, while the wing landings near x±25 need their separate
work volumes and openings. This is a purposeful expansion around real occupied
space, not an enlargement of the reactor itself.

Hall roof 16, crown access volumes at least 10.4 and ordinary mezzanine roofs at
least 7.2 explicitly address the previous low-ceiling failure mode. The crown is
outside the bioshield, and the 17 m TF coils remain taller and nearer the vessel.
It can read as assembly access around heavy machinery rather than a walkway
placed on a pressure-vessel roof. The original published envelopes remain intact.

The A contract names south, east/port and rear stair arrivals with separate
landings. This supplies a credible next step beyond the raw large upper rectangle.
The B contract distinguishes a sheltered lower planted room from an adjacent open
loading void and retained ground rim. That distinction makes upper control affect
a crossing without automatically granting an uninterrupted view of every lower
action. These remain drawings to develop and test, not accepted sightlines.

The revised lower bridge top -2.8 with 0.25 m thickness leaves 0.20 m over the
specified outer PF coil top -3.25. It addresses the obvious section conflict of
putting the complete bridge at -3.2. The contract also explicitly requires
openings through the vessel, cryostat and bioshield, plus real excavation where
foundations occupy a route. Those are source/modeling obligations, not permission
to solve collisions with navigation exclusions.

## Consequential development requirements

**O1 — A must become an occupied freight room around a void.** The reserved
18 × 20 m upper platform is large enough to become an undifferentiated balcony.
Draw the central freight opening, bridge/edge widths, arrival landings and work
masses together. Preserve a clear ground Cask/W-port journey below. The three
upper entries should arrive beside different useful parts of the freight operation;
their stair shapes and landings should follow that operation rather than repeat
the same doorway-and-box module. The contract recognizes this issue; its actual
plan still needs to demonstrate it.

**O2 — B needs a legible boundary between shelter and loading void.** Show the
retained ground shelf's exact edge and underside, the lower room's continuous
floor, the open void and all three lower arrivals on coordinated plans. Check the
ground rim from opposite sides. A simple depressed rectangle with an exposed
perimeter would lose the spatial benefit that makes this alternative worthwhile.
The west work-room arrival and rear descent should give different initial reads,
with a useful lower move after either entry.

**O3 — The crown's work rooms should interrupt both space and observation.** Its
R≈20 centerline arc is about 48.9 m before wing bridges and stairs. That is a real
circulation commitment, not a short site switch. Make the west equipment room and
east transfer room fit their different destinations: one returns toward raised A,
the other descends through +3.2 and 0 before B. Draw their inhabited widths,
thresholds and intermediate landings. Selected lower views should emerge from
those working faces; an empty uniformly exposed arc or repeated identical cover
blocks would weaken both the industrial composition and the attention problem.

**O4 — The central curved stair needs a complete occupied section.** It is a
useful choice between the ground open work arc and lower passage, and its curve
has a direct source in the radial machine. The proposed R5.1 centerline over 80°
gives approximately 7.12 m of horizontal arc run for a 3.2 m change in level.
The nominal lower outer strip R6.1..7.3 is only 1.2 m wide before its rails. It
should be treated as a deliberate single-file interface, not a wide passing zone.
Show the slab hatch, supported bottom landing, outer bypass, rail thickness and
temporary support relocation in the same section. The surrounding ground route
must remain continuous while the lower stair is occupied. Actual curved tread,
capsule, follower-view and corner-clearance tests remain necessary.

As a narrow source-envelope check, intersecting the archived B04 inner radial
polygon across a 1.8 m vertical span above floor -3.2 yields approximately
R3.803..7.537. The proposed lower floor band R4.1..7.3 is therefore not rejected
by that profile envelope alone. The calculation includes all relevant polygon
vertex elevations; original script/results are in
`reviews/evidence/l04-radial-reservation.py/json`. It excludes capsule radius,
rails, floors, beams, collars, supports, stairs and azimuthal facets. This is not
a substitute for the selected physical experiment.

**O5 — Retain the distinction among the three cross-map routes.** The equator
serves ordinary reactor transfer, the lower route reaches shielded work rooms and
the crown has long elevated acquisition/exit costs. The current selection avoids
adding a direct lower Receiving connection or a new defender north reactor port.
Preserve that bounded selection while developing the proposal; adopting those
comparators too would be a fresh macro change. Name destinations and intermediate
working spaces so level changes remain understandable through geometry, not just
through a map overlay.
