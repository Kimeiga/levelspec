# Independent quick spatial-interest review — S01

Verdict: **promising**.

Reviewed the user brief (original 5v5 CT/T two-site bomb defusal in a high-tech industrial spy-villain lair nested on a steep mountainside), `sketch.svg`, the rendered `generated/blackglass/sketch-r01/sketch.png`, and the footprint/connection record in `layout-data.json`. This is a first-impression spatial review, before author strategy rationale, production geometry, timings, obstacles or competitive assessments.

The sketch offers a coherent place to explore. The lower freight/cooling wing has shared walls and several successive thresholds: Freight can enter Cooling Pumps or Lower Gallery, those spaces communicate laterally, and the journey can continue through either Cold Airlock or Pump Control into the lower Data Vault. These are packed adjoining rooms rather than isolated boxes linked by tubes.

The middle journey materially changes level and territory. Arrival Concourse climbs S1 into Mid; Cable Port climbs S2 into Heat Exchangers, whose shared side threshold lets the player transfer into Mid before committing to Reactor. From Mid, the suspended Q gallery crosses the lower cooling circulation without an invented movement transfer, then S5 descends toward B's east airlock. The alternate onward journey through Cleanroom can climb S4 to Reactor's west landing or follow the rear service spine. These are distinct destinations and decisions during travel, rather than three lanes meeting only at their endpoints.

The rear sequence also earns its space: Service Spine opens onto a wider crosswise Rear Bridge; the route can climb the security flight to the summit or wrap through Coolant Manifold and Switchgear to descend to B. That makes the large vertical separation readable as a built circulation network. The outline, adjoining orthogonal equipment halls, stair bays, uphill reactor mass and hanging valley-side vault make architectural sense for an engineered mountain installation. Shape restraint fits the industrial theme.

The 72 × 96m envelope, 8–24m main rooms and 4–6m stair/door widths communicate substantial 5v5 spatial depth. The declared 24 room/circulation nodes and 32 ordinary movement edges exceed the provisional 18/24 floors. Some small named spaces are stair landings, so the exact 24 is a coarse authoring convention; the actual developed extent and several interconnected room sequences support the scope independently of that count. Q/L is correctly excluded as a movement edge.

Carry forward the drawn shared interfaces, the Q-over-L crossing and the Mid-to-rear choice. The small northwestern landing sequence should read as one coherent service wing in the developed plan; avoid turning V/U/N into a collection of tiny unrelated rooms. Mountain contours and exposed versus enclosed boundaries need clearer definition at the developed stage, but their present roughness does not obscure the journeys.

This verdict opens detailed layout development. It does not accept spatial/visibility, tactical, structural, traversal or multiplayer balance gates. No comparison with unseen project layouts is claimed.
