## Tactical hypotheses to challenge

1. A can be approached from three different rising transfers. An upper defender
   gains a crest advantage but cannot see all three landings without changing
   position. Losing gallery support changes how a partner acquires the mezzanine.
2. B has a lower planted room, an open transfer void and a useful ground rim.
   Upper control can pressure a crossing while the lower room protects a different
   action. Retakes require choosing a descent and clearing its lower landing.
3. The lower vessel service passage can switch a west commitment toward B without
   controlling both equatorial ports. Its endpoints remain contestable work rooms;
   its central stair, if feasible, adds a costly mid-route re-entry choice.
4. The crown is a high investment in information and a distinct A return, not a
   universally fastest rotation. Coils and equipment divide observation. A lone
   player cannot use its long transfer as though the team supplies live information
   at every exit. Check 2v2 objective clocks without assuming spare support.
5. Main ground circulation remains useful when upper or lower territory is lost.
   Fresh 5v5/3v3/2v2 analysis and route measurements must assess that claim; B04
   timing and visibility acceptance do not carry over to moved objectives.

## Proposal dispositions

- Adopt spatial Alternative B's raised A, sunken B, partial lower vessel route and
  partial high crown as the coherent organization for detailed development. The
  objective elevations change what each connection buys, meeting the user's request
  for a substantially harder map more directly than additional parallel balconies.
- Select the central curved stair as a bounded experiment. Without it, the lower
  passage is a two-ended territorial commitment; with it, teams can change levels
  mid-journey but must contest another exposed junction. Compare those consequences
  explicitly. The stair is not neutral extra connectivity.
- Retain ground/local return routes. The crown's approximately49m arc plus vertical
  transfers makes it an advance commitment; do not assume it is a timely2v2 retake
  or claim every alternate route is a distance shortcut.
- Defer the separate upper internal/southern torus spine and external lower
  maintenance crescent. The selected lower internal route and high crown already
  provide different cross-map systems; adding both alternatives would change the
  selected organization again and dilute its exposure/territory costs.
- Decline the proposed fourth north reactor port for this candidate. Keeping the
  rear band's ring access indirect preserves an acquisition cost for the new
  central vertical choices. This is a design choice, not a claim that a fourth port
  is inherently poor.
- Preserve the transformer crossover as an optional later local comparison only.
  B's new lower work room, basin/rim relationship and three descents must first be
  resolved as actual occupied spaces.
