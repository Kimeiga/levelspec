## Target-population and casualty scenarios

- **5v5 defaults:** CT2B/2A/1Mid can branch from operations R without the original compulsory double descent. A is56.80m from the center CT anchor versus80.80m from T, giving an estimated7.06s center-anchor margin to the plant; the B margin is much tighter. Mid's array, Cleanroom baffle and lower/upper separation require an information choice. One flex cannot monitor every destination without moving, and can retreat through D/W.
- **Five-player B commit:** K and PC permit a split frontal push. The actual core blocks the long-to-short direct view, while B-rack bounds the east/rear fight. Attackers can clear either side with support and make the defender choose a core side or withdraw toward S6. Five-player throughput, trade windows and utility clearance have not been simulated. Entry width alone does not prove a successful rush.
- **Five-player A commit:** S3 and S4 remain exposed uphill commitments. The S3 forward/reverse views show the crest and lower preparation space; A-low1 offers a refuge after arrival rather than a protected climb. Short turns through AW and its low fixture before the actual A doorway. Reactor and A-screen split the next fight. A pair can support the same crest or use the other approach after acquiring the required territory; no safe single-player uphill duel is promised.
- **4+1 attacks:** Mid control makes Q/S5 a B-side threat and Cleanroom/S4 an A-side threat. The measured Mid-to-site costs mean the fifth player needs a head start or the main group must stage; a simultaneous flank does not materialize merely because the route exists. A lone B pressure player can complicate the defender's read before the upper execute, while CT can retain the flex rather than rotate everyone on first contact.
- **CT push and withdrawal:** A Mid defender can probe around M-array toward S1/H, then reset toward D. B information pushes through K or PC give up fast access to the rear and expose a second route through L/PC. Q has no physical transfer into the lower passage, so observing or occupying one level does not control the other automatically.
- **3v5 retake:** A rear-plus-short or B rear-plus-J retake is plausible only with survivors already holding the relevant routes. Players starting at the far objective may arrive too late. A paired same-entry trade can be more practical than a long solo flank. No scenario is accepted on an assumed bomb clock.
- **2v2 postplant:** A's reactor wrap and distinct rear/short approaches let the pair reposition or split attention. At B, the west plant is hidden from portions of the east approach by B-core; retakers must clear a side, while attackers exposing the opposite side risk being traded. Two retakers together at S6 cannot claim an immediate J split without paying the Mid route cost.
- **1v2 postplant:** The survivor has a core-side choice and room to reset, but cannot safely clear every opponent at once. Objective pressure and information must force the final commitment. No material color, low refuge, static ray or route result proves bullet protection, smoke coverage, sound information or a winnable clutch.
