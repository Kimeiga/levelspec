# Santa Bruma — independent spatial-interest review

**Verdict: promising.** The selected proposal has enough developed journeys and interacting volumes to merit gameplay development. It reads as a coastal rail terminus through its organization: a covered arrival pier feeds a public headhouse; the headhouse meets a different railway alignment behind the buffers; goods handling occupies a separate road-served hall; a lower passage crosses beneath the railway. The station identity survives removal of the names and decorative treatment.

This review covers the initial spatial proposal for an original, substantial train-station-themed 5v5 bomb-defusal map. I first assessed selected sheets 01–03, then considered the reference photographs and sheet 04's alternative organizations. I inspected the full, aspect-correct PNG renders of the SVGs, including both coordinated sections. No other trial output or verdict informed this review.

## Drawn relationships that justify development

The pier-to-platform journey has a substantive sequence. The landing opens into arrivals and the lower booking hall; S1 then rises within that hall, crosses its void on a bridge, and joins the east gallery before reaching the buffer concourse. Section A–A confirms that the gallery occupies the public hall's volume. This is a change in spatial experience and orientation, rather than a second room simply stacked over the first. The transition from the town-aligned hall to the diagonal railway is visible in the actual floor arrangement.

The west quay offers a materially different continuation. It wraps outside the headhouse into rivet court, where the mail bay projects overhead. From there the porters' stair reaches the mail bay at B, while the luggage subway continues below the platforms toward plant court and dispatch. S4 introduces a real transfer to the island platform within that crossing. The lower passage therefore connects several station functions and elevations; it is more than an extra straight path between the same two rooms. Section B–B makes the relationship between court, projecting bay, railway and subway legible.

The southern market/customs route stays at quay level and reaches the parcel exchange and loading apron. Onward movement through the service side can reconnect with the rail deck via S3. Together with the booking-hall route and the under-rail crossing, these form different journeys through a single place, with opportunities to change destinations. Their distinctions come from public, waterfront, railway and goods-service relationships, rather than cosmetic bends in parallel corridors.

The reference study has affected organization. Wemyss's aerial shows the close relationship between ferry pier, headhouse, railway and shore; that relationship is recognizable here without tracing its plan. The Rossio and São Bento photographs show the importance of long platform space within a large roof volume; the drawings give the rail shed a corresponding shared section and retain access behind the buffers. The parcel hall is less developed than these public and railway spaces, but its adjacency to customs, dispatch and the road already gives it a coherent role. The proposal's substance lies in these connected sequences and occupied levels, rather than the declared site envelope.

## Focused next changes

No macro replan is needed for this stage. Carry the following into the next drawing pass:

1. **Draw the northern playable termination of each platform.** Sheet 02 says the railway continues scenically, but the continuous platform fill reaches the sheet edge. Mark where walking ends and how the scenic continuation is separated. Until then, the usable length and commitment of the northern platform stretches remain ambiguous; their blank extension should not be counted as developed map substance.
2. **Develop A's goods-handling circulation around its existing interfaces.** The parcel exchange currently has two isolated equipment masses inside a largely undifferentiated floor. Show the working aisle from customs to dispatch and the loading apron, together with the actual loading openings. This will establish whether its approaches meet in an interesting sorting space or merely enter the sides of one broad room. Preserve the coherent goods/service relationships already drawn rather than adding unrelated rooms.
3. **Make the mail-bay/court relationship explicit in an edge or opening detail.** The projection is clear in section, but the west-facing windows are mostly asserted by annotation. Show the window band, solid lower wall and court-facing edges so this important relationship can be judged from the drawing itself. This is clarification of the chosen idea, not a request for another movement link.

## Evidence limits

These are drawn spatial hypotheses. There is no inspected AML, compiled model, performed movement, eye-level visibility study, measured travel/contact evidence or playtest. Stair landings, body clearance, subway structure and precise thresholds remain schematic. This verdict establishes neither physical reachability nor competitive balance, and it does not resolve whether the broad shared concourse and platform stretches produce suitable encounters for ten players. Those questions belong to subsequent layout and blockout work.
