# Spatial-interest review — Saltturn P01

**Verdict: promising.** The drawn district has enough coherent outdoor journeys and consequential connections to develop into the requested 5v5 bomb-defusal map. Its interest comes from streets and yards wrapping substantial buildings, with a sheltered market edge and a separate waterfront return. The largely level ground supports this composition; extra storeys are unnecessary to establish spatial variety.

## Evidence and scope

Reviewed the raw `01-ground-plan.png`, `02-circulation-levels.png` and `03-sections.png`, with the ground-plan SVG inspected for its mass/roof distinction, before any author rationale or alternative studies. Also inspected the supplied Chioggia canal market, Tsukiji alley, Essaouira quay and Trapani colonnade photographs. No other trial proposals or verdicts were consulted. This is an initial spatial-interest judgment, not gameplay, balance or physical-reachability acceptance.

## What makes the proposal worth developing

The westward journey has a tangible sequence: Road court enters the street between Merchant row and the awning-fronted Retail block; the fish-shop mass then divides movement toward Lantern elbow/Retail bend from movement through Wash court. Auction court opens beyond that street frontage, while the loggia provides a covered margin around the sealed Auction office. The loggia is useful architecture rather than a name for another corridor: its west and south arms connect the open court to the eastern district, while Market back lane supplies a different way around the office volume.

The lower journey has a different relationship to buildings. Road court opens into Net yard beneath the Filleting workshops; players can continue around the workshops toward Weighbridge and the Ice forecourt, or turn up Fillet walk toward Wash court. That cross-district connection gives the workshop mass a real organizing role. Moving around its north side is a change of destination and relationship to Auction court, not simply another bend along the same approach.

The Cold store separates Scales cut from Sea walk. Consequently, the route from Service yard down the water side reaches Ice apron around a different face of a large building than the route through Customs and the central courts. Ice plant and the quay further shape the apron. This creates a legible distinction between market-side circulation and the waterfront rather than treating the coastline as a decorative border.

The sections support the brief: ordinary circulation remains at grade, the loading ledge is only 0.45 m higher with both ends returning to grade, and the market roofs shelter ground-level edges. The photos' useful relationships survive in the proposal—shop frontage and awning margins, an open working quay, and a roofed public edge beside a court—even though this is an invented district rather than a reconstruction.

There is sufficient provisional substance for a whole-map study: two different approach sequences, a central opportunity to change direction, circulation around the auction volume, and a waterfront return. Several labels subdivide continuous ground, so they should not be counted as independent spaces; the actual massing and alternative journeys carry this verdict.

## Consequential refinement and later questions

No macro repair is required before detailed layout. The main refinement priority is the broad Net yard → Repair turn → Weighbridge area. These currently read as one extended piece of open ground around the workshop, with little spatial transition between their labels. Develop its junctions and building edges so the trip has deliberate pauses, exposures and onward choices; do not rely on naming or scattered props to supply that distinction. Preserve the useful connection north into Wash court.

Wash court and Auction court also share a fairly open interface around the fish shops and loggia. Establish whether they should read as one connected market volume or two territories with a clear threshold. Either choice can work, but it will determine how much of the central district is experienced at once.

Detailed layout should resolve the actual objective areas, site approaches, long views, player-scale widths and the cost of the two defender return journeys. Those are later-stage questions requiring developed geometry and eventually movement/gameplay evidence. They do not justify rejecting this initial outdoor, mostly flat spatial proposal.
