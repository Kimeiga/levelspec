## Five-player approach, response and next decision

### Initial development: two west, one reactor, two east

The western pair develops Cask/A access, initially deciding between a ground
approach and the existing upper-gallery relationship. The solo reactor player
probes the ground arc rather than claiming both ports. The eastern pair develops
the ground rim/bypass and trench/lower relationship. This is information and
territory development, not a simultaneous five-player execute split across two
objectives.

If the western pair sees an A anchor committing to the south crest, one player
can maintain that threat while the reactor player attempts to join the port-side
ascent. The second western player either supports the local clear or retains the
freight junction. The east pair can contain a return or prepare a B commitment.
The team must choose whether that distant containment is still worth two players
before the objective carrier moves. Crown occupation replaces one of these jobs;
there is no uncounted sixth observer.

A plausible initial defense is two players related to A, two related to B, and
one flexible ground/rear player. The A pair divides upper objective presence and
the lower/port approach; the B pair divides the sheltered lower room and an
upper/rim or E-port duty. If a defender takes the crown, their former lower or
ground responsibility becomes thinner. Opponents may pressure that vacancy,
contain the crown's next exit, or change objective instead of always hunting the
high player.

### A commitment: southern crest plus port-side ascent

The southern A entrant rises near x−36, y0→8 and reaches the y8..11 landing.
A second entrant reaches the east/port ascent near x−24.1, y5→13.
Their paths arrive on different sides of the
freight volume rather than stacking in the same doorway. The supporting player
must have a usable view of the defender contesting the next landing, or be close
enough to follow that encounter; merely occupying an adjacent elevation is not
support. The objective carrier follows whichever landing has actually been
cleared.

A defender can give up the first crest and hold a later upper corner, or have
the lower partner contest the port ascent before the attackers synchronize.
That response can leave the freight/ground through-route open. An attacker who
uses it may threaten another ascent or lower destination, but must communicate
the lost immediate upper pressure.

If the upper supporting attacker dies or is forced away, the southern entrant
cannot treat an old call as a live clear. Three practical continuations are:

- Wait at a lower preparation position while the port-side player gains a new
  support view; synchronize a later clear rather than crest alone by schedule.
- Reunite the local pair on one landing, accepting less angle diversity for
  usable immediate support.
- Leave the A commitment through territory still held, possibly using the
  freight/lower route toward B if the objective carrier and remaining team can
  make that journey. The lower route is an option, not a claim that a late full
  switch fits every round clock.

If the port-side player instead dies, the western pair can still use the south
route, change to the retained Cask-gallery acquisition, or disengage. The crown is not an
instant substitute for the dead port player: access and travel must be paid.

### B commitment: lower work room plus trench-side arrival

One player develops the lower work room from the reactor/west connection while
a partner descends from the trench side. A ground-rim player, if assigned, can
watch a selected loading crossing or contain an upper response. That third job
should be explicit; the two lower entrants do not automatically receive it.

The sheltered plant room prevents ground-rim occupation alone from settling the
fight. A defender can hold inside the lower room while another contests a descent
or the loading void. A coordinated attack should force those defenders to choose
between the two lower arrival directions, rather than demanding one player
expose to every height at once before entering.

If the rim supporter is lost, the lower pair must recheck the open loading area
or use a sheltered threshold before crossing. If one lower entrant dies, the
other must decide whether the rim player can descend and replace the missing
objective-plane task. Staying above with partial information is not the same
as providing an immediate trade. A defender who loses their lower partner has
the inverse problem: descend into changed territory, contain a likely exit, or
retain information for the incoming retake.

## Three-versus-three casualty state

Three players cannot retain both upper/lower site pairs, two equatorial ports,
the lower passage and a crown observer. A practical attack is a local pair plus
one relevant return or information job. At A, the pair may trade through one
ascent while the third contains the port-side arrival. At B, the pair may clear
the lower work room while the third watches the loading crossing from a nearby
rim position that has an actual onward descent.

After one of three players is lost, two survivors should be able to simplify:
reunite on one plane, concede a remote passage, or switch a local entry. They
need not maintain the five-player plan with absent jobs. The freight intermediate
ground exit and ordinary equatorial circulation are particularly valuable here:
they provide a reset between distant upper and lower commitments.

A remote crown player must judge whether their information still has an actor.
If both teammates have moved below B, an old W-port sighting may not help them;
continuing toward a timely local return may be better than remaining high.
Conversely, if a teammate is already at an A crest, a fresh relevant crown/wing
sighting may be useful without the observer descending. The value depends on
the actual view and teammate position, not the height's nominal strength.

## Crown roles and return costs

The R20 centerline, 20°..160° arc is approximately 49 m before rooms, wing
connections or stairs. This is a sketch measurement. At a provisional5 m/s it
is already nearly10 seconds of unobstructed travel; that division is not a
measured tactical time and excludes stopping, clearing and vertical movement.

Useful roles are: territory acquired in advance for a later A return; a selected
view that a nearby teammate can act on; a prepared cross-map flank; and containment
of an opponent who would otherwise use the high connection. The crown need not
be the fastest response to justify those roles. Its threat can matter when
unused, but only if the threatened arrival remains reachable in the objective
window.

The two work rooms can support gathering information, receiving a teammate and
deciding whether to continue. A player should leave a high hold when its relevant
ground support disappears, the watched arrival has passed, the objective moves
to a task the hold cannot affect, or interaction is now urgent. At the west end,
remaining near A+3.2 may be rational. At the east end, reaching the +3.2 gallery
still leaves separate ground and lower commitments before B interaction.

Do not promise that a crown room sees the inner ports merely from its plan
position. The coils remain taller and may occlude those rays. A bounded view
of a wing approach can be useful; no panoramic view or full-site certification
is required.

## Detailed return: A access inventory and final frontiers

There are **four ground-fed upper arrivals plus one crown connection**:

- A-S climbs from `(-36,0,0)` to the southwest/front platform, with its landing
  screen prompting a side choice before reaching plant territory.
- CG climbs at x−24 from y−14 to−6, turns, then joins A's southeast front platform
  through gallery x[-25,-22], y[-3,5]. It is a real alternative acquisition route.
- A-E climbs at x−24.1 from y5 to13, arriving east of the freight void and behind
  its own side screen.
- A-R climbs from `(-29,28,0)` to the north landing at y17..20, behind its turn
  screen and the northern tooling relationship.
- CW reaches the northwest upper territory through the landing around
  `(-34.5,25,+3.2)`, after the high service walk and crown stair.

These five connections do not create five independent immediate plant-facing
angles. CG meets the front platform **before** the A-E stair opening and must
turn into shared front/void territory; it cannot continue through the filled
flight. Its final attack overlaps the southern/eastern front, while its earlier
acquisition and information are different. A-E arrives east of the void and guide
mast; crossing toward the plant is another decision. A-R and CW reach northern
territory on different sides of the cask/tooling mass before rounding it toward
the plant.

That convergence makes a two-player A relationship plausible without requiring
one permanent guard per connection. It does not certify that two players can
watch everything. A pair can contest the front and a northern/eastern return
while conceding uncertain remote territory; attackers can invest in a third
arrival to punish that allocation. Getting that third arrival requires an
actual route and changes what they can cover elsewhere. Preserve all four
ground acquisitions in this layout rather than deleting CG solely to meet an
entry count. In compiled review, check whether CG and A-S instead create an
unintended uncontestable early upper race from the selected attacker starts.

The crown connection also remains an advance commitment. Its west service walk
goes north to the crossing stair before descending into A. Its lower landing
joins the mezzanine without requiring circulation under the low end of that
flight. This is more specific than a generic direct balcony flank. The section
allocates headroom above A-R, but physical acceptance belongs to native tread,
body and follower inspection.

## Detailed return: east exposed rim and west shielding

Retain the explicit **x41..43 ground rim route** as a selected tactical experiment.
Its potential approximately60 m axis at x42 connects Cooling toward Control and
can also enter the B shelf/ground-crossing territory. It offers rear pressure and
an alternative to the trench/basin descent. It does not see through the shelf
into the sheltered lower plant, and merely reaching the rim is not B completion.

An attacker using the strip gives up lower objective presence while exposed to
a possible long northern receiver. A defender holding that receiver spends
attention and a player who could support E port, the crown transfer or the lower
room. The attacker can pressure the basin with a partner, stop at a useful shelf
entry, or abandon the exposed advance. A planted-side survivor can contain a
return from the rim, but cannot simultaneously interact below it. Those are
coherent manpower costs for the route; no invented intermediate occlusion or
free safe reset is credited.

The selected west Y−14 shielding return has a different job. It blocks the
former straight western approach, leaves the x−30..−22 mouth open, and requires
reacquisition before the longer northern ground segment. A-S, CG and the freight
well then provide choices after that threshold. This intentional asymmetry does
not require a matching east baffle. The map's longer east line should nevertheless
be checked for collateral coverage: if one protected northern position also
denies the trench exit, shelf access and lower rear descent with negligible
repositioning, the actual problem would be excessive combined influence, not
the line's length by itself.
