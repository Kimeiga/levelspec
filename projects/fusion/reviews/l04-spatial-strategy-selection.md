# L04 early selection — spatial interest and strategy

Date: 2026-11-11. Reviewer: `skill_forward_review`.
Scope: early spatial-interest and strategic comparison. This is **not a detailed
layout-production gate**, a compiled clearance review, or a balance verdict.

**Verdict: promising. Develop spatial Alternative B.** Its raised A, sunken B,
lower internal vessel route and high external crown provide substantially richer
spatial relationships than B04. The arrangement contains enough meaningful
overlap to answer the expanded brief. It also supports plausible five-player
plans that deliberately leave some territory unwatched. Its difficulty should
come from choosing and adapting those plans, rather than pretending every layer
can be continuously controlled.

Spatial B is not selected because it contains the most elevation labels. Its
lower arc is **inside** the D vessel beneath the work deck, while its crown is
**outside** the shield and below the coil tops. Its two objective floors differ
from the equatorial transfer plane in opposite directions. Those are architectural
and tactical differences that remain even if decorative props are removed.

The drawings already distinguish inaccessible machine space, actual occupied
floor reservations, and partial rather than duplicated full rings. The result
reads as an unfinished reactor and its handling infrastructure. That is a
sufficiently substantive place to develop, rather than another set of named
corridors waiting for theming.

## Why spatial B has useful overlap

The west freight well can put lower travel, ground Cask movement and an A ascent
in a shared volume. A player seen entering that volume may be heading upward
toward A, remaining on the ground, or preparing to cross below the vessel. A
nearby teammate can act on the sighting; a distant crown observer cannot assume
the destination is still known after traveling to the exit.

At B, the lower work room, retained ground shelf, open loading void and descending
approaches create a different relationship. Winning the ground rim can provide
information and support over a selected crossing, while an opponent remains in
the sheltered lower objective room. The objective interaction still requires
arrival on the correct plane. The drawing's sheltered room is important to this
idea: an entirely open basin visible from its whole rim would collapse the
distinction into blanket high-ground control.

The ground reactor route remains an ordinary transfer that can reach either
wing without paying for the crown or staying in the lower passage. The lower
route reaches a useful B-side room directly but has fewer exits and different
information. The crown is a more substantial commitment that reaches A's upper
territory and an east transfer sequence. These are alternatives with different
costs, not three equivalent parallel corridors.

The crown's nominal R20, 140-degree arc alone is roughly 49 m before endpoint
travel and vertical transfers. That is a geometric sketch estimate, not a
measured path. It makes the crown plausible as territory acquired in advance,
a delayed return or a prepared flank. It does **not** justify describing it as
the universal fast rotation. Reaching B from +6.4 requires a total 9.6 m descent
to the objective plane, with actual intervening landings and room commitments.

## Plausible coordination, including loss of support

A five-player attack can initially develop a west pair, a ground-reactor probe
and an east pair. Within that allocation, the west pair chooses a ground/upper
relationship and the east pair chooses a rim/lower relationship. The reactor
player gathers enough information to join a commitment or contest a transfer.
This is five jobs, not an execute that simultaneously staffs both sites, both
ports, the lower arc and the crown. A crown investment replaces another task.

For example, if the western upper player is lost before A is secured, the ground
partner no longer has a live upper clear. The reactor player can join the
port-side ascent, the pair can regroup at the freight interface, or the team
can redirect toward B through a lower approach it has actually secured. The
map offers substitution and a destination change; it does not make either
instant or guarantee that an absent teammate's old information remains valid.

A plausible defense uses an A upper anchor and west support, a B lower anchor
and rim/E-port support, plus one flexible ground/rear player. Occupying the crown
requires reallocating one of those players. That gives the opposition a useful
response: pressure the lower/ground responsibility that was vacated, contain
the crown exit, or commit to the other objective. Five defenders cannot also
permanently staff every crown room, stair crest and lower bridge. Deliberate
concession is viable coordination, not evidence that the drawing needs fewer
connections by default.

At 3v3, the team should simplify to a local pair plus one relevant return or
information job. Retaining two separated long-range observers while asking the
last player to take a multilevel site alone is a weak plan. The map need not
make it successful. The useful test is whether a player who loses support can
return to ordinary ground circulation, join a nearby ally or change the local
approach without an unavoidable full-map excursion.

At 2v2 after A is planted, a defender arriving beneath the mezzanine must choose
an ascent and obtain useful information about its landing. A defender already
near the west crown end has a different upper arrival. Two defenders both on
the far eastern ground route do not get that arrival for free. After B is
planted, a player above the basin can contain a crossing or support a descent;
the upper position does not perform the lower objective interaction. A survivor
on the far crown may correctly abandon that position rather than finish a
beautiful but late flank.

These plans are plausible layout hypotheses. They establish enough strategic
substance to develop the candidate. Actual contact times, utility, sound,
trading, body visibility and objective-clock feasibility remain unobserved.

## Return against the L04 contract

The contract retains the selected candidate's strongest relationships and does
not simply merge all four alternatives. Its larger hall, separately roofed
crown-transfer volumes, opened freight well and sheltered B room address actual
spatial consequences of the change. Keeping the direct defender-spawn-to-ground-
reactor connection out of this candidate is coherent; the earlier north-port
proposal remains a materially different comparator rather than a compulsory
feature.

The widened lower-arc reservation and the central curved stair are new development
choices beyond the raw spatial B drawing. They deserve the explicit experiment
status already given in the contract. The elevated lower radial bridges also
change section details. I have not treated the stated PF-ring clearance or the
new stair reservation as independently verified physical facts.

Four consequential development suggestions follow.

1. **Keep the shared freight and B loading volumes legible in the next drawing.**
   Show the actual slab openings, solid shelf boundaries, occupied landings and
   limited cross-level views. This is the main source of local coordination and
   uncertainty. Preserve separate preparation positions before their exposed
   transitions; do not turn every edge into a simultaneous fresh angle. At A,
   develop the three arrival landings around a freight volume rather than leaving
   one broad uninterrupted platform from which all stair crests may be read.

2. **Treat the central curved stair as a topology comparison.** Without it, the
   lower route is a two-ended commitment reached through the wings. With it,
   a player arriving from the southern ground ring can enter or leave the lower
   network mid-journey. That makes a fake, support substitution and a B redirect
   more credible, but may also make the lower B arrival the dominant attack.
   Compare stair present/absent from the same staging positions, including one
   lost support player. Inspect both the retained ground outer strip and the
   lower single-file passing strip; a stair that physically fits may still
   prevent a follower from seeing the encounter the leader has just entered.
   Its curved shape is valuable only if the changing views and onward choice
   survive the actual hatch, rails and support frame.

3. **Give the crown's endpoint rooms concrete jobs, and retain the ground return.**
   The two work rooms should interrupt views while providing a place to receive
   a teammate, obtain limited information, or decide whether to continue. Do
   not assess the whole high route as one panoramic hold. At the east end, show
   the +6.4 → +3.2 → 0 sequence and the separate commitment to B−3.2. At the west
   end, show what reaching A+3.2 enables and which A arrival remains unseen.
   This will reveal when a crown player should stay, descend, abandon or return.

4. **Enlarge the hall through real interfaces, not uniform scaling.** The crown
   reservation reaches close to the new x±22 and north y27 hall limits. Develop
   the wing penetrations, guard clearances, supports and raised access enclosures
   together. The west gallery cannot remain an unchanged window in a wall that
   has moved through it. The expanded shared hall is an opportunity for an upper
   landing or bridge to occupy the same volume as a lower journey. Keep the
   reactor dimensions fixed and preserve breathing room where players prepare.

These suggestions do not require another whole-map alternative before the next
developed drawing. They identify the interfaces that will determine whether the
promising arrangement works. None constitutes a claim that an uncompiled passage
already fails.

## Selection disposition

Advance **spatial B / the L04 sectional-objective direction** to coordinated
plans and sections, retaining the central stair and enlarged hall as explicit
selected experiments. Keep organic B as the strongest lower-disruption comparator
if the crown/lower-system integration later proves incoherent. Do not revert
merely because spatial B imposes harder information and allocation problems;
that added complexity is now part of the user's request.

This closes the early spatial-interest/strategic-selection scope as **promising**.
Detailed local visibility, strategy/clock scenarios, architecture/fidelity,
construction and compiled movement remain later gates for the developed revision.
