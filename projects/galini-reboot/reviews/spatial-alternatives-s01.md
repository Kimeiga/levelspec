## Initial comparison

**A / Harbor Fold: promising; my provisional spatial preference.** The harbor, market shelf and upper civic neighborhood form a coherent inhabited slope. Net Street reaches a roofed Winery Passage through a building; the other lower stairs arrive at Laundry and Market/Sea Balcony territory. Above, Cypress and Bell approaches lead into different sides of the upper town. The eastern descent then returns through a turning shelf to the working harbor. The nearby projections of Chapel and Boat Court therefore coexist with several distinct access journeys. This is a substantial place to develop, with architectural reasons for changes in height and direction.

The direct comparison with the original matters: the old plan separated large open courts with ribbons of wall. A now puts complete building footprints along the routes and makes a few of those volumes traversable. It must retain that intent in 3D. Several middle and upper gaps are still broad enough to become empty terrace pads if the building fronts are ignored; one cannot fix that merely by adding crates or circular planters.

**B / Ravine Quarter: promising.** The inaccessible ravine gives a legible reason for cross-town commitment. A player on either shoulder can change district locally before choosing the upper crossing, middle passage or lower shoreline journey. The bridge entrances are part of occupied buildings, especially the Taverna gateway and Inn archway, so the crossing is not simply a line between anonymous open pads. The objective split across shoulders creates a more radical territorial change from the original than A.

The spatial cost is the separation itself: the lower detour and two bridges risk making the town feel like two local stair networks joined at a few exposed toll points. That can be interesting if the landings and buildings let players prepare, observe and disengage. If both bridges become long bare sightline channels held from the far gateway, the topology would be rich on paper but thin during contact. This is a development risk, not grounds to fail an early sketch. No timing or balance conclusion is supported yet.

I prefer A for the continuous inhabited cliff/harbor relationship and the range of public street, passage and terrace transitions. B is a credible alternative if the team prefers the stronger crossing-control strategy. This preference does not compel keeping A's current objective or spawn positions.

## Return requirements and evidence limits

Both are **promising** at the early gate. Production remains dependent on a developed drawing with actual playable limits/retaining faces, openings, roofed-passage clearance, stairs and landings, and height-aware views. The S01 sections explain intended relationships but are not physical verification. A needs focused views from Bell/Sea Balcony toward the harbor and around the central ascent. B needs opposing bridge/landing views and measured alternative-route costs later. Roof-on contextual views must demonstrate real town massing rather than low partitions.

The right-side labels on the viewed Ravine Quarter PNG are clipped at the image edge; the core plan and section were still understandable. Repair the sheet bounds for subsequent review/export. This is presentation feedback, not a spatial-interest blocker. The original overview alone does not establish whether hidden roofs were absent; my comparison concerns visible building organization and the source plan.
