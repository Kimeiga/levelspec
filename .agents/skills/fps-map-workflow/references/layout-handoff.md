# Layout and evidence handoff

Use the smallest packet that lets a reviewer judge the current stage and affected
scope. The [workflow](../SKILL.md) owns progression and the
[review skill](../../fps-map-review/SKILL.md) owns roles and verdicts.

## Stage-appropriate packets

| Stage | Include |
| --- | --- |
| First proposal | Known brief/theme, image sources and spatial observations, considered architectural organizations, and a labeled spatial proposal with substantial journeys/connections and relevant level indications. Mark assumptions; planning notes can be concise but the design must be developed enough to judge. |
| Developed layout | Consequential game/body assumptions, target/stress scenarios, actual boundaries and openings, provisional dimensions, object IDs, sections and visibility/cover predictions. |
| Compiled blockout | Editable source and identity, matching layout/object mapping, actual views, route/physical checks, measurements and explicit unavailable evidence. |
| Revision | Changed and retired spaces/objects, affected neighbors and updated evidence; reuse unchanged accepted work. |

Do not demand precise combat detailing or compiled proof for the first proposal.
Substantial theme/architecture planning precedes that drawing; simplicity of the
deliverable is not permission to abbreviate the spatial design.
For local edits, show the affected encounter with its entrances, neighboring threats,
retreats and support routes rather than reopening the whole map.

## Developed drawing

Use a readable SVG with revision, units, orientation and a common origin. Show actual
spatial boundaries, named spawn/objective anchors, intermediate callouts, connections,
thresholds and consequential obstacles. Relationship reasoning can be embedded in the
plan; a separate graph artifact is optional. Distinguish movement from optical/support
relationships and mark inaccessible/scenic masses.

Show traversable openings, observation apertures, overhead features and actual level
transfers distinctly. Use aligned plans or sections when a single plan is ambiguous.
For important holds and transitions, include observer body/eye assumptions and FOV,
walking/slab levels, relevant tall occluders and low-cover base/top heights. Show
predicted opposing views, including intermediate positions on a grade or climb;
label predictions separately from compiled observations.

For windows, show aperture width and sill/head levels without implying traversal.
For drops, show takeoff/landing, direction, return/commitment and uncertain mechanics.
For shaped routes, show usable turn boundaries rather than only a centerline. Guards
and unguarded edges follow design intent, not a blanket rule.

Plan low-cover/preparation/repositioning opportunities where the encounter calls for
them, alongside intentional exposed commitments. Record how neighboring control and
elevation affect their protection. No fixture count or nominal height establishes
usable cover.

## Obstacle notes

Give gameplay-relevant obstacles stable IDs mapped between drawing and source.
Keep IDs for retained objects; record replacements/removals. An assembly can be one
tactical feature with mapped parts; cosmetic groups need not become separate entries.

For each consequential feature record briefly:

- Footprint, height/elevation and provisional dimensions.
- Intended role and the approaches it protects from or exposes to.
- Entry, body clearance, follower support, repositioning and escape/commitment.
- Visibility, collision, ballistic, destructibility and utility properties separately;
  unsupported behavior stays unknown regardless of material names.
- How usefulness changes with adjacent territory or objective ownership.

## Review packet

For whole maps, provide a macro view of objectives, territory, rotations and major
spatial relationships plus focused named sections/interfaces. A local packet needs
bounds, affected IDs, adjoining routes/threats and relevant levels/cover/views. Include
enough context to judge consequences without requiring every local reviewer to audit
the entire map in fine detail.

For design-development assignments, state the reviewer's perspective or encounter,
explicit user constraints, provisional design choices, and the question to explore.
Provide candidate alternatives where available. Hold the author's preferred rationale
and sibling proposals until the reviewer has recorded an independent first response.
See the [proposal protocol](../../fps-map-review/references/design-development.md)
for concrete changes, tradeoffs and experiment records. Give shared interfaces an
explicit owner and include context from both adjoining areas.

At blockout, supply revision-matched views from consequential opposing and supporting
positions, not just an aerial image. Pair them with actual movement and geometry
checks. Include spawn areas and offsets, important holds, low-cover positions and
level transitions; note stance/tool limitations. Retain source/capture identities so
reviewers can distinguish changed geometry from changed cameras.

A closed finding identifies the inspected revision and supporting evidence. Update
the drawing when the design intentionally changes, and recheck changed interfaces;
do not relabel a broken connection or weaken the body/route assertion to hide it.
