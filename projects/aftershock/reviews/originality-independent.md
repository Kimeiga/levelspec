# Independent originality probe — Aftershock reset

The user rejected sketch r01 as three parallel routes with a middle overpass. This probe visually inspected the existing layout SVGs and read the accompanying briefs for Greywatch, Apogee, Blackglass, Ninefold, Switchback, Rivergate, Kiln Yard and Spillway. Review screenshots are retained in `reviews/evidence/originality-*.png`; Quick Look crops some right-hand notes, so relationship descriptions were checked against the source SVG/brief. This is an independent spatial/topological comparison, not a competitive-balance judgment.

## What repeats

Greywatch is the closest predecessor: south-centered T arrival fans into a west route, lower middle and east route; the two sites sit at the northern ends of the outer wings; CT occupies a northern transverse rear connection; an upper transverse walk crosses lower middle, with stairs returning near the wing fronts. Aftershock r01 repeats that geography and sequence almost literally. Train names and wreck cover do not change the organizing relationship.

Apogee replaces the wings with rooms and raises A, yet still proceeds from southern T through western/eastern approaches and middle alternatives toward flank sites and northern CT. Ninefold winds the paths and stacks the eastern wing, but retains a southern three-way departure, northwestern A, northeastern B and rear northern CT. Spillway retains southern departure, parallel outer approaches, middle room network, northern flank sites and CT rear. Switchback states the pattern most clearly: three ascending longitudinal chains feed A / shared Valve / B, then an upper rear service bar joins CT.

Blackglass makes vertical travel consequential and relocates CT to a middle-high service room. It is a stronger change than cosmetic bends, but its plan still follows southern insertion through west / mid / east progression to opposing flank sites and a connected rear service wing. Rivergate turns the direction sideways and uses three river crossings; its major relationships still form parallel route choices across a separating strip. Kiln Yard changes local geometry through packed polygonal rooms and shared boundaries, yet its two sites remain forward flank destinations with south T, north CT and central short routes.

The recurring signature is therefore more than straight corridors: **opposite centered spawns, peer sites abreast near the defender end, three forward journey families, a shared middle access to both sites, and one defender-side lateral rotation.** Bending routes, rotating the entire drawing, adding another door, or changing the elevation of one lane leaves that signature largely intact.

## Three different station organizations

### 1. Scissor terminal with an inboard site and an outboard site

Put T in the western inbound Red Line at z0, CT in a northeastern emergency-control mezzanine at z4. Put A within the station's central triangular scissors platform at z0; put B at a southeast elevated terminal at z4. A and B are successive/offset station components, rather than a pair of flank rooms abreast beyond three lanes.

The Red approach divides before A. One passenger route enters A's concourse. A sealed lower express bore physically passes underneath the A platform without opening into it, then curls around the east end to a long rising B arrival. A second route from the western tickets climbs around A's outer rim and joins the B mezzanine through a distinct transfer hall. CT can descend into A from control or follow a curved outer terminal wall to B rear. Site-to-site rotation uses a switch-house and perimeter stair; it is not a straight rear bar and is not unlocked solely by controlling a universal middle room.

Journeys have different orders: a T can contest the central station directly, or disappear under that contest and emerge at the outboard terminus. A defender seeing an A probe cannot infer a B clear, because the underpassing bore is movement-separated. The disaster can buckle the triangular platform and throw one train through a buffer above another moving line, making the interweaving structural.

Risks to resolve in a sketch: do not make A a mandatory movement gate to B; the bypass must be genuinely independent. Do not leave B as a long one-door dead end. Check CT B deployment against the deep T bypass, and avoid a single rim perch seeing both A plant and B's only ascent. This direction changes the old relationship signature most clearly.

### 2. Eccentric split spiral around a failed transfer drum

Organize circulation around a large offset escalator/drainage drum, with an outer rising passenger crescent and an inner descending service crescent that overlap in XY at different levels. T starts at the southwest lower tail; CT starts on the east intermediate ring. Place A on the upper outer crescent at the northwest tangent and B in the lower *interior* concourse east of the drum, rather than on the opposing outer flank.

The A journey wraps away from B before turning inward; the B journey descends along the inner crescent and doubles beneath a segment of the A approach. Transfers occur only at the north headhouse and southeast fan room. There is no central crossbridge and no universal center hub. CT moves from an intermediate ring to the upper A head or lower B fan, with two different vertical commitments. A change of objective requires choosing a transfer interface and traversing a portion of the spiral; it does not mean taking middle and selecting left/right doors.

A train wrapped against the drum, collapsed radial escalators, an offset ring fracture and stacked line fragments provide a station identity that follows the route structure. Opaque drum sectors and retained shear walls should limit view sectors; clear route colors/ceiling cues are particularly valuable.

Risks: a complete open ring becomes constant 360-degree exposure, and a fully connected hub returns to the old universal-middle problem. A dead end after a long spiral is punitive. Use substantial broken/closed sectors, stage each vertical commitment, and keep site-to-site travel short enough to be a real late-round choice. A simple pair of north/south crescents between west/east spawns would merely rotate the old three-route organization; the nested objective and separated transfer interfaces are what make this version different.

### 3. Skew X with transfers outside the crossing

Run the Red platform/bore southwest–northeast at z0, from T arrival to B. Run Blue northwest–southeast at z4, from A to CT emergency arrival. The lines cross without a movement junction. Put actual transfers around an *offset* collapsed rotunda west and an east switch-house; one can follow a line through the station or leave it at a remote transfer to reverse direction on the other level. A and B occupy opposite diagonals and floors, while the spawns occupy the other diagonal ends.

The central X is an observation/architecture event, not a bridge that serves as the common lateral shortcut between standard west/east lanes. Lower Red travellers pass beneath a damaged Blue carriage. To attack the other objective, teams must move along their own line to a transfer, climb or descend, then travel back toward a different terminal. CT deployment toward A is along Blue; deployment toward B commits to the east descent. That separates first-contact geography from the geometric middle.

Risks: direct train-bore sniper views and asymmetric deployment distances are consequential. Remote transfers can make rotations too late or make one transfer the only useful strategic territory. If an added central room simply connects all four branches, the design collapses into a hub with four arms. If transfers become short stairs at both ends of a central bridge, it starts resembling the rejected overpass again. Keep the crossing physically independent, make the transfer rooms spatially substantial, and place meaningful contests at different longitudinal points on the two lines.

## Recommendation

The scissor terminal provides the clearest topological departure because one objective is inboard and the other is outboard, while a genuine grade-separated bypass changes what controlling A permits. The split spiral is the strongest architectural expression of a tangled interchange if its nested objective/remote transfer geography is retained. The skew X is immediately legible and easier to construct, but must avoid becoming another middle overpass with stairs at each wing.

Draw at least two of these as actual occupied shapes before selecting. Compare journeys and where information/route changes occur, not only silhouettes. None has yet earned a layout, visibility, movement or balance acceptance.
