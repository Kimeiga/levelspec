# L01 independent strategy / player behavior review

**Status: accepted for layout progression.** This verdict concerns the developed drawing and provisional rules, not compiled reachability or competitive balance. The raw `layout.svg` / PNG, `layout-data.json`, and `brief.md` were assessed before author rationale or other review verdicts.

The two spawn-side loops provide comparable initial decisions without a complete upper circuit. A can commit South or Arcade; B can commit North or Cinema. The Court is the common contested territory rather than a free connector behind the objective. Loft occupation changes support/exposure but does not confer a separate capture position. This is a workable arrangement for the stated five-player control mode.

## Scenario trace — drawing hypotheses

- **5v5 opening / 3+2 split:** A can send tank, damage and support through South while a second damage/support pair uses Arcade. B has the rotated equivalent. The kiosk gives a preparation pocket before the narrowest ground mouth; the outside pair can choose ground pressure or spend time ascending. At the Court, the Lift prevents the three-player group from automatically seeing the pair on the other side: a team must announce a chosen side or preserve one support per group. The split is useful but has an actual coordination cost.
- **Five-player rush:** Both six-meter primary mouths provide space for a tank, a follower and offset support under the declared body dimensions. The group gives up an orthogonal pressure angle; area denial at that mouth may stall it. Reversal to the other home approach is relatively expensive because the safe link runs back through the anchor store, while the short link goes through Court. That commitment is intentional and does not require an additional connector before modeling.
- **Point occupation / tank and two supports:** The solid Lift offers a central break in opponent fire but also breaks friendly healing and damage visibility. Low fountain rims aid a crouched reset against some same-level rays; they should not be counted as protection from the +3m lofts. A plausible hold is tank plus support on one side of Lift, one damage watching the opposite mouth, and the remaining pair applying pressure from a home wing/loft. This trades broad awareness for thinner presence at the other two entrances.
- **Loft hold versus grounded retake:** Each loft is a cul-de-sac. It contributes oblique fire into part of Court and can threaten an opponent's primary arrival, but it cannot capture the ground point. From the corresponding ground mouth, an enemy must spend roughly forty meters crossing the home wing, ascending and returning along the loft to attack the hold directly; approximately seven seconds at assumed 5.5m/s before clearing/combat, not a measured timing. That is too slow to treat as the only immediate answer. Direct ground-to-loft fire, the opposing loft, Lift occlusion, and capture pressure must remain usable immediate responses. The narrow front aperture/cheeks and tall Lift make this plausible in drawing, with offset sightlines a priority compiled check.
- **3v3 after casualties:** Three survivors cannot hold all four Court mouths and both lofts. Conceding one approach and shifting around Lift can create a temporary local advantage. An isolated loft player may keep information but leaves fewer players contesting; going down the stairs to reinforce has a real cost. This is a meaningful casualty decision rather than a guarantee of recovery.
- **2v5 stagger:** Two survivors should retreat to a kiosk/anchor regroup pocket or intentionally spend their remaining lives contesting. The layout does not promise a safe solo re-entry through a fully held Court. Independent survivors can use separate mouths, but that is not a viable substitute for waiting for the respawn wave. The stated eight-second wave plus roughly seven-second spawn-to-Court trip makes a lost fight consequential without a long empty traversal.
- **Team wipe / reinforcement:** Rotationally matched home loops make initial reinforcement costs comparable in drawing. The protected anchor interior and kiosk can host a regroup before either primary or side entry. With the provisional 20-second unlock, a team can reasonably reach Court and choose a loft before activation; these values need target-engine timing.
- **Spawn camping:** Court ownership alone does not provide a straight shot into the distant spawn anchors. A camping team must advance into one or both home wings and give up point presence. Both six-meter exits prevent a single literal doorway bottleneck, but the rooms have no implemented team barriers. The brief correctly requires barrier/invulnerability rules in the target game; source marker semantics alone must not be described as protected spawns. Verify protected offsets, entrance sightlines and simultaneous exit coverage in the compiled geometry.

Document that the marker is an accessible POI beside the solid Lift if the actual target-engine capture volume is centered `(40,36)`. Do not silently make a left-offset capture area that removes the drawing's rotational equivalence. The blockout may retain a displaced navigation marker; target-game zone shape/scoring remain unimplemented.
2. **Loft lockout test:** inspect offset standing/crouching positions across both aperture edges, views from South/North staging and the Court side of Lift, and views from intermediate stair positions. If one loft controls both opponent mouths plus most usable capture floor while remaining effectively invisible to ground fire, narrow/reorient its aperture or redistribute the tall Court mass before accepting the blockout. Do not assume the single center section establishes all rays.
3. **Support test:** inspect occupied point positions on both sides of Lift with the two proposed support locations. A team must be able to choose a readable supported fight without demanding healing through opaque geometry. A support moving to reconnect should not be forced to cross every active enemy angle simultaneously.

## Evidence limits

All behavior above is a drawing hypothesis based on the stated one-tank/two-damage/two-support assumptions. No actual hero kit, healing range, projectile/utility behavior, spawn protection, multiplayer body blocking, capture volume, score rate, or ten-player playtest has been tested. Compiled geometry, route walkthroughs, reverse views and actual target-engine play remain pending. Flying heroes, large tanks and fast mobility can change the value of both loft cul-de-sacs and spawn boundaries.

Marker `(36,36)` is an accessible validation/POI inside that zone, not its center. Explicit zone data and brief note will preserve this distinction. This resolves follow-up1; no gameplay geometry change is requested by this strategy review at layout stage.
