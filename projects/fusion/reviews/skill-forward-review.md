## Initial scenario response

Retain the four features as a provisional baseline. The supplied facts do not yet
demonstrate a geometry defect. They define useful comparisons, especially where the
same feature changes value after a plant or loss of support.

### Protected rear rotation and exposed central shortcut

Before planting, compare a real attack with a fake under the same defender setup.
Record when defenders acquire credible information, when they leave, and whether
they can reach useful cover and act before the attack resolves. Do not compare only
running time. The rear route might preserve a defender's option to respond while
the shortcut trades exposure for urgency. That is a plausible distinction to keep.

After planting, replay the routes with territory ownership reversed. Establish who
can see their exits, whether a second defender can support the first arrival, and
the time still needed to clear and perform the objective interaction. The rear route
need not be fully safe at its destination to be useful. Conversely, an apparently
fast arrival is not a viable retake if every exit is simultaneously exposed to
positions the team cannot contest with its remaining players and tools.

Conditional edits should address the demonstrated failure: if the rear route lets
one defender cheaply negate either destination after every credible fake, test a
longer or more committed segment, or narrower influence at arrival. If it strands
retakers after both sites can be lost, test a revised exit relationship or staging
position. If the shortcut has no exploitable time benefit once clearing is included,
test that specific crossing. Do not protect it merely because it is exposed.

### Observation window ten seconds from the supported entry

First identify the user of the information. Ten seconds of travel for the observer
is different from ten seconds before anyone can act. A teammate already staged at
the entry may act on a live call while the observer continues watching. That use
could justify the window without moving it.

Compare (a) the observer looking and then travelling alone, (b) a staged entry player
acting on the observer's call, and (c) the same pair after the observer is forced
away. Give the opponent its actual relocation options in each case. Record the last
observation time, entry commitment time and whether the sighting warned the target.
Inspect the observer's body exposure and what they leave uncovered elsewhere.

If the window's only useful job requires the same player to make a ten-second trip
while the target can change position unobserved, treat the information as a lead,
not a cleared entry. Test a more relevant view, an intermediate recheck, or an altered
approach if stale information makes its intended job fail. A window that reports
rotation direction or objective activity may remain useful without revealing a
target's exact later position. No universal information-expiry threshold is implied.

### Strong elevated hold with infrequent use

Low occupancy is inconclusive. Determine what the position actually controls, how
the occupant arrives, what a follower can see during the ascent, and what is lost
by assigning a player there. Then compare occupying it, visibly threatening it,
and conceding it while contesting elsewhere. An infrequently occupied position can
still consume an opponent's attention or make a flank credible.

Test whether its advantage converts into objective pressure during the relevant
phase. A long return or unhelpful view can explain low use without requiring a buff.
If opponents must always spend disproportionate resources on it even when empty,
inspect that dominance rather than increasing its attractiveness. If it is useful
only after a plant, that may be a deliberate situational role.

Name a plausible departure trigger, such as losing the supporting angle, the other
site becoming urgent, or the objective moving beyond its influence. Inspect the
return cost. A committed ambush may intentionally lack a safe retreat; the test is
whether its benefit and opponent responses fit the intended role, not whether every
hold has an escape door.

### Loss of the support player

Repeat the window-entry and elevated-hold situations after removing the named
support job. Also test suppression with the player alive: manpower and usable
support are different. Record exactly which observation, firing angle or return
route is no longer covered.

A rational response might be to delay entry for a recheck, substitute another player,
bypass the off-site holder while containing the return, concede the hold, or switch
objectives. The map need not preserve the original plan unchanged in a casualty
state. A concern arises if supposedly independent options all secretly require the
same lost support and no timely adaptation remains within the intended mode.

## Smallest useful validation sequence

1. Establish consequential movement/body, utility, objective-clock and interaction
   assumptions. Treat the supplied ten seconds as a scenario premise until measured.
2. Inspect the named window, shortcut exits and elevated ascent from opposing,
   leader and follower positions. Separate optical views from traversable openings.
3. Exercise both rotations with the actual body, including destination preparation.
   Keep path-distance estimates separate from performed movement timings.
4. Run the paired observation/entry comparison and pre-/post-plant rotation scenarios
   with support intact, suppressed and absent. Record alternate decisions and failed
   attempts, not only route usage or kills.
5. Select a bounded geometry variant only when a comparison identifies a concrete
   failure or a worthwhile opportunity. Recheck its affected neighbors and phase
   reversal. Multiplayer trading, utility and audio remain target-engine questions.

## Skill behavior observed

The revised instructions supported conditional decisions without forcing a new
route, additional cover, equal usage or a universally safe fallback. They caused
the ten-second observation case to be evaluated as a team handoff, and caused the
rear rotation to be evaluated differently before and after planting. The infrequent
hold was not automatically removed or strengthened. Unsupported balance claims and
mechanics were left open.

No consequential ambiguity was demonstrated by this bounded scenario. No canonical
skill edits are requested from this review. This is one successful reasoning example,
not evidence that the skills reliably improve built maps or play outcomes.
