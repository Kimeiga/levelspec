# Rivergate / design hypotheses

## Arrangements considered before 3D

1. River across the map east–west, attackers south and defenders north, sites on opposite banks: rejected because one site would be available before crossing and reinforcement would repeatedly demand the same exposed crossing.
2. River north–south, one large center bridge and two banks: rejected because all five attackers funnel through the same early commitment and a single smoke could suppress most movement.
3. Selected: river north–south, T west, both sites east, CT between them behind administration. Three separated crossing encounters: north stone with an offset toll shelter, dogleg island with an approach hidden by a pier, and south lock with bounded views and a roof. Mid control grants access to the backs of the two site gatehouses; it is a split opportunity rather than a single overlook into both plants.

The river is a deliberate large nonplayable void and a strong orientation landmark. Adjacent architecture narrows its influence; the rest of the plan uses shared gatehouses and courts rather than three detached rooms at the ends of corridors. Spawns have two exits around solid masses. Bank routes allow quiet staging and changes of plan before a crossing; east gallery control adds the risk of being pinched between sites.

## Why this elevation

A wholly flat scheme preserves clean crossing silhouettes but leaves A's rear a broad ground hold. A district-wide grade would give the eastern bank continuous superiority over every crossing; stacked bridge routes would introduce more independent threats at mid. A local 1.2m customs loading hold creates a single bounded elevated defender/postplant choice with two stair accesses. A-customs and E-archive keep it from also becoming a mid/B observation tower. Exposure of A-refuge from the hold is intentional; low cover there is directional, not universally safe.

## Roles and responses to test

- North: attacker pair can prepare behind N-stage, advance by the bridge's low parapets and toll shelter, then choose the customs north lip or stall and return. Defender influence on bridge costs attention on A-short around the south of A-customs. N-toll blocks one direct lane but allows more than3m passing room above it. Smoke coverage, sniper timings and trades require a real engine.
- Island: a 1–2 player contest can open A-short or B-short. The pier forces a local direction change and allows a return to west quay. It does not provide immediate view of either plant. A team conceding mid must decide whether to reinforce each short entrance or push a side crossing for information.
- Lock: a3–5 player approach can stage west, use a6m-wide enclosed crossing, and enter B around turbine. A short split arrives north of the turbine mass; coordination should matter more than merely adding a third doorway to the plant. S-piston and S-lock create two protection scales; roof changes grenade arcs only once configured in the destination engine.
- CT: rear lane remains the reliable A↔B rotation; gallery is shorter in some positions but contested. Administration stops direct spawn-to-mid views. Two defenders can retreat from a site into the rear, but should lose plant observation while doing so.
- Postplant2v2: hold the site plus one approach, or concede plant observation for information at gallery. A hold trades superior sight height against visible stair approaches. B generator separates plant and rear views; two retakers can choose main and short or move together. In1v2, survival and bomb time can favor the attackers; geometry need not make the solo retake equally likely.

## Object roles / limits

W/E building masses are optical and physical blockers, not enterable rooms. N-toll, mid-pier, S-piston and site cargo/generator offer full-height protection with several sides to clear. Amber low obstacles and bridge parapets provide duck/preparation positions and direction-dependent refuges. Each gap between refuges remains an exposed commitment. Other elevation or controls can invalidate low cover; inspect opposing eyes and bodies. All physical pieces are static opaque solids. Bullet resistance, penetration, destruction, boost access and grenade interactions remain unknown.

## Engine playtest priorities

1. Five versus five, alternating sides and initial2A/1mid/2B defense: time first contact at all bridges, defender setup lead, attacker ability to reach island before every approach is held. Track actual times, utility spent and reasons for abandoned routes.
2. North execute versus sniper+support: test whether a bridge pair can advance and trade; adjust toll/shelter/entrance shape locally if it becomes five serial duels. Test aggressive CT crossing and west-bank spawn-exit containment.
3. Three lock/two island split against two B and mid support: verify the main/short split is timely and changes turbine clearing. Test lock smoke/incendiary denial and reset space.
4. A and B2v2/1v2 postplants with bomb timers varied: compare grouped and split retakes, escape/repeek positions, A hold dominance, audible rotations and low-cover exposure.
5. After opponents learn the plan: test whether mid fakes and crossing changes remain credible and whether rear rotations are routinely too quick/slow. Collect participant explanations as well as side win rates.

No human or combat-bot playtest has been performed. Static success is geometry evidence only.
