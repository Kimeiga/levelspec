# Greywatch — design hypotheses

The chosen composition is a split castle ward around a solid keep, with a cross-wall walk passing above lower mid. A perimeter-only siege layout was rejected because it made every site approach a single long commitment. A full underground crypt was rejected because it hid all late rotations in a third disconnected-looking level. The retained wall walk makes a visible lateral option and allows mid to branch beneath it into site approaches; all four stairs rejoin ordinary floors.

T may pressure A from Outer Ward then use the west stair tower to switch to the wall walk before reaching site. Taking the walk costs a climb and crossing exposure but allows an eastward reinforcement. Opponents can contest either landing or give the bridge up and hold the exit stair. A's outer-ward and short entries do not share one doorway. The fountain offers a directional crouch refuge while the east stone provides a tall reset, vulnerable from the north cloister.

Lower Mid offers ground access to A short or the chapel vestibule, while the wall walk is a separate route overhead. A low well and chest provide preparation and retreat; the keep cheek turns the A-short approach so one central hold should not watch both objectives. Holding mid should help communicate a split, not reveal the full spawn or plant zones.

B's kitchen lane bends around a service wing and enters a vestibule below the east landing. The separate west transfer from mid joins this vestibule. The nave wall limits the first appearance into the roofless chapel; nave pillars and sarcophagus let a pair establish successive positions. The broken eastern ambulatory branches at the vestibule, wraps outside the nave wall, and enters from the east through x78,y64–70. It costs extra travel and an exposed turn but creates a distinct side threat; a pair can delay the main gate while supporting this approach. A CT can contest the vestibule or save space to use the rear apse entry in a retake. Whether vestibule control still overconcentrates the split depends on clearing and utility in engine testing.

CT rotations use the rear cloister around the keep: shorter than returning through contested T territory, but they abandon information at the vacated site. The fifth CT is a rear flex: a mid contest requires advancing through A short or the chapel vestibule, not a dedicated direct CT-to-mid route. This limits free central reinforcement. Loss of a site makes the rear cloister a retake route, not a protected spawn guarantee.

All first-contact and rotation times must be measured after compilation. Distance / 3.4 m/s is only a harness travel estimate, excluding player interference, weapon movement speeds, utility, clearing, spawn variance and combat. No symmetry or route count is offered as proof of balance.

Playtest follow-up: compare fast five-player pushes against 2-site defaults; log first contact, plant success, trade delays and abandoned routes. Test 2v2 retakes with low utility; inspect elevated exposure of every low refuge. Record repeated unreachable holds, dominant post-plants, timing gaps and whether bridge use remains valuable after discovery. Tune only supported findings.
