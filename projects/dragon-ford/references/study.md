# Reference study — Dragon Ford (Kowloon Walled City, inspiration mode)

Lookup on 2026-11-09. Purpose: ground an invented map in the place's spatial organization. Nothing here is traced; images are research evidence only, not textures. ## Observations per image (what the image shows)

1. `plan-streets-1980s.jpg` — park exhibit plan of building lots and alleys. Site is a rough rectangle with a bowed north edge. Alleys (purple) form an irregular, jogging quasi-grid, not a clean grid and not pure maze: roughly 6-8 north-south runs and 5-6 east-west runs, few of them straight for the full site; many dead-end spurs. Thin red lines with dots thread through blocks off the alleys (interior passage/stair routes; legend not legible). Small triangles mark entries all along every perimeter edge. Centre: a large pale compound labelled 老人中心 (old people's centre = Yamen) with 青年中心 (youth centre) on its north side and a highlighted long narrow block on its west side captioned "Lung Chun Free School Building". 天后廟 (Tin Hau temple) is a separate lot one block north-east of the compound. Green dots (captioned as factory sites) cluster along one north-south alley on the east side, captioned Kwong Ming Street. A long east-west alley passes just south of the Yamen front. "Tung Tsing Road Sitting Out Area" labelled at the east edge.
2. `aerial-1989.jpg` — oblique aerial. One solid mass of abutting towers of unequal height; the only hole is a rectangular central void, with a low dark roof at its floor and sheer tower walls rising straight from its edges on all sides. Roofscape is a field of stepped terraces, each lot a different height. Park/playground and a ball court on two sides (cleared Sai Tau Tsuen); a road and long slab block on the far side with a quarried hill behind.
3. `south-side-sai-tau-tsuen-1975.jpg` — tower wall rising directly behind a carpet of 1-2 storey tiled/tin-roof squatter houses and sheds; bamboo scaffolding on a tower; vehicles on a lane at the squatter edge. No setback between low village and towers.
4. `alley-covered-1993.jpg` — alley about 1.5 m wide, wet uneven concrete floor with a slight rise/step, corrugated sheet canopies and cable bundles forming a ceiling at about 2.5-3 m, fluorescent tube lighting, shuttered timber/metal shopfronts flush to the alley, baskets stacked at a doorway. No sky visible.
5. `alley-sai-shing-7th-lane.jpg` — alley with a name plate (西城七巷, Sai Shing 7th Lane, erected by the kaifong welfare association); a dense ceiling of pipes and hoses just above head height; alley kinks and widens at a junction lit from above; barred windows and gates; rubbish piled at a wall.
6. `perimeter-street-night-1993.jpg` — perimeter vehicle street: continuous lit shop frontage with signboards at ground and first floor, caged balconies above for 10+ storeys, facade line stepping in plan as lots change.
7. `dentist-frontage-tung-tsing-road-1991.jpg` — pavement-level dentist/clinic shopfronts with roller shutters and folding grilles, tin awnings, a projecting first-floor tin terrace over the pavement, signs on steel outriggers; one sign reads 東正道11號 (11 Tung Tsing Road). Street slopes gently along the frontage.
8. `park-yamen-overview-2018.jpg` — today's park from the air: the Yamen is three parallel tiled halls in a row with two courtyards between them, enclosed by side wings; a paved forecourt in front; the sunken South Gate excavation lies a short walk in front of the forecourt. Ground visibly terraces up toward the road and hill side.
9. `yamen-front-courtyard.jpg` — view through the entrance door: steps up into a paved court about three bays wide, flanked by two-storey-height side wings with small openings, closed by an open-fronted hall with columns and a second flight of steps. Each hall sits higher than the last.
10. `yamen-entrance-cannon.jpg` — single-storey gatehouse with recessed porch, "ALMSHOUSE" lintel, steps plus ramp, flagstone forecourt, cannon on a timber carriage at the forecourt edge.
11. `south-gate-remnants.jpg` — excavated pit about 2 m below park level with brick retaining walls: granite paving and wall footings, and later concrete column bases/ground beams sitting on top of them.
12. `section-panorama-overview.jpg` — whole cut: about ten distinct tower groups of 8-14 storeys with deep notches between; ground line steps (not a smooth ramp), rising by about one storey along the cut.
13. `section-panorama-detail-lo-yan-street.jpg` — labels include passage "Lung Chun 1st Lane", main passage "Lo Yan Street", "Lung Chun 2nd Lane", "corridor connecting to the next building", "this wall is shared by both buildings", "no lift". Alleys appear as one-storey-high covered slots under bridging upper floors; a full-height stair shaft sits between two buildings; roofs at four or more different levels with ladders, antennas, cages, plants; ground-floor shops, clinic, butchery, small factories.
14. `section-panorama-detail-tai-chang-street.jpg` — labels include main passage "Tai Chang (Big Well) Street", "passage linking three buildings", "all pipes exposed", "extensions built into the gap from both sides become passage or room", "rubbish piles up on the roof", bakery, carpentry, mahjong parlour, dumpling maker. Ground floor is double height in places; a lower roof between towers is a rubbish-covered pit.

## Spatial organization findings

1. **Yamen void.** Sole substantial open-sky void (text-sourced: Metropolis, Steadman; image-observed: 1, 2). Towers front it directly with sheer walls, no arcade or setback (image-observed: 2). Void about 35 x 50 m including the youth centre; Yamen itself about 15 x 30 m, 1 storey with taller halls (estimates scaled from image 1 against the stated site width, and image 8; +/-20%). Three halls, two courts, side wings, floor stepping up hall by hall (image-observed: 8, 9; LCSD "three rows and four wings"). Used as almshouse/old people's home from 1949 (text). Entered from alleys on all four sides, with the main east-west alley across its front (image-observed: 1). Refuse was thrown into the courtyard before collection began (text: Steadman).
2. **Alley network.** Irregular quasi-grid with jogs and spurs; blocks about 15-40 m (image-observed/estimate: 1). "Only four alleys" gave a direct north-south link between Tung Tau Tsuen Road and Lung Chun Road, and Lung Chun Road was a footpath on the south edge (text, snippet only). Widths "often only 1-2 m" (text: Wikipedia); about 1.5 m seen (4, 5). Named: Lo Yan St, Tai Chang St, Kwong Ming St, Lung Chun 1st/2nd Lane, Sai Shing 7th Lane (image labels). Fort "built on a slope" (text); ground steps in the section (image-observed: 12) and the hill lies on the Tung Tau Tsuen Road (north) side (image-observed: 2, 8), so north-higher is likely; total fall about 3-5 m is an estimate. Stairs within alleys: only slight steps seen (4); flights unknown.
3. **Institutions and industry.** Tin Hau temple is its own lot, about 15 x 15 m, north-east of the Yamen (image-observed/estimate: 1). A small temple near Lo Yan St was "buried under rubbish" with sky nearly invisible (text: Metropolis). An overhead wire net is NOT confirmed by any source reached (unknown). Free school building and youth centre adjoin the Yamen (image-observed: 1). Factories cluster along Kwong Ming Street and at ground/low floors: noodles, fish balls/minced fish, roast meat, sweets, plastics, textiles, golf balls (text: Steadman; image-observed: 1, 13, 14). A few government standpipes; private wells pumped to rooftop tanks, hoses hung from corridor ceilings (text: Steadman, Metropolis).
4. **Upper circulation.** Upper-level corridors link buildings; one could cross north-south without touching ground (text: Wikipedia, Metropolis). Stair shafts and party walls shared between buildings; floors cantilever over alleys until they touch (text; image-observed: 13, 14). Two lifts in about 350 buildings (text: Steadman). Roofs are stepped terraces crossed by ladders, with antennas, tanks, cages, pigeon lofts, rubbish (image-observed: 2, 12-14; text). Alleys are mostly covered tunnels with pipe/cable ceilings at 2.5-3 m, opening to slots only at junctions (image-observed: 4, 5, 13).
5. **Perimeter.** Continuous shop and dentist frontage on the vehicle streets, including Tung Tsing Road on the east (image-observed: 6, 7); "dental clinics lined one edge" (text). Two-storey Sai Tau Tsuen squatter area abutted south and west until 1985, then park and ball courts (text; image-observed: 2, 3). South Gate was the main entrance; its footings survive in a pit in front of the Yamen forecourt, with post-war column bases built over them (image-observed: 8, 11). Two 1802 cannons at the Yamen front (text; one seen in 10).
6. **Dimensions.** Site about 210 x 120 m, 2.6 ha (text; the brief's 213 x 126 m is consistent). About 350 buildings, 10-14 storeys, height capped by the Kai Tak flight path (text). About 1 m gaps between buildings (text: Steadman). Storey height: unknown in text; about 2.6-3 m is an estimate from 14 storeys within the roughly 40-45 m cap. Flats typically 23 m2 (text).

## Architectural relationships worth inventing from

1. A low tiled courtyard compound at the bottom of a sheer-walled sky void, the only daylight in the map (1, 2, 8).
2. A compound that steps up hall by hall: gate, court, open hall, court, rear hall, with side wings as flanking routes (8, 9).
3. A forecourt with a cannon and a sunken ruin pit (old gate footings under later column stumps) between perimeter and centre (8, 10, 11).
4. Tunnel alleys under pipe ceilings that open to brief lit slots only at junctions, so junctions read as landmarks (4, 5, 13).
5. One long east-west spine across the compound front versus few direct north-south links, most of which jog (1; snippet).
6. A stepped site: alleys gain about a storey across the map by short flights, so one block's ground floor meets the next block's first floor (12, text slope; magnitude estimated).
7. A stair shaft and corridor shared by two or three buildings, giving a first/second-floor crossing over an alley (13, 14).
8. A factory street: one alley lined with double-height ground-floor workshops (noodle, fish ball, plastics) with deep interiors (1, 14).
9. A small temple lot hemmed in by towers, sky nearly blocked overhead; a debris net is invention, not evidence (1; Metropolis).
10. Roofscape of unequal terraces joined by ladders, with a low rubbish-filled roof pit between higher neighbours (2, 12, 14).
11. An outward-facing shop/dentist arcade with tin awnings and a projecting first-floor terrace over the pavement (6, 7).
12. A low squatter-shed or park/ball-court apron pressed against the tower wall on one side, an open approach contrasting with the interior (2, 3).

## Limits

- Lambot/Girard's book plans and survey drawings, and cityofdarkness.co.uk (404), were not accessible; alley counts beyond the exhibit plan rest on a search snippet.
- The plan photo is oblique and its small Chinese labels are only partly legible; alley-name-to-line matching and all scaled dimensions are estimates.
- The section's cut direction and position are not stated by the source reached.
- No photo of the Yamen inside the towers at ground level, the Tin Hau temple, standpipes, upper corridors or rooftops at eye level was retained; those rest on text, the aerial and the drawn section.
- Three retained images (the section panorama scans and the Sai Shing lane photo) are copyrighted: keep them out of published outputs.
