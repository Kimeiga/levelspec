# Greywatch — independent tactical layout review r2

Verdict: **changes required before production blockout**. Scope: strategy/player behavior, target 5v5 CT/T bomb defuse. This is a drawing hypothesis review, not observed play or compiled geometry acceptance.

I read the brief and inventory and visually inspected the full layout PNG before reading the author's design hypotheses or other review verdicts. I did not read other reviewers' conclusions.

## Supported strengths

The two sites flank a solid keep and share a rear cloister. A CT rotation abandons the old site and traverses an identifiable commitment; the keep prevents a central omniscient perch. T can switch approaches in the south apron, then choose ground mid, outer ward or the kitchen side. The wall walk crosses mid without a junction and offers a later cross-wing transfer at the cost of a climb and bridge exposure. These are materially different journeys, not interchangeable corridor labels.

A supports a 2-player outer-ward push combined with A-short pressure. `W-court-west/east` gives a bounded first entry, while the short route emerges farther east. A pair holding fountain and east stone must change coverage when both entries activate. The north cloister is a plausible retreat and retake route, with the fountain and bench making a concrete post-plant commitment.

## Required layout changes

**T1 — B-nave gate concentrates every T approach at one final frontier.** Lower mid, kitchen lane and east wall-walk stairs all reach the same vestibule, then cross the single x62–67 gap in the wall at y58. A defender can give up the vestibule and retain one common last appearance; the bridge does not purchase a different B entry. In a 5-player B execute, numerical advantage is converted largely into sequential entry through that frontier. In 3v5 or a low-utility 2v2, switching from kitchen to mid does not alter the defender's final coverage task. This is not a universal requirement for two doors: the concrete issue is that three costly approaches converge without buying a changed site threat.

Repair the local chapel interface: add an architecturally plausible side aisle/ruined ambulatory that branches during the vestibule approach and reaches the nave from a different side or height, with a real travel/exposure cost and a receiving space. An east exterior aisle wrapping the nave is one option. Two adjacent gaps in the same south wall would be a weaker repair. Preserve the nave mass, the rear CT approach and legible room purpose. Review the changed branch and plant/rear-door relationship before production.

**T2 — brief's default mid CT needs a revised description.** CT spawn reaches lower mid only by passing through a site and continuing south beyond its final entrance. T has the much more direct northbound route from the gatehouse. Therefore “2 A / 1 mid support / 2 B” reads as a starting allocation the drawn CT deployment does not safely provide. Call this 2 A / 2 B / 1 rear flex, with optional forward mid pressure acquired later, or explicitly measure and justify the proposed mid deployment. A direct CT-mid bypass is not required; the existing commitment can be intentional.

## Scenario assessment / checks carried forward

- **5v5 default vs 2–1–2 probe:** A pair can support two distinct A entries; the T mid player can choose which site to reinforce. CT rear flex has a legible decision after information. Mid ownership should be treated as contested/acquired, not a free CT starting position.
- **Five-player rush A:** 5 m ward gap and 6 m stair widths offer potential throughput, while the dogleg and court threshold give CT a delay/retreat. Verify real bodies can fan into site and that the upper west landing does not see over low cover into all A holds.
- **Five-player rush B:** unresolved T1 currently compresses all methods of attack into one final gate. Reassess after the aisle repair; do not infer grenade effectiveness from the drawing.
- **3v5 after an early loss:** A survivors can sell outer pressure and leave through mid/upper routes; retreat costs travel and abandoned information. B survivors currently cannot change the last site entry, reinforcing T1. An undermanned CT team can save the rear route rather than needing simultaneous forward control everywhere.
- **2v2 post-plant, one player low on utility:** A's rear approach faces a broad courtyard and can be supported by a longer route through a lost site/mid, but that flank must be timed against the actual planted clock. B's rear apse door is distinct from the T gate but flank viability is unresolved until T1 and travel measurements. A north-only retake may intentionally remain difficult; do not promise a usable late flank without timing it.
- **Isolated 1v2:** low objects let the holder change exposure but cannot conceal standing bodies. Check that `A-stone`, chapel piers and the upper merlons permit an actual next move, not an invulnerable hold. Opposing pair views and trade paths are required.

## Deferred blockout / engine evidence

Measure spawn-to-first-contact and site-to-site path lengths on actual navigation; record distance/speed estimates separately from collision-aware walking time. Inspect both ends of all four stairs and the bridge passing strip. Capture attacker/defender views at standing/crouch eyes for A, B, both landings and lower-mid fork. Check planted zones are unobstructed and allow a supported body. Verify spawn areas, not only center pairs, remain visually screened. In the target engine, test body blocking, utility, penetration, sounds, actual movement clocks and ten-player rounds. No balance or multiplayer acceptance is claimed.

## r4 adjudication — layout accepted for production blockout

I independently inspected the updated r4 layout image and changed brief/inventory before reading the revised design explanation. The new broken eastern ambulatory occupies x78–84,y50–70, branches from the eastern vestibule, wraps the south nave screen, and meets the nave from its east side at y64–70. `B-aisle-screen` preserves a bounded turn; `B-aisle-bench` offers one directional preparation point on the approach. This is a real side-entry journey into a different threat angle rather than a second adjacent door.

**T1 closed at layout stage.** A T pair can now delay or show at the main nave gate while a partner uses the aisle; a CT watching the south gate must either allocate another player to east or move behind a pillar and concede some observation. An isolated holder has a meaningful coverage cost. The aisle is slower and more committed than the direct gate, and its final turn can be challenged from the nave. A five-person execute can distribute between the direct and side entries. A 3v5 T group can change the final site angle after learning the main hold, while a CT rear entry still gives a separate retake direction. The extra path length depends on the chosen fork/start and plant target, so the proposed 15–20 m cost remains an estimate to replace with measured paths; no particular balance outcome is claimed.

**T2 closed.** Brief and design now describe 2 A / 2 B / 1 rear flex and a forward-acquired mid contest.

The targeted repairs preserve accepted A/ward, bridge/underpass and rear-cloister relationships. **Tactical layout gate r4: accepted for production blockout.** Carry forward all deferred 3D/engine checks above, including actual first contact, spawn-screen throughput, side-entry opposing views, pillar trade visibility, plant usability, reverse retreat and timed 2v2 retake options. This acceptance is a design hypothesis gate, not gameplay or blockout acceptance.
