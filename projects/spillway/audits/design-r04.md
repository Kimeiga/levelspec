# Spillway — design hypotheses

The upper crossing trades access and exposure for a late destination choice. The lower mid route is shorter and broad enough to support a pair, but threatens the ground B-short and A rear-gallery connectors rather than providing bridge access. Two ground sites keep plant-state communication readable; elevation changes approach and support, not objective-floor ambiguity.

The first independent layout reviews receive brief, drawing and dimensions before these hypotheses.

## Scale

Repository Dust II source XY profiles span about 91.7 × 98.7 m; their valid XY polygon union is about 4,826 m². Spillway envelope is 84 × 92 m; its initial ground union was 5,044 m² before cover/boundaries. R04 removes 96 m² buried beneath the filled ramp from the ground slab contract (4,948 m² remaining). These are authored blockout footprint comparisons, not Valve measurements or equal travel times. Spillway adds upper walking surface; the two maps need not have identical combat density.

## Consequential choices

- **West Long / A:** W-01 breaks a straight long-range channel; W-02 makes the forecourt a dogleg with a staging pocket. The A gate has a 6 m nominal opening. An entry/support pair can stage, but must contest A-01 and the north/rear side after crossing. The ground connector from mid reaches the rear gallery around S-02; it cannot cross the filled stair body directly into A. An upper attacker can descend S-02, while a ground flanker must clear the rear. A defender at A-01 switches protection between gate and rear; a successful split removes the protection. A-03 is a committed back hold, vulnerable once its position is known. A-02 offers low cover with exposure to standing peeks; engine body/camera checks remain open.
- **Mid:** T-01 and M-01 interrupt a spawn-to-spawn firing lane. M-01 forces a visible side choice while leaving an 8 m nominal eastern bypass. Mid control opens the A-gallery connector and B-short but does not confer the upper bridge. A team conceding mid can reinforce rear entrances and delay a site, at the cost of information and split risk. No simultaneous-observation claim from a drawn centerline.
- **Upper maintenance route:** S-01 is reached from lower-mid/stair court; S-02 descends into A rear; R-01 reaches the B approach. U-N and the south screens prevent a player from watching both sites and all of mid. The six-meter bay U-V watches only south-mid. Occupying it exposes an elevated player to the reverse ground view and requires attention away from bridge access. Conceding the bridge and holding its exits is a plausible response. It is not an unanswerable global sniper perch.
- **East / B:** E-01 creates a narrow local approach with a connected elbow from lower-mid. B-02 separates east-entry checks, while B-01 provides plant protection from one direction and is exposed to a ground-short split. B-03 offers a limited low hold. R-01 approaches B through the same forecourt as East Long, so crossing the bridge is a repositioning option, not a free entrance behind every defender.
- **Rear gallery:** CT starts closer to sites; rotations are available behind them. CT-01 prevents a straight view into mid, creates a local 3 m nominal passage and costs attention at the mouth. Losing a site changes rear-gallery ownership: that circulation is not guaranteed safe after a plant.

## Engine playtest priorities

1. 5v5 opening: two site holders, one mid, two flexible defenders versus a distributed attacking default. Record first reliable information, early contact, rotation time, utility spent and whether taking upper control offers a timely choice. Compare opponents holding exits instead of contesting the bridge.
2. A execute: three gate / two rear-gallery or upper players against two holders plus rotating support. Test first entry and follower sightlines; record whether coordinated split beats a known hold without a mandatory perfect grenade.
3. B execute: east/elbow approach plus ground-short pressure. Check whether B-02 isolates checks or produces serial deaths. Test an early ramp hold and a deliberate bridge concession.
4. 2v2 post-plant at each site: two plausible plant positions, different attacker holds, upper/rear retake pairs. Record if cover changes ownership meaningfully and whether the second player can trade.
5. 1v2 post-plant: different remaining clocks and utility; confirm a solo defender can seek information and isolate a fight without assuming equal winning odds. Bridge rotation must fit actual remaining time to count as a response.
6. Movement/utility checks in a target engine: exact body and eye heights, jump/boost onto 3 m walls, ramp visibility while ascending, smoke coverage, projectile clipping, penetration and sound. Adjust clocks/equipment only after observations; no static balance certification.
