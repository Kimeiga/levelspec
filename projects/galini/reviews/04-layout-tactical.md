# Independent tactical layout review — L03

**Verdict: accepted for blockout development.** No supported tactical defect requires a layout change before modeling. This verdict supersedes the L02 tactical review and applies to the curved/angled L03 proposal only. Compiled inspection and multiplayer balance remain unperformed.

Inspected raw `reviews/evidence/layout-l03.png` and the L03 floor/stair/object coordinates in `plan.json` before reading `brief.md`, `layout-packet.md`, then `design.md`. Scope: 5v5, one life, two-site defusal; provisional 115s round, 40s planted clock, kit/no-kit defuse and 5m/s run assumptions. These mechanics are not implemented by the harness. No geometry was authored by this reviewer.

## Routes and decisions

Ferry still offers outer-west, civic-square and outer-east approaches, but their movement and information now follow different spatial forms. Sea Ribbon bends into a broad belvedere and a turning two-flight stair, whereas Bell takes a shorter diagonal ascent out of the piazza. Taverna and Harbor wrap the headland before entering B. They are not interchangeable journeys. Once an outer attacker advances, switching sites requires a substantial return south or taking the defended site/upper lane. That commitment is legible and reasonable under the assumed round clock.

Olive Piazza control offers Bell pressure, Fishmarket pressure or the Gate climb. The first two retain proximity to a site split; the third can threaten CT reinforcement but takes a player away from the plant. Gate's diagonal stair and tall sidewalls should constrain its high view toward the rounded foot rather than automatically showing both plaza branches. That is a drawing prediction, requiring actual offset body views.

The Upper Ribbon joins the sites through a bent route with the CT square and Gate connection. A→B via this route is approximately 80–90m along provisional plan waypoints, allowing for obstacle clearing: roughly 16–18s at the assumed 5m/s, or 24–27s at 3.4m/s harness walking. This is a geometric estimate, not a navigation measurement or observed game travel. A prompt rotate can fit a 40s postplant and kit attempt; a delayed lone retaker may have little time to clear. Gate control can make this route unsafe, so the proposed reinforcement advantage has an actionable cost.

## Scenarios and responses

1. **5v5 opening.** A 2A/1Gate/2B CT distribution fits the connections. The Gate defender covers the northern ascent without personally securing both sites. A T 1Sea/2Piazza/2Harbor spread can discover resistance and consolidate through the square. CTs have shorter drawn access to both sites; Ts appear to reach the lower civic area before CTs can descend into it. Compile actual distances and first-contact views before making a timing claim. The cliff turn makes Sea pressure later than Bell pressure; a split needs staging or deliberate timing rather than simultaneous sprinting from spawn.

2. **3T versus 2CT A split.** One pair can prepare Bell while a Sea attacker clears the +2m landing and upper flight. These fronts arrive from different directions. A defender around the cistern can move to limit one view but cannot assume a universal view through the chapel and both stair sidewalls. A CT can concede a stair head and play deeper court; T can occupy the vacated threshold, threaten the plant and force a response. The upper entrance behind the chapel remains the reinforcement/retake front. No roof or chapel interior escape is credited. Leader/follower sight on both cliff flights and the diagonal Bell flight must be inspected; nominal 6m width does not establish a trade.

3. **3v3 B execute.** Harbor and Fishmarket are distinct entry frontiers, though adjacent enough that a defender near (84,65,0) may watch both within a modest angular sector. This is the principal B hold to test. It is not automatically an unanswerable hold: from the drawing it is also exposed to both attackers, and moving behind the boathouse changes which approach the defender sees. Ts can coordinate pressure from the Market bend and Harbor staging corner, then clear the boathouse's eastern side before leaving it unattended. A lone attacker entering either frontier has a materially harder clear. Utility effectiveness is unknown until engine testing.

4. **2v2 A postplant, little utility.** Ts can hold cistern/court plus a stair-side position or actively contest the upper entrance. CTs can enter together through Upper Ribbon and clear around the chapel/cistern, or invest time in Gate and Bell for another direction. The long alternative is clock-sensitive and need not guarantee a fair duel. Cistern h2.5 provides a same-level hard optical break; the low planter cannot be treated as universal cover from every upper or stair angle.

5. **2v2 B postplant / 1v2 kit retake.** A Harbor holder and Market holder must account for CT descent through Boat Stair and the boathouse pocket. Retaining-house mass keeps the upper lane from becoming an unobstructed lower-district perch, but the stair's own axis is intentionally exposed. A descending CT may see over the h1.1 salt bins. A lone CT can use house/boathouse edges to sequence information, but has no guaranteed isolated duel or enough time after a late call. This difficulty is an intended casualty/clock state, not a reason to add another doorway.

6. **CT push after lost square contact.** Descending Gate gives CT information about the foot and plaza at the cost of abandoning the high hold and upper-lane watch. Ts can wait near the bakery bend or threaten Bell/Market while the defender advances. The forward CT cannot simultaneously clear the plaza and retain both sites. Confirm the high Gate viewpoint does not collapse this trade by revealing both plaza branches and deep bakery safety without exposing a useful body target.

7. **Failed outer approach.** Sea or Harbor players can withdraw through their curved approach to Ferry and regroup at the square. This costs time and gives up pressure, but the route remains meaningful in an early or middle round. A very late pivot may fail under the assumed clock. The calm belvedere and taverna stretches are useful preparation space; they do not need added flank doors solely to create constant decisions.

## Blockout priorities and qualified risks

- **B combined hold:** inspect standing/crouched views around (84,65,0), with opposing Market frontiers around (73,58,0) and Harbor around (83,55,0), plus their followers behind each bend. Determine what the defender sees, what exposes it, and how repositioning changes the other frontier. Do not classify this hold solely by an FOV angle.
- **Gate:** capture head, mid-climb, rounded foot and reverse plaza views. Check both branch visibility and visible body exposure, including lateral offsets instead of only the stair centerline.
- **A split support:** inspect the two Cliff flights and intervening +2m turn independently; then Bell and the A-facing exits. Include follower frames and cistern-side defender offsets. The lower and upper cliff flights cannot be treated as one straight support line.
- **Rotations/clock:** measure Upper Ribbon A↔B, CT→each site, T→each approach and Mid→each split. Keep game-speed estimates separate from harness walk times. Inspect the O-ROTATE bypass during contact from Gate and during a site rotation.
- **Bypass clarification accepted:** the author corrected the packet to describe the southern O-ROTATE bypass as approximately1.4–1.7m, a deliberate narrow alternative to the wider northern route. A narrower optional side is not itself a tactical defect or a reason to widen both sides. Verify unchanged-body passage and the actual intended teammate passing route in the compile.
- **Boundaries:** verify five stair flights, full endpoint support, low sea guards and no unintended scenic/roof shortcuts. Rounded and oblique joins must preserve the intended route commitments rather than admit a bypass.

These are drawing hypotheses and scoped review questions. Actual compilation, collision-aware movement and opposing views are required for blockout acceptance. Crowd blocking, trading, grenade trajectories, penetration, sound, networking, objective clocks and competitive balance remain target-engine/playtest work.
