# Galini S01 — opposing plans and information

Reviewer: `/root/reboot_opposing_review`. Both alternatives are **promising**. I favor **B / Ravine Quarter** as the more consequential opposing-plan experiment: choosing a town shoulder and a level changes which objective and reserve routes a player can influence. A / Harbor Fold is also coherent and has a lower-risk continuous retreat/rotation structure. B's bridges must remain commitments that players can contest or avoid; simply making both bridges broad simultaneous firing corridors would squander the concept.

## Initial observations from the drawings

**A / Harbor Fold.** Net Street, the Winery/Fountain region and Market Stoa give attackers a real choice after leaving Ferry Court: stay near B at the waterfront, gain the market shelf through one of three ascents, or continue upward toward the chapel district. CT can move between A and B through the two-stage eastern descent, but doing so is a journey rather than a vertical shortcut. The proposed V1 begins at Sea Balcony **+4**, not the chapel **+8**, and points toward Harbor Passage; V2 begins near Bell Porch and looks toward Market Stoa. These should remain distinct information positions. A single broad upper edge that watched the waterfront and market would erase the intended response cost.

**B / Ravine Quarter.** West-side attackers can gain Laundry/Press and threaten either the A-side upper streets or a middle bridge crossing toward B. A defender on the east middle shelf can react up to the civic bridge or down toward Boat Court, but that defender does not directly hold either objective. The ground Shore Toe route bypasses both bridges at the price of a long shore journey. The upper bridge arrives through Inn Archway; the middle bridge arrives through Taverna Gateway. These are promising architectural thresholds, not just lines across a void. The two drawn bridge observations V1/V2 are separated in both position and elevation; I do not claim that one player currently sees both from a single station.

The photographs support a settlement of thick building volumes, changing street levels and through-building passages. The dry ravine and bridge system are inventions, not source reconstruction. Neither alternative's pictured scale proves its travel costs are appropriate.

## Proactive proposals

### OP-GA-01 — Preserve partial, costly cross-level information in Harbor Fold (retain/develop)

Affected Sea Balcony V1 `(91,63,+4)→(103,47,0)`, Bell Porch V2 and eastern descent. Retain V1 as a **bounded view of a Harbor Passage arrival**, using the actual Sea Houses/Boathouse/retaining geometry to stop it expanding across Boat Court and its plant area. Keep Bell's Market view a different position. Do not treat an entire terrace edge as a transparent rail.

Before/after distinction: an excessively open edge would let an upper reserve confirm the B execute and cover the retake without leaving safety. A bounded opening gives that reserve one useful sighting, then forces a decision: stay to watch reinforcement, descend through S6, or rotate back toward A. A ground teammate exploits the information but must still clear the site. Attackers can show at the visible arrival, withdraw behind the Boathouse or continue through another ground threshold.

Cost: less upper fire support and incomplete knowledge of the waterfront. That incompleteness is useful. During detailed drawing, mark at least one meaningful ground plant/retreat position outside V1 and a reciprocal ground challenge to the opening. This is preservation of the drawn concept, not a finding that the current schematic has an unwanted actual sightline.

### OP-GA-02 — Give Taverna's observer a deliberate facing choice (B experiment)

Affected Middle Bridge, I04 Taverna Gateway (`+4`, west door approximately `(74,52)…(77,59)`, east door `(87,49)…(90,54)`) and Fountain Court. Compare its simple through-passage with a **short opaque internal return that offsets the east departure**, keeping both doorways and a usable through-building route. The bridge-facing position should observe the crossing; watching Fountain/S5-side reinforcement should require leaving that position or turning around the return.

Before: a generous straight gateway could let the same reserve inspect a long bridge front while remaining ready to fire onto the B-side reserve route. After: a bridge observer gains useful west-side evidence but needs a teammate or a relocation to watch the east district. T can demonstrate at Press while a shoreline teammate reaches B; CT can hold the bridge and risk less B help, or concede the entry to reinforce. Crossing remains exposed on the span—this does not add a safe tunnel across the ravine.

Cost: less through-gateway support, an extra corner to clear and a possible trap for a lone crosser. Keep the return shallow and compare leader/follower positions; do not automatically narrow the whole passage. The 3.0 m reserved interior headroom and 31 m span are still unverified concept dimensions. Another reviewer should assess an adopted return before production.

### OP-GA-03 — Separate Upper Bridge information from direct A defense (B experiment)

Affected West Lookout, M08 West Homes, A/Cistern Court and the western Upper Bridge receiving area. Develop the existing building mass so a player watching along V2 must stand on the **bridge-facing shoulder east of the A precinct**, with an opaque building corner separating that stance from immediate control of both A's S3 and S4 arrivals. Preserve a short ordinary retreat into the A district; do not add a mandatory drop.

Before: if the future enclosure leaves the west upper terrace completely open, one A anchor might see both local ascent pressure and an entire CT retake crossing. After: choosing the bridge stance earns rotation information at the cost of giving up one A-side approach; a teammate must cover that local arrival or the observer must retreat after contact. A CT team can pressure through Inn/Bridge while a west-middle survivor climbs, creating an actual attention trade.

Cost: fewer easy long crossfires and an extra receiving corner for bridge retakers. There should be a reciprocal bridge-side challenge, not a one-way slit. Compare this against leaving the current open terrace as drawn; the exact wall height, extent and any negative effect on A's own retake must be reviewed at layout stage.

## Gate and unresolved evidence

Both alternatives are promising within this early opposing-plan scope; B has the stronger experiment, while A remains a valid fallback if B's measured crossing/rotation commitments prove too expensive. Do not solve an overlong B rotation automatically by adding another bridge or ladder. First compare site/arrival positions, local reserve access and the role of Shore Toe.

Detailed review still owes both spawn departures, every objective front, the three cross-town commitments, upper/middle gateway receiving zones and all stair interfaces. Full 5v5 and reduced-player scenarios need measured routes and actual opposing views later. Sound, utility, planting behavior and competitive balance remain target-engine questions.
