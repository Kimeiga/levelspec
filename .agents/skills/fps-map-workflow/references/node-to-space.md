# Spatial exploration and design guidance

Use this for spatial invention or a consequential layout change. The
[workflow](../SKILL.md) owns stages and reviews. These principles guide judgment;
the AI chooses their concrete realization within the brief. No previous map is a
model answer, and no map type, numerical budget or feature bundle is prescribed.

## Optional design directions

These are possibilities to choose, combine or depart from, not a form to complete
or a checklist to fill. A direction can emerge through research and planning and
change later.
User specifications take precedence. Keep any direction note brief.

| Parameter | Possibilities |
| --- | --- |
| Exploration method | Concept/architecture-first, outdoor site/massing-first, node-and-edge, subtractive, terrain-led, hybrid, or another useful approach |
| Spatial composition | Room networks, corridor/hall networks, courtyard/street networks, open areas, or mixtures |
| Enclosure and spacing | Adjoining interiors, separated rooms/buildings linked by halls or paths, interior/exterior sequences, or open precincts |
| Elevation profile | Mostly flat, sustained grades, local rises/dips, or mixed |
| Storey organization | One, two, three, four, or another arrangement suited to the brief |
| Crossing relationships | At-grade junctions, overpasses, underpasses, stacked paths, or none |
| Passage character | Narrow, broad, straight, winding, or changing along a journey |
| Connectivity | Branches, loops, interlinked approaches, hubs, distributed networks, or hybrids |
| Sightline character | Bounded, long, intermittent, oblique, vertically layered, or mixed |
| Geometric language | Rectilinear, curved, angular, terrain-adapted/organic, or mixed |

Setting and form inform one another without a fixed mapping. Strict building order,
cliff-following stairs, irregular rooms or circular plazas are possible choices.
Industrial architecture need not always be square; natural terrain can coexist with
regular rooms. Choose a coherent organization rather than including every feature
or deforming everything to signal creativity.

Proactively ask what would make the place architecturally interesting. The
[architectural guidance](../../level-authoring/SKILL.md#architectural-interest) gives
illustrations, not an exhaustive palette. Explore other spatial ideas and use real
image study to discover possibilities where helpful; do not repeatedly select the
same features from a checklist.

Lean toward expressive spatial shapes and interactions where the theme permits,
including playable outlines shaped within regular construction by equipment,
setbacks, damage or terrain. Modest invention beyond literal realism is welcome.
The target is not an irregular polygon collection: a hallway can become a balcony
within a shared room volume, pass through a building, or wrap beside another space.
Invent other relationships too. Do not keep a generic box-and-tube organization
merely because its individual rectangles are architecturally possible.

## Layout order

Sketch the spatial drivers before partitioning the plan. Use reference observations
about parcel boundaries, terrain, building phases, structural bays, room programs
and public or service circulation. Trace how those drivers affect the footprints
and a route through them. Test a different organization when a sketch inherits
uniform spacing, identical junctions or a repeated room-and-corridor pattern from
the drafting grid or graph layout.

A regular office floor can follow repeated structural bays and a clear core.
A mall can have regular shop units along a concourse that widens at an anchor or
atrium. A compact lab can fit regular clean rooms along a service spine, with
equipment and transfer rooms shifting the usable route. A narrow market can keep
regular stalls grouped around an inherited alley with setbacks or loading pockets.
Choose the relationships supported by the brief and references. These examples
describe possibilities for each setting.

A mostly orthogonal plan can feel organic through different bay sizes, offsets,
staggered thresholds and changes in width and enclosure along a journey. Use
oblique or curved elements where they respond to a concrete spatial driver.
Compare the resulting routes and junctions before choosing. Rotating a repeated
layout or adding isolated angled walls leaves its underlying organization intact.

## Original composition

Create a distinct spatial organization for the brief. Let the setting's use,
architecture or terrain shape the actual spaces and movement, not just their labels
or decoration. Do not repeatedly start from
three parallel corridors with a bridge running sideways across them. Routes can be
fewer, more numerous or intertwined; their number and arrangement are design choices.
Change the underlying connections, progression, adjacency and placement of important
territories, rather than merely renaming rooms, bending lanes, mirroring the plan or
adding a different height feature to the same graph. If a recurring template is
limiting invention, change the starting method or structural assumptions. The goal
is a coherent new proposal, not arbitrary complexity or uniqueness at any cost.

## Questions for a promising spatial proposal

**Substance:** does the drawing contain enough meaningful spaces, journeys and
connections for the brief? A few named district symbols may conceal missing spatial
development. For an ordinary whole-map brief, use the
[size/substance benchmarks](../../fps-strategy-design/references/size-benchmarks.md)
as rough scope calibration while inventing a different composition. Judge what
players can actually traverse and decide, rather than an
arbitrary room count or empty footprint. Appropriate extent depends on the complexity
of those journeys: a dense room network and a few open plazas/long halls need different
treatment. Non-grid shapes alone do not establish substance. Use
[size and encounter pacing](../../fps-strategy-design/SKILL.md#size-and-encounter-pacing)
to develop that relationship rather than selecting a meter target. A compact brief
can deliberately be simple.

When the user asks for complexity comparable to developed FPS maps, inspect actual
overviews and local views of several relevant maps before accepting the proposal's
substance. The [Nuke/Overpass/Inferno study](../../../../reference/fps-complexity/README.md)
illustrates how a simple macro description can conceal substantial intermediate play.
Compare a concrete journey, its later choices, changes of view and usable responses;
do not substitute callout totals, random crossings or validation passes. If AI
development merges several explored regions into a few broad volumes, assess what
decisions survive and develop the missing sequences. A more complex starting graph
does not prevent the author from simplifying away its useful structure.

**Connection choices:** what can players do along a journey, and where can they change
plans? A bend changes a view but is not another connection. Cross-links, bypasses,
rooms joining routes and reconnections can create alternatives; deliberate commitments
and dead ends can also serve the map. More edges are not inherently better. Consider
what a link enables and what its absence preserves.

**Spatial integration:** do movement and spaces form a convincing place? This can be
adjoining interiors or separate rooms/buildings connected by long halls, courtyards,
paths, streets and avenues. Integration means coherent relationships, not compulsory
shared walls or compact packing. Develop the connecting spaces and their junctions,
thresholds, views and onward choices; they can be substantial parts of the map.
Judge actual repetition and missing development rather than rejecting long connectors,
discrete rooms, open areas or simple geometry by type. Do not mush all rooms together
or dissolve enclosure into an undifferentiated blob to avoid separate spaces.

**Coherence:** do masses, voids, room relationships and circulation suit the theme or
terrain? Let use, construction and site conditions inform shape without requiring
an engineering defense of every corner. Inspect the drawn organization rather than
relying on thematic labels. Quiet stretches, solid boundaries and scenic/inaccessible
masses can be intentional. Do not fill an arbitrary rectangle or optimize an area ratio.

**Spatial variety:** how do information, exposure, support and commitment change along
important journeys? The mechanisms might be horizontal, graded or stacked. A flat map
can be compelling; multiple levels can be coherent. Neither stairs nor straightness
establishes quality by itself. Preserve orientation and breathing room where useful.

Treat these as related questions, not independent quotas. Strong room-heavy and
corridor-heavy designs can answer them differently. If the proposal feels thin,
repetitive or incoherent, improve the relationships causing that result rather than
adding cosmetic bends, redundant rooms or a token feature. Later gameplay work can
revise the whole arrangement when evidence justifies it.

## Optional starting methods

Choose the method for the brief and uncertainty; no separate abstract graph or
generation tool is required. Explore materially different arrangements when the
important relationships remain unresolved.

**Outdoor site/massing-first:** for built outdoor settings, default to sketching the
site, roads/avenues and coherent building masses first, then develop playable streets,
paths, courts and passages between and around them. Let setbacks, facades, corners,
topography and connections shape the negative space instead of labeling a chain of
adjoining room cells as outdoors. The outline can change as the design develops.
Natural outdoor settings can use terrain masses and landforms instead of buildings.
Indoor room-and-hall composition remains useful; mixed settings can combine methods.
This is an early spatial sketch, not production 3D before its review gate.

**Concept/architecture-first:** begin with a compelling idea for the place and its
spatial identity: how its masses, voids, rooms and movement could form an interesting
architectural whole. Let that concept suggest the layout, then develop connections,
objectives, sightlines and strategies through it. The first sketch can express this
idea with provisional circulation; detailed gameplay development follows. Treat the
concept as a source of spatial relationships, not just a theme applied to an existing
lane diagram. Later evidence can still require structural changes to the architecture.

**Node-and-edge:** start with meaningful spaces or territories and their connections,
then organize them into physical architecture. Positions and links can be deliberately
chosen or randomized. Develop the connections as intentional halls, paths, streets or
other spaces; use the graph as a relationship guide rather than a literal floor plan.

**Subtractive:** start with a coherent footprint or mass of any useful shape, then
iteratively remove regions, notch boundaries and shape retained areas until rooms
and circulation emerge together. This can reveal shared boundaries, changing widths
and less predictable room shapes than placing independent boxes first. Distinguish
playable space, solid masses and open voids in the sketch. The starting outline can
change too; it is not a compulsory rectangular envelope. Keep the result architecturally
coherent rather than deforming every space or assuming irregularity establishes quality.
This is a spatial exploration method, not a requirement for a particular boolean
operation, procedural generator or implementation technique.

**Hybrid or other:** use a graph to suggest connections, carve and reshape a footprint,
then update the graph as new spatial relationships emerge. Terrain, architectural
massing or adjacency studies may instead lead the proposal. Switch or combine methods
when useful; none guarantees interest or prescribes the final geometry.

Develop provisional named spawns/objectives, intermediate territories and purposeful
connections before extensive dependent geometry. This reasoning can live within the
spatial sketch. Nodes may be rooms, junctions, courts, circulation spaces or territories;
edges may be doors, shared thresholds, passages or longer journeys. Keep the chosen
abstraction useful and consistent. An optional node/edge budget can support planning;
no universal minimum applies, and cosmetic subdivision is not additional substance.
Respect any explicit user minimums.

For randomized exploration, sample positions, sizes and candidate connections, retain
the seed/raw sketch, then deliberately organize the result. Proximities can suggest
shared walls or courts; crossings can become junctions, separated levels or links to
remove. Merge, move, reshape or discard weak elements. Do not preserve a random edge
merely because it was generated, or regularize every result back to the same template.
Randomness suggests possibilities; it does not establish architecture or quality.
A generated node or passage outline need not become its own enclosed room or hall.
Let the place's function and shared volumes reshape those boundaries, rather than
giving every random polygon a thematic name and preserving its walls.

For iterative graph exploration with explicit mobility, locks, directed approaches,
crossings, replay and several spatial interpretations, use the
[exploration lab](../../../../docs/EXPLORATION.md). Run multiple seeds or intentionally
different targets, inspect the comparison board and native geometry, then develop
the architectural relationships rather than treating the highest score as a plan.
Look up and inspect real reference images before selecting a composition and again
for uncertain detailed assemblies. Ask independent macro and local reviewers for
alternative structural changes before defending a favorite. Keep a short disposition
record for adopted, experimental, deferred and declined proposals.

For a lighter optional procedural seed, the experimental stdlib Python
[helper](../scripts/random_spaces.py) emits varied space polygons, passage ribbons
and graph metadata; use `--help` for seed, amount, extent, connectivity and output
options. Its counts/bounds are experiment inputs, not map-size targets or content
requirements. Raw shapes and declared links need architectural interpretation and
review; the JSON is seed evidence, not a LevelSpec authoring format.

## Translate relationships into space

Shape rooms, routes, masses and voids together. Related spaces can share a threshold,
view, wall or building volume, or stand apart across a courtyard, street or long hall.
Both can be coherent. Connecting outdoor space can carry routes and decisions rather
than merely fill a gap between buildings. Other boundaries intentionally withhold
access. Nodes and edges do not dictate building adjacency or one room/corridor ratio.
Architectural discoveries can change the graph as well as the reverse.

Distinguish movement, observation and support. A window can reveal a neighboring
space without admitting passage. An XY crossing is a junction only at a real transfer.
A room can connect routes directly; a corridor can wrap beside or above another
space. These are examples, not required ingredients for every map.

Develop this at the scale of a single connection as well as the whole map. Two
edges can run alongside one another at different heights for part of a journey;
a route can pass through a larger occupied room, and a turning ascent can share
its volume with another path. Do not automatically give every graph edge its own
isolated corridor. Compare what the shared volume reveals, conceals and connects,
using the [architectural guidance](../../level-authoring/SKILL.md#architectural-interest)
for turning and curved circulation as well as other possibilities. Keep actual
movement transfers distinct from visual proximity.

When choosing elevation, consider its spatial and tactical role rather than adding
height for its own sake. Broad grades, local changes and stacked routes have different
implications for support, information and return travel; flat outcomes remain valid.
Where needed, use aligned levels or a small section to explain actual relationships.

For an intended drop or other directed movement, show takeoff/landing levels, access,
return route or deliberate commitment. An unguarded edge is a design choice; a guard
is not automatic. Height alone does not prove one-way travel. Verify real step, jump,
boost, fall and recovery behavior later, or retain the uncertainty. Do not add synthetic
navigation links merely to obtain a pass.

Once a direction is promising, use [strategy design](../../fps-strategy-design/SKILL.md)
and [combat geometry](../../fps-combat-geometry/SKILL.md) for consequential decisions.
The [layout handoff](layout-handoff.md) covers detailed IDs, dimensions, sections and
evidence; the [review skill](../../fps-map-review/SKILL.md) owns the review protocol.
