## Initial comparison

**A / Courtyard Stack: promising.** The Cart Gate journey can enter Print Court and Repair Shop, then remain in the lower lightwell network or climb into a continuous inhabited gallery. This is more substantial than the original's short isolated gallery: Laundry, Well Gallery, Clinic and Tea are a district with several returns to ground, and the roof site requires another transition. Tea actually overlaps the Machine Court in plan, while the Well Gallery occupies its named lightwell edge. Those relationships can make the architecture meaningful rather than a set of stair-connected boxes. The two objective territories also differ: ground industrial interior versus roof service court. The north service route is long, but this stage does not establish travel timing.

The weakness is architectural ambiguity of the enormous uniform gray remainder. It can be adjoining inhabited buildings with lightwells, or it can become one extruded maze wall. The photographs show distinct buildings, projections, deep shop fronts and varied roof additions; they do not support treating every gray region as a single uniform tower. The open Gallery could also become merely a long fenced sidewalk unless its facades, void edge and thresholds are developed.

**B / Loft Street: promising; preferred spatial starting point.** The ordinary street passes through a real occupied factory footprint: Underpass is below Machine Loft, with Lift Lightwell cutting into the loft rather than being an unrelated room. Butcher's lower continuation to Drain/Boiler and its School ascent produce different experiences within one neighborhood. The School and East approaches arrive on opposite sides of the loft, while the optional roof transfer reconnects them away from its main floor. Porcelain Works occupies a separate ground precinct, with Sign Gallery above part of it. This changes the original's west-ground/east-roof arrangement into a more interdependent inhabited section.

Neither candidate is just the original's three northward feeds with different labels. A retains more of the original layered-gallery idea; B makes the stacked factory and through-street the center of the composition. Neither drawing yet proves a plausible enclosed building or a useful firing relationship. B's preference is a reason to develop it, not a mandate to preserve every link.
