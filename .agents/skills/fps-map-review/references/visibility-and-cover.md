# Visibility, attention and low-cover review

Use this for new FPS maps and consequential visibility/cover revisions. Keep a local
repair proportional. For a whole map or broad LOS problem, give a dedicated
independent reviewer a section inventory covering spawns and their exits, route
approaches, sites, connectors, elevated positions and transitions. Record which
sections were inspected, from which positions/heights, and what remains unknown.

## Design deliberate combat connections

Review what players should be able to see, shoot toward or support across spaces,
as well as what walls should hide. Separate movement links from observation,
firing and utility/support relationships. Full-height partitions around every
platform can erase useful cross-level fights; opening every boundary can erase
manageable fronts and protected preparation. Neither is a default solution.

Identify consequential missing interactions. Could a selected lower edge, window,
gap or gallery view make control of one space influence another approach or
rotation? Inspect actual reachable observer/target positions and useful timing,
not a scenic ray into empty space. Bound the relationship with appropriate masses,
offsets or apertures so it does not incidentally watch every route or spawn.

For each important proposed/retained view, name the action it enables, exposure
from both sides, teammate contribution, access/exit or commitment, and feasible
opponent response. Check objective ownership reversal and attention alongside
relative heights and moving bodies. A theoretical late flank is not counterplay.
Full-height isolation, deliberate exposed crossings and strong holds can still
be appropriate; no quota of windows, low walls or cross-level fights applies.

Use the [sightline research revisit](../../../../docs/research/fps-sightline-opportunities.md)
for supporting cases and counterexamples. These are design hypotheses until checked:
optical visibility alone does not verify firing, penetration, utility or trade behavior.

## Review in plan and in 3D

Before modeling, inspect the XY layout together with predicted 3D views and sections.
Use actual or explicitly provisional eye/body heights and horizontal/vertical FOV.
Include floor and slab elevations, parapets, low cover, tall buildings and overhead
occluders. Ask what an upper observer sees over lower walls, what the observer
exposes in return, and what appears while climbing the approach. Buildings can be
gameplay occluders; their tops must be high enough for the intended relationship.
Preserve the openings and headroom of routes passing through or beneath them.

For layout-scale issues, compare architectural repairs with added cover: route
bends, offset entrances, room outlines and building projections may create a clearer
encounter than another isolated block. Inspect the overall room identity and visual
composition as well as each ray. Preserve useful direct routes, passing/support
space, utility preparation and legibility; winding geometry is a means, not a goal.

After compiling, repeat the same observer/target studies against actual geometry.
Use opposing ground/elevated views, offsets across spawn and holding areas, and
positions along ramps and stairs. A top-down drawing or attractive aerial camera
does not establish player visibility. Recheck repaired sections and connected
regions in another round, retaining the source/configuration revision and evidence.

## Review elevation at its chosen scale

Compare flat maps/sections, gradual grades across corridors or districts, local
steps/crests and stacked routes where relevant. Name the decision the chosen scale
creates and relate higher/lower territory to objective approaches and microfeatures.
Uniform height noise or a few isolated mounds do not establish useful variation.
Check whether high and low threats become visible together and whether the
following teammate can support the same fight. Flat is a valid design outcome.

Use sections during planning and actual views along the transition after compiling.
Compare eyes and body while standing, ascending and occupying the level; inspect
opposing and supporting positions, cover effectiveness, access/exit and reversibility.
Repeat with objective ownership reversed. Keep gameplay claims separate from a
successful stair route or a view at the final perch; neither establishes feasible
trades, a safe retreat or a contestable post-plant position. See the
[elevation research revisit](../../../../docs/research/fps-elevation-and-visibility.md)
when concrete mechanisms or counterexamples would help.

For a sustained grade, repeat these checks along its full length. Inspect relative
cover heights and base contact, intermediate views, transitions/headroom and the
return or one-way costs. Endpoint-only views can miss the consequential exposure.

## Interpret sightline warnings

Treat sampled long LOS as a warning for review, not an automatic design failure.
An intentional long lane can support information, utility and route choices when
its exposure and counterplay fit the brief. Repair accidental spawn-to-spawn views
and extreme bare straight channels that bypass those choices. Examine diagonals
and offset positions; center-only checks can miss a shared open channel.

Long lines should connect plausible supported player locations at their real XYZ
elevations. Rays into empty sky or inaccessible building strips do not establish
player-to-player sightlines. Meter-grid scans can prioritize investigation but miss
thin gaps and tangent views. Report admitted surfaces, spacing, posture assumptions,
opacity limits and incomplete budgets; use selected finer probes where needed.

For stationary holds, identify feasible enemy-entry frontiers with width and height,
including alternate approaches. A roughly 100° sector containing all sampled visible
entries is a candidate for review. Use the smallest circular horizontal arc and
inspect vertical spread too. List hidden, partly occluded and unsupported portal
samples separately. A fan of visible directions is not an entrance inventory, and
hidden entries are not proof that every threat is covered.

Judge the hold's practical value: can it watch the objective and every approach
without moving, looking away or needing a teammate? Can opponents change the fight
within useful time? Protected spawn pockets, transit corners and committed ambushes
can be intentional. No rule requires every point to exceed a 100° spread. Document
the reason for retaining a flagged lane or hold; do not change thresholds or omit
an approach merely to clear the report.

## Review distributed low cover

Make ducking and repositioning a deliberate part of the encounter network. Inspect
exposed crossings, bridges, approaches, sites and retreats for useful low refuges,
preparation positions and intermediate cover. A sparse collection of low props is
not enough when players must traverse long exposed stretches with nowhere to duck.
Plan sequences that support movement after contact, with visible costs and responses.
Check integrated low cover such as benches, planters, machinery and retaining edges
as well as crates. Look for a coherent circulation and cover sequence, not repeated
props used to hide an unresolved straight or rectangular layout.

For each consequential low obstacle, compare standing and low-eye exposure from
opposing and elevated positions. Inspect sides, feet and weapon gaps, body clearance,
entry/follower views, passing room and the next protected move. Ask whether the cover
permits a useful peek or creates a stationary tiny-target advantage. Verify actual
crouch/body behavior where supported; a low inspection camera is only an optical
study. Preserve deliberate exposed commitments and avoid cover clutter that creates
snags or excessive hiding checks. Dimensions and spacing follow the actual game,
not a universal crate quota or height.
