# Source-grounded plans and sections

Use plans when stacked spaces, ambiguous interfaces or substantial reconstruction
make them useful. Small maps and diagnostic 3D trials need no mandatory preapproved
2D package. Resolve consequential uncertainty before extensive dependent detail;
choose the representation that reveals the relationship, not a drawing quota.

## Draw the relationships that matter

Use shared axes, origin, units and level elevations. Distinguish measured, estimated
and assumed dimensions. Show usable spaces, floor ownership, voids, stairs and both
landings, wall thickness, thresholds and overhead obstructions relevant to scope.
For multiple levels use aligned sheets or a legible stack diagram; add sections
through clearance-critical overlaps. Conventional door/window and stair symbols
help readability, but do not invent hinge types or tread counts hidden in sources.

Distinguish structural slab footprint from usable width and soffit edge. Include
nearby columns and walls that intrude into the body envelope. Identify section cut
height, profile axes and viewing direction. Hatching denotes cut material; mark
removed cutaway geometry and uncertain boundaries separately from known overhead
edges. Keep closed openings separate from claims about unseen rooms or routes.

Cross-reference consequential assemblies to relevant originals and inspect rendered
drawings at readable size. A polished drawing can still misinterpret a photograph.
For complex uncertain relationships obtain independent review before dependent
production geometry. Reuse accepted drawings for unaffected repairs and update
changed interfaces when they matter. Drawings generated from the candidate can
explain it, but cannot independently validate its source interpretation.

## Verify the plan-to-model translation

Use the shared [conformance audit](../../level-authoring/references/structural-review.md#when-drawings-are-used)
on actual compiled plan cuts and sections at common axes/elevations. Check both
missing intended structure and extra pieces obstructing openings or voids. Keep
one active definition per assembly and retire replaced contributions, including
aliases and child closures. Audit saved/reloaded XML, real stair endpoints/counts
and finite wall/slab joints, not just names or bounding boxes.

If the original evidence contradicts a shared corner or level, reopen the affected
relationships together. Revise the model, or intentionally revise the drawing with
new evidence; do not redraw it from the model just to manufacture agreement. Recheck
source fidelity and physical usability separately after consequential repairs.
