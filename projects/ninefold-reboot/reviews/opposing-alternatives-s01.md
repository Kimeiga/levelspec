## Initial opposing-plan observations

**A / Courtyard Stack.** A defender can begin near Dye/A rear and choose Clinic through S3, while an attacker from Print can either continue through Repair toward S1/Laundry or stay in the lower Lightwell and use S4. Contact near Repair therefore does not identify the eventual objective. The middle gallery provides an intelligible switch, but an opponent controlling Clinic appears able to affect the S4 arrival, the Drying stair and the Tea/gallery connection from neighboring territory. The useful opposing question is whether those are distinct positions with attention costs, not how many connecting arrows exist. B/Tank Court's western Laundry and eastern Drying arrivals permit a planted team to orient in two directions; a defender remaining at Clinic has to climb before directly retaking B.

**B / Loft Street.** At Butcher, the attacker can retreat via Paper to the Underpass, continue Drain/Boiler toward Dye/A, or climb School toward B. At Awning, the Tailor journey can threaten A directly or turn up S2 toward the east B entrance. A defender hearing or seeing a player in the lower street cannot automatically conclude that B is being entered. Conversely, the shown Loft→Lift Well observation may let a B player check a lower rotation from very near the scoring space; its exact aperture is strategically important. CT routes via Dye/S3 and A/Sign/S4 have different ground commitments but converge around East/Boiler before B. They should not be counted as fully separate B retake fronts merely because they begin at different stairs.

## Proposals for the author to compare

### OP-NF-01 — Give B's lower-route information a position cost (experiment)

Affected B areas: Machine Loft south bar around `(52,36,3.2)`, current observation from `(50,40)` into Lift Well, and the east leg near `(58,49,3.2)`. Compare the shown south-edge opening with a **short west-facing observation aperture at the north end of the east Loft leg**, approximately the boundary `x=56, y=48…50`. Keep the remainder opaque. These coordinates identify an experiment, not final wall construction.

Before: a player close to the bomb area may inspect the well while staying close to both site's interior connections. After: a player walks into the east leg to inspect the well's northern exit toward Dye. The information is useful but partial: movement through the Underpass can still reverse or take another continuation without being continuously watched. A teammate must retain the southern bomb space while the observer commits north; a ground opponent can challenge the aperture reciprocally or withdraw below it. This also leaves School-side pressure relevant.

Cost: the site anchor loses immediate lower-well information and some cross-level support. The pocket could become a strong northern hold if its sightline reaches Dye/S3; do not extend the aperture into a panoramic rail. Compare both drawings with two opponent positions (Underpass threshold and Dye-side Well exit) plus a teammate at the bomb area. Later verify actual standing/crouching and follower visibility in compiled geometry. Another reviewer must assess an adopted change's gameplay consequences.

### OP-NF-02 — Make A's Tea observation an optional stop (experiment)

Affected A areas: Tea Landing, the Well Gallery→Clinic through-route, and the view toward Machine Court's A threshold. Put the A-facing observation at a **shallow side recess separated by an opaque return from the gallery circulation**, rather than treating the entire Tea edge as an overlook. Preserve the through-route and ordinary S2 access.

Before: a mobile middle player may acquire A information while rotating between Herb and Clinic. After: checking A requires a short stop and facing away from the gallery continuation. An attacker can show at the south threshold to pin that observer while a teammate pushes S2; a defender can choose to abandon the recess and maintain the Clinic reserve. The recess does not require a new route or another floor.

Cost: less immediate support from the upper route and a possible isolated pocket. A second escape doorway would erase its intended cost; retain the option to omit the recess entirely if local review finds it a trap. Draw the observer, S2 leader/follower and ground A response before deciding.

### OP-NF-03 — Keep the B roof a commitment, not a surveillance shortcut (retain with bounded detail)

Retain Roof Court→Reservoir as an optional reversible change of approach. Keep the proposed Roof→Boiler observation limited to one edge of Boiler; build opaque rooftop service masses so a traveler cannot continuously read Drain, Dye and both objective approaches. In a 3v3 before plant, one T can spend time to reach a changed Loft entry while teammates hold ground pressure; CT can recognize that absence only through earned information and decide whether to push the vacated ground territory. In a 2v2 postplant, the same journey can simply be too expensive.

Cost: the roof contributes less direct fire support and may be infrequently used. That is acceptable if it makes an early rotation meaningful. Compare roof travel against the same-floor Lower route during the next stage; do not shorten it merely to equalize route counts. If it proves to add no viable state beyond decorative travel, removing the roof transfer is a legitimate alternative.
