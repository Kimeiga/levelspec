# Last Light S01 — opposing plans and information

Reviewer: `/root/reboot_opposing_review`. Both alternatives are **promising** for early development. My preference is **B / Twin Atrium Exchange**, because a team can occupy the lower mall without contesting the raised Control floor, then choose a materially different ascent. That creates a useful distinction between ground relocation, visible pressure and actual scoring commitment. It still needs staged ascent/support development; four stair arrows alone do not establish a successful retake.

## Initial observations from the drawings

**A** places a large enclosed store beside public and service loops. The middle core divides the ground objective; a player on Returns balcony or Cinema gallery can support a ground team but cannot score. The two upper entries occur at different ends of the public/service system, so a defeated team can reconsider which floor to use. However, Returns is a substantial dead-end spur: an enemy taking U2 can isolate that player from ordinary descent. It should be a deliberate commitment, not the default place every support must stand.

**B** makes X1 and X3 the obvious atrium ascents, with X2 and X4 reaching the north/south galleries from service-side journeys. The Under-court passage transfers between atria below the zone. A team crossing below can change its ascent, but cannot rescue an expiring point by occupying G1. The diagonal spawns also mean the northern and southern outer routes can put an attacker near an opponent's re-entry area. Those routes should reveal pressure through earned positions; an open view from a winning gallery to a spawn exit would turn regrouping into continual stagger.

The architectural reference observations support shared roofed volumes, substantial store edges and distinct public/service circulation. They do not establish that this particular raised food court or spawn arrangement is balanced.

## Proposed comparisons

### OP-LL-01 — Stage the underpass exits without turning them into extra chokes (B experiment)

Affected G1 `(42…78,44…54,0)`, W2 and E2. Compare the straight passage mouths with **short offset retail-back-wall returns at its atrium interfaces**, retaining a generous shared passage. The returns should make a traveler emerge into the atrium beside an identifiable stair/column mass before obtaining a view of the opponent's deeper spawn approach. Do not enclose the whole atrium or insert a new tiny doorway.

Before: G1 may read as a direct cross-map firing/rotation corridor. After: a player can use it to relocate below the objective, but must clear an atrium threshold and select an ascent before influencing the point. A teammate at the original atrium can hold that exit while the traveler changes side; opponents can pursue into lower territory, at the cost of leaving the raised zone. The new return must not make the traveler emerge into an untradeable trap below an all-seeing balcony.

Cost: reduced lower cross-support and extra clearing time. Compare the unmodified mouths first; adopt only if developed sightlines show a direct rear-pressure problem or an uninteresting tunnel shot. This is a proactive experiment, not an observed S01 optical defect.

### OP-LL-02 — Make re-entry a choice with two distinct departures (B development requirement)

Keep the existing W0→W1/W3 and E0→E1/E3 departure pairs. During enclosure design, make them **two offset vestibule sequences with opaque returns**, rather than two apertures visible from the same atrium/gallery station. A full regroup can then choose public stair pressure or the service journey after seeing an opponent push one side. A winning opponent can contest one exit by entering the corresponding district; controlling the second requires a second position and fewer players on the objective.

Cost: slightly slower re-entry and less immediate cross-support between separated exits. Do not add a deep invulnerable maze or engine barrier assumption. Test each pair from the enemy gallery, atrium and outer-loop approach; a physical minimum exists in the raw plan, but exposure is still unproven. This is consequential for a respawning mode even if both sides receive nominally equivalent routes.

### OP-LL-03 — Compare a shorter Returns commitment (A experiment)

Affected U3 Returns balcony (`x=36…44, y=46…70,+3.6`) and U2. Compare its current long southward spur with a shorter balcony terminating around the store's northern half, using an opaque store fascia at its south end. Keep the continuous U2 gallery and existing X1/X2 stairs.

Before: a support who moves deep into U3 can be cut off by a push onto U2, while still not scoring. After: the useful upper station sits closer to a retreat decision and sees a bounded part of the ground encounter. A tank on the store floor must still choose how far to advance beyond that support; the opponent can fight through the other half of the core rather than automatically climb to remove every upper player.

Cost: less upper influence over the southern ground doorway and less balcony extent. Do not automatically add a third descent to cancel the commitment. Compare the original and shortened spur with ground approach/support pairs; retain the longer version if it offers a worthwhile costly hold rather than a required support position.
