# Independent spatial-interest review — sketch r1

Verdict: **promising**.

Reviewed the user brief (5v5 CT/T defuse in an abandoned medieval European castle) and the actual rendered `sketch.svg` before author design rationale or detailed gameplay assessment. This is an early spatial judgment, not layout or gameplay acceptance.

The memorable journey is the outer ward climb onto the +3 m bridge: the player can leave the west approach before its objective, cross above somebody continuing through lower mid, and descend on the chapel side. Continuing below the same bridge produces a different enclosed-to-open sequence and then a split around the keep. That is a real lateral route during progression, rather than three lanes meeting only at spawns or sites. The rose court, chapel nave and rear cloister establish recognizable destinations around one retained keep mass.

The castle order is plausible at sketch scale: gatehouse into the outer ward, a substantial inaccessible central keep, two distinct courts/building wings, and a rear service/cloister connection. The upper crossing can read as a surviving wall walk between wings; preserve enough supporting masonry and a readable underpass when developed. The larger chapel footprint needs internal architectural definition in the developed layout so it feels like a ruined nave rather than one long strip bearing a themed label.

Preserve the bridge/underpass separation and the mid-to-both-wings choices. These are drawing-development requirements, not a request to replace the composition.
