## Territory and choices

The opaque drum and incomplete levels divide information meaningfully. A control does not imply B access is blocked, and B control does not make A inaccessible. The eastern transfer is a real vertical commitment; the western turning stairs offer a different, contested site-switch route. No position in the drawn middle is credited with universal access or observation. This is sufficiently different from the rejected parallel routes and rear connector: the objective journeys wrap, climb and return at different places.

A has a broad west/south attacking frontier and an eastern defensive frontier. S1 and S2/S3 are separate journeys, but should not be described as opposite-sided A entrances. The derailed car separates northern wrap from inner southern arrival locally. A two-player defense can divide train/site and eastern support, with retreat around Signal; an attacker holding one staircase does not prove the other clear. The wide arc allows repositioning while the train and Signal break some simultaneous views. Their actual mutual exposures require compiled opposing views.

At B, Cable Seam's shorter northwest arrival and Red Loop's southern arrival threaten different sides of the L-shaped collision. The long loop costs roughly a further station curve of travel; it is not an equal-speed rush alternative. Its plausible value is an independently concealed southern threat after a service probe or in a staged split, and a postplant route into the south side of the cars. It does not need equal usage to be useful. A five-player service rush remains possible and should be a priority stress case: the 4m service section and car corner are intended trade/utility contests, not evidence that five bodies can enter simultaneously.

Fan/S4 is the defenders' short rotation commitment. Losing it makes their easy A–B response unavailable and can isolate the two anchors. That is a consequential territorial reward, not automatically a defect: taking Fan requires penetrating B's eastern frontage or crossing A toward the eastern upper tail. A remote western rotation remains possible but is too long to count as a late emergency flank. Neither side gets a safe transverse rear bar.

## Scenario traces

- **5v5 default:** 2A/2B/1flex is plausible with the flex near the eastern upper approach, rather than an all-seeing central perch. Two upper defenders can contest the west train frontier without owning the lower service passage. At B, one plays the eastern/northern car relationship and one protects Fan/plant. The flex's reinforcement has travel cost and leaves the other site weaker; do not promise instant assistance.
- **Five attackers at A:** outer and turning stairs let the group stage at two separated climb interfaces, then arrive on north/south sides of the upper wreck. They still challenge the same broad frontier. Defenders can delay around the train/Signal and retreat east, while a B rotate must climb S4 and travel the east arc. If one A anchor dies early, that rotate should not be counted as already present.
- **Five attackers at B:** Cable Seam gives early pressure; the second player needs a clear supporting view through the Cable-screen corner. The long Red party arrives later, so a full split needs a wait/staging decision. CT can contest the service mouth from the train/Fan relationship or concede part of the bowl, then use the east approach for a paired retake. L02 improves deployment, but actual opening contact is still the highest-priority measurement.
- **3+2 split / defender information:** a service probe can hold B attention while two climb the turning stairs toward A. Hearing or seeing an empty service mouth does not clear Red Loop. A defender push down the outer stair threatens T's original decision area, but commits away from the upper site; the turning staircase can remain occupied. These are tradeoffs rather than free information routes. Audio inference is unverified.
- **3v5 after a site loss:** the remaining CTs cannot cover both plants and both transfer ends. They must choose whether to keep Fan, stage a grouped retake, or preserve the other objective. No theoretical remote western flank is credited as a quick response. The train masses permit some local delay and regrouping without requiring a global extra doorway.
- **2v2 A postplant:** attackers can divide northern train wrap and inner/south preparation. Defenders arriving east can use Signal to separate first appearance, then coordinate their clear. One retaker taking the western stairs is a long commitment and needs explicit clock evidence. The model must check whether a single attacker can see plant, east arrival and both western arrivals from one tiny position; the drawing alone does not prove or disprove it.
- **2v2 B postplant:** the two trains split north/east and south/west views. One attacker covering Fan while another plays the southern plant relationship is plausible. Retakers can work the car corner together; a solo unsupported straight push may be punished. Verify that the train/window/underbody treatment does not introduce unintended slits or an untradeable micro-angle.
- **Isolated 1v2 / late switch:** both sites have useful local mass to break a duel or reposition, but a lone defender cannot be expected to hold multiple approach directions indefinitely. Changing objective requires a named transfer and a length of platform; neither side has a magic middle shortcut. Exact late-switch viability depends on the eventual clock and measured route length, so no remaining-time claim is accepted now.
