# Mechanisms from individually reviewed sources

Use these when the corresponding decision matters. They are conditional design
hypotheses, not a feature checklist or consensus established by a large source count.
The [research transfer record](../../../../docs/research/fps-review-transfer.md)
records provenance, incomplete coverage and source corrections.

| Mechanism | Design question and useful comparison | Transfer limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information expires | Compare the opponent's relocation options with the delay from observation to a supported entry. Inspect the scout's body position as well as the view. | A remote drone, direct peek and sound cue have different costs; do not assume another game's equipment. |
| Attacks have dependencies | Draw only consequential parallel jobs and handoffs: clearance, pressure, flank containment, objective interaction. Remove a role or delay a job and compare substitution, bypass and plan change. | Coaching phase durations are not universal optimal schedules. |
| Rotation serves a phase | Identify who must respond, what they know and their remaining task. Compare protected circulation and contested shortcuts before and after the objective changes hands. | Fracture's safer defender return and Nuke's slower inter-site vent address different problems. Neither establishes that rotations should always be faster or safer. |
| Holds have changing value | Compare coverage, support, exit trigger, departure incentive and cost of regaining the position. Test an ally being forced away or an objective becoming urgent. | Some strong ambushes are intentionally committed. Every hold need not have a safe exit. |
| Threat is not frequency | Compare hunting an off-site player with containing their return while advancing. Include the attention demanded by a credible unused flank. | A theoretical late return has no value when the objective resolves first. |
| An opening has a job | Distinguish movement, firing and observation links; examine both initial use and the opponent's later use after territory changes. | Exact setup diagrams, role assignments and success percentages require their own evidence. |
| Control must convert | Show what taking the position actually enables at a site and what remains possible when it is conceded. | An influential tower or central junction can make alternatives irrelevant; extra connections are not inherently beneficial. |

Primary developer examples:

- [Fracture development](https://playvalorant.com/en-gb/news/dev/controlled-ruptures-making-valorant-s-fracture/): protected defender circulation and neutral areas with early-contest, flank and retake jobs. Design rationale, not measured win-rate evidence.
- [Nuke reintroduction](https://www.counter-strike.net/reintroducing_nuke): longer inter-site vent travel and restricted rafters were intended to change control and post-plant response. Historical version, not a description of current geometry.
- [Split development](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/dev/the-creation-of-split/): iterated tower, mid and overwatch influence. Test the conversion of off-site control into objective progress.

The recovered practitioner reviews add information age, parallel tasks and fallback
triggers. Treat them as reasoning to test; their embedded media, exact setups and
historical equipment were not validated by the prior text review.
