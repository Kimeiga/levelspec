## Actual local encounters and strategic implications

**A: broad observation trades against exposed occupation.** `a-east-hold.png` at (39,65,+2.4) confirms an exposed eastern observer can see both attack thresholds inside one modest angular fan. The derrick does not universally require separate observers for the two entrances. `west-head.png` and `short-reverse.png` show the reciprocal approach and continued clearance responsibilities. The observer has no immediate low refuge at that point; Short pressures its eastward/rear withdrawal, and moving toward the toolhouse gives up or changes entry slices. This is a strong hold worth testing, not an established all-seeing safe bunker. The corrected rationale accurately limits the derrick claim. Do not advertise a mandatory two-player A defense solely from the two entry count.

A West attackers can pause at the stair head, then choose the derrick flanks; Short attackers complete a visible final ascent with a broad head landing. The actual stair images support a follower's physical passage, but not a guaranteed rifle trade. A 4+1 execute needs synchronized pressure or utility rather than assuming the two doorways automatically force the defender's attention far apart. A lone A anchor plus a heavier B stack is a plausible adaptation for playtesting; its cost is conceding A more readily when the anchor is displaced.

**B: opaque same-deck cover, with height-dependent limits.** `compressor-exit.png` and `b-west-entry.png` show the separator masses separating south and west entry slices. The sealed pedestals remove foot gaps at same-deck standing/crouched height. The rounded crowns are not full-height rectangular walls: an elevated observer can obtain different strips around their sides. `b-stair-head.png` shows a constrained view through O16 rather than an unobstructed panorama over the complete site. The descending CT must still clear both tank flanks and the west plant region. B-pump is a useful local crouched refuge against its protected direction; `b-pump-low.png` does not establish protection from SB or a south flank.

**Rear retakes: a deliberate concentration, not a second invisible flank.** A retake initially enters through O14; B retake initially descends SB/O16. Both are six-meter thresholds with staging space and physically supported access for a pair. Each can be pre-aimed by a postplant player. A second lower entry requires earned territory or earlier positioning and should not be counted as automatically available within the fuse. The captured spaces support a credible paired retake hypothesis; only target-game utility and combat can establish whether one smoke or one safe hold can deny the remaining clock.

**CT muster: shelter has a circulation cost.** The extended CT-screen hides the recorded north offset in `ct-spawn-offset.png`. Revision-matched `visibility.json` adds 3,960 doorway-to-spawn optical rays across all five CT anchors, body offsets and standing/crouched eye studies, with zero visible pairs. All 36 sampled rear/A baffle-to-lower pairs are blocked. These are bounded samples, not a continuous visibility proof. It leaves approximately 2.10 m clear north and 2.85 m clear south passages; both were walked. That gives two physical ways around the screen but does not demonstrate five-player congestion behavior. A split default can choose passages deliberately; a full-team emergency A rotate should be a body-blocking stress test.

**Low cover and follower qualification.** The targeted optical results confirm V-console and B-pump hide the sampled crouched eye from the sampled standing opponent, while standing eyes remain visible. A-cable, A-reel and P-cart do not hide the sampled crouched eye from the sampled standing opponent; they are partial body shelter and directional position cues, not guaranteed crouched concealment. For SW, the last sampled entrant is fully exposed to its selected A defender while a follower two meters behind still has no view of that defender. SA and SB provide sampled entrant/follower sight earlier, at the cost of exposing the climbing upper body. Side-by-side support, utility and actual trading remain game tests. These observations strengthen the priority of testing the committed West exit and prevent nominal cover height or passage width from being treated as proof of a safe reset.

The source-matched native arrival study adds all 20 spawn-to-site combinations. At 5 m/s, T→A ranges 11.69–12.52 s versus CT→A 6.65–7.85 s; T→B ranges 13.23–14.19 s versus CT→B 7.10–8.43 s. These are navigation-distance estimates to common markers, not measured first-contact or hold-establishment times. They support the intended initial CT site access without proving equipment, clearing or utility margins.

A feasible rear chain through the separately walked anchors A→R→Q→SB-head→SB-foot→B sums to approximately 88.65 m (17.73 s at 5 m/s). This is a conservative anchor chain, not an optimized rear path or a measured uninterrupted multiplayer journey. Against a provisional 40 s fuse it leaves a finite clearing/defusal window, which can shrink substantially with information delay and utility. The existence of that chain is not proof that every late flank is timely.

The independent exported collision consumer reaches both sites from one spawn on each team. Its CT→B result walks approximately 80.00 m in 17.32 s rather than the harness's short rear path. The consumer plans on a 0.5 m standability grid; SB's 0.375 m treads can skip two 0.15 m risers between adjacent grid rows, exceeding its 0.25 m neighbor step test. The longer route is therefore evidence of reachability on that consumer's selected path, not an actual short-rear timing and not proof of a broken SB. The separate browser SB up/down walks resolve the local harness traversal question. Destination-engine collision remains unverified.
