## Stage verdicts

| Alternative | Verdict | Supported spatial opportunity | Main uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Harbor Fold | **Promising** | The working waterfront, market shelf and upper civic district are distinct territories. Market occupation can lead uphill toward A or back down toward B; close visual relationships need not imply short access. | How much of the broad market shelf becomes one shared fight, and whether the eastern two-flight descent is intentionally a commitment or should permit a middle-level change of plan. |
| B — Ravine Quarter | **Promising** | The sites lie on different shoulders. A same-bank ascent, a middle crossing, an upper crossing and a low shore transfer serve different decisions, with inhabited gateways at important arrivals. | Whether bridge control remains a regional advantage or becomes an all-or-nothing lock on cross-town responses; whether the shore investment is useful in realistic round states. |

**Preferred development direction: B, Ravine Quarter.** It changes where a team must commit territory to reach the next objective. The preference follows the traced journeys, not the number of stairs or bridges. A has a strong architectural identity and recoverable market choices, but both sites remain beyond the attacker's progression through the same overall hillside. B more clearly separates approaching a site from changing town shoulders. Neither is accepted as balanced, traversable or adequately enclosed.

## Macro scenarios

### A — Harbor Fold

**5v5, developing an A attack.** An attacker can climb from Ferry/Net Street through S1 or S2 to the middle shelf, then commit to Cypress/S4 and Upper Lane or Market/S5 and Bell Porch. These are different upper arrivals: the western route trades a longer upper-town journey for avoiding the direct market ascent. A teammate retaining Market can threaten the Bell ascent while another group works Cypress. CT can contest Bell, protect the chapel/upper-lane approach, or yield an outer part of the district while retaining the site. Exact timing and coverage are unknown.

**After first market contact.** An attack stopped near Bell need not repeatedly climb S5. It can hold the market boundary and redirect toward Cypress, or drop through S3 toward Taverna/Boat Court. This is the strongest supported change of plan in A. The defender's meaningful response is to decide whether the lost market is still worth contesting or whether to retreat into the upper district and reinforce B via the east descent. A view from above is not itself a route or proof that CT can monitor the whole market.

**5v5, developing a B attack.** The low Fish Quay/Cooper route approaches the boat district while a middle group can descend S3 into the Taverna approach. CT's distinct rear response is the S6a/S6b descent into Harbor Passage. Thus an attack can spend players winning the market to influence a waterfront fight. The Boathouse interior has actual entrances and can eventually make this more than an open dock, but its current shell does not establish a safe hold or a viable plant.

**3v3 after an A probe.** Leaving one player at Market while two return to B asks that player to preserve information or delay pursuit across a real elevation boundary. That is a useful choice if Market contains separable fronts; it becomes dubious if a single upper view surveys both the retained position and the descent. A defender leaving A for B must travel the eastern two-stage stair or surrender a longer interior route. The intermediate +4 m S6 landing is a separate floor island in the raw data: it does **not** connect to Sea Balcony/Market. Do not invent a midpoint escape based on matching height.

**2v2 A postplant.** CT can approach from the upper town while another response may come from Bell or the western ascent, depending on retained territory. A lone attacker cannot assume that holding the chapel's two doors secures the entire terrace; the exterior district remains relevant. The plant position and information cost need development. **2v2 B postplant:** CT's east descent is readable but commits both flight segments before reaching Harbor Passage; an attacker holding the low district may choose to delay that route while a partner watches Taverna. Whether the CT can clear, descend and defuse on the assumed clock is unmeasured. The map should not quietly count the unused +4 m shelf as an available bypass.

### B — Ravine Quarter

**5v5, developing an A attack.** T can gain the west middle district via S1/S2 and reach A through S3/Potter or S4/Press. CT can initially reach the west upper district via Upper Bridge/Inn. An attacker who controls Press can choose the same-bank ascent or contest the middle crossing into the east district; those choices apply pressure to different parts of the defense. CT may hold a west upper threshold, contest Press from the middle, or retain the bridge response and give up an outer part of A. No opening race has been measured.

**5v5, developing a B attack.** T can commit to Fish Quay/Shore Toe/Lower Harbor, staying at ground level, or win Middle Bridge/Taverna and descend S5 or S6 from the east middle shelf. CT starts on the east upper shoulder and must descend through its occupied districts to establish B. Middle control therefore has a concrete reward: it changes the front of a B attack and threatens the routes between CT's upper and lower territories. The lower shore path has a different cost: it offers no intermediate cross-level exit through the scenic ravine. It is a committed transfer, not a chain of little optional connections.

**After contact at Middle Bridge.** If T cannot cross, the west bridge approach can still withdraw toward Press, climb toward A, or retreat to the shore route. This is a change of objective and travel investment, not a free parallel door around the same defender. A CT holding the Taverna end should get useful information about a crossing attempt, but should not automatically know which of those withdrawals was chosen. A bridge that constrains a late rotation can be an intentional territorial consequence; it is not automatically a missing-edge defect.

**3v3 after losses.** An attacker who wins the east middle district can descend toward B or climb S7/S8 to pressure the upper defense. A teammate retaining Press/bridge gives that advance a return route. Conversely, if the middle crossing is lost while the team is divided across shoulders, reconnecting through the upper bridge or shore is costly. This creates a credible reason to preserve the bridge flank instead of sending every player onto the objective. It also requires the developed geometry to provide a place to stop after crossing rather than compel an immediate second fight at the far doorway.

**2v2 A postplant.** CT can attempt the direct upper crossing or cross the middle and use a west ascent, provided it still controls those approaches. Those are different clear-and-commit sequences. One surviving attacker cannot be assumed to watch both bridge axes from West Lookout; the two crossings occupy different heights and longitudinal positions. **2v2 B postplant:** the local east descents are the direct CT responses. A shore flank requires leaving that district and investing in a much longer transfer; it should not be advertised as an emergency retake without measurement. Attackers can hold a low waterfront boundary or spend a player preventing descent, but cannot presume information through a floor or building.

## Concrete proposals

### GA-S01-M1 — Test a middle exit from Harbor Fold's eastern descent

**Type:** proactive alternative, not a missing connection or required repair. **Scope:** A, S6's isolated +4 m turning landing and Sea Balcony.

**Physical change:** Test a roofed +4 m passage from the eastern turning landing westward beneath the upper headland terrace toward Sea Balcony. Treat it as a real passage with a declared floor, enclosure and headroom; do not draw a same-height line through solid terrain and call it connected. Offset its sea-balcony exit behind the headland/sea-house mass. Retain the S6 two-flight route.

**Before → after:** In a 3v3 B response, a CT descending from A currently commits from +8 to ground without an intermediate district exit. The passage lets that player stop at +4, choose to pressure the market, join a teammate there, or continue descending while the teammate threatens S3. An attacker retaining the middle shelf can contest the side exit and learn that the rotation is taking place.

**Opponent response and cost:** T can occupy Sea Balcony to deny the new exchange, or hold Harbor Passage and force the descending player to choose which front to clear. The cost is a substantially more flexible CT response and another approach to the market; it could make earlier T market control too fragile. It also adds a genuine under-terrace construction burden. Compare the lost-market/B-retake scenario with and without this passage before accepting it. Keeping the existing committed descent is a valid outcome.

### GA-S01-M2 — Make Ravine Quarter's bridge arrivals territories, not firing axes

**Type:** opportunity / concentration risk; current sightlines are not verified. **Scope:** B, east Taverna gateway and Inn archway, with the corresponding west receiving streets.

**Physical change:** Reserve an offset receiving porch inside/just beyond each gateway, using the occupied building's depth and a full-height return wall. The far doorway should not align the entire bridge with the next district's stair or court. Preserve the through-building route and a reversible retreat to the bridge. On the west side, use Press-house/West-homes frontage to make a receiving boundary rather than leaving the crossing as an extension of a large undivided court. This is a change to the arrival sequence, not another bridge.

**Before → after:** In a 3v3, crossing Middle Bridge can otherwise become one continuous exposure followed immediately by a fight against the whole east shelf. The porch gives the successful crossing a territorial reward: the group can gather, exchange information and choose Fountain/B pressure or School/upper pressure. The same logic lets a CT returning over Upper Bridge decide how to enter the A district rather than see or be seen through its full approach at once.

**Opponent response and cost:** A defender can hold the next district threshold, contest the porch at close range, or withdraw toward the stair network while a teammate pressures the bridge return. The attacker can retain the porch with one player and redirect the others, but loses tempo clearing it. The cost is another clearing stage and potentially a very strong bridgehead hold. Keep the porch small enough to remain a transitional territory and check that a lone holder cannot observe both the bridge and all district exits without moving.

### GA-S01-M3 — Give the shore commitment an inspectable retreat before B

**Type:** opportunity, not a request to make the scenic ravine traversable. **Scope:** B, Lower Harbor between Shore Toe and the Boat Court approach.

**Physical change:** Shape the Salt-store/Boat-shed frontage into a sheltered lower-harbor bend before the final Boat Court exposure, with a visible return toward Shore Toe. Keep the approach at ground level and retain the existing district transfer. Do not add a middle-of-ravine stair or shortcut merely to make every point a junction.

**Before → after:** After a two-player shore advance meets a stronger B hold, both players should be able to stop short of the final site exposure, let a teammate's middle approach develop, or retreat along the route they paid to take. The current broad lower-harbor floor does not yet establish this boundary. The bend makes waiting or retreating a spatial decision while preserving the shore route's long commitment.

**Opponent response and cost:** A B defender can push into the lower harbor to deny the pause, but must leave its site-facing hold and become vulnerable to pressure from the east middle descents. The cost is a potential attacker staging pocket and less immediate waterfront visibility. Evaluate a 5v5 split and a 2v2 delayed approach; do not justify it solely by adding another piece of cover.

## Development priorities

Carry forward the chosen district commitments before multiplying connections. Show where a player can stop after first contact, what a teammate must retain for a rotation, and which view actually informs an objective change. For Ravine Quarter, compare both site attacks, a lost-middle 3v3 and each 2v2 postplant; for Harbor Fold, compare the market-to-waterfront change of plan and the two variants of the east descent. Body-only travel and actual viewpoints must later replace the present route hypotheses. No timing, grenade behavior, audible information, continuous movement or competitive balance follows from these S01 drawings.
