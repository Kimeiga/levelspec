## Route and territory reading

### A — Courtyard Stack

At 5v5, an attack can establish Repair/Low Lightwell pressure while another group takes Herb. From Repair, committing through S1 and Laundry threatens B; staying low through Pump or Low Lightwell instead threatens northern service territory or the Dye approach to A. Thus a stopped western advance has a spatially different continuation. The northern ground flank is substantial: it reaches the CT service area without requiring a roof fight. It will need to be evaluated as a territory investment, not assumed to be a harmless perimeter lane.

For **A as the target**, Noodle's covered-street approach and Herb's valve alley arrive from different ground directions. Herb also permits an upper Tea commitment. CT can reinforce from Dye, while pressure won through Low Lightwell can threaten that reinforcement route. A coordinated attack therefore has a reason to spend players away from the immediate site. A defender's response is to concede one outer approach and protect the Dye connection, or contest the middle before it becomes a rear threat. Which response is viable remains unmeasured.

For **B as the target**, Laundry/S5 and Clinic/S6/Drying arrive at opposed roof precincts. A western attack does not receive the east arrival automatically: it must win the middle or make a longer northern ground advance. CT can answer from North Service through S3 into Clinic, but that makes Clinic unusually consequential. The roof itself is comparatively simple; its interest would come from the territory required to reach each side and from the postplant arrangement, not the roof connector's shape.

In a **3v3 after early contact**, an attacker holding Repair may threaten B while a partner stays low, but splitting all the way to Herb is an investment in separation and information. A lone CT moving from A to B has an intelligible route via Dye/North Service/Clinic; loss of Clinic could invalidate that entire response. In a **2v2 postplant on B**, the two roof approaches are readable enough to reason about with few players, but one defender cannot be assumed to check Laundry, middle, and north service in sequence within the bomb time. In a **2v2 postplant on A**, upper Tea offers an alternate information position only if its opening has a bounded view; it must not silently become a way to supervise both the site and the middle rotation.

### B — Loft Street

At 5v5, Butcher supports an early decision among the School ascent, the lower Drain–Boiler route, and Paper Arcade toward the underpass. Those continuations are strategically different: they approach B, pursue CT-side ground territory, or cross below the loft toward Dye. Awning also leads to the underpass, while Tailor postpones the A-versus-B decision until the S2 fork. A teammate can hold a former contact boundary while others change level or district; the plan does not require every withdrawal to return to Canal Gate.

For **A as the target**, Tailor can threaten the front while the lower western advance through Boiler/Dye threatens the rearward connection. CT may contest Dye to deny the split or defend A and retain its rear access to Water Street. Sign Gallery is a plausible alternate response to a ground hold, but its proposed A view must require commitment: a player should not obtain free A surveillance while also occupying the B reinforcement route.

For **B as the target**, School and East give the attackers substantially different approaches. CT has a Dye/S3 route to Boiler Gallery and an A/S4/Sign route toward East. However, both CT continuations currently meet the east side of the same U-shaped loft. Their distinct starting stories are not enough to establish distinct retake fights. The upper roof route can deliver a CT response to School, but it adds two flights and a roof crossing. Its usefulness is an open timing and territory question.

In a **3v3 after Butcher contact**, a pair can keep attention at School while the remaining attacker stays low toward Boiler. That is a useful change of plan only if the loft's lightwell opening does not let the same B hold also stop the lower transfer. In a **3v3 B postplant**, a north Boiler arrival and an east Sign arrival should demand different information and coordination from both sides; otherwise the apparent split reduces to one east-side clearing sequence. In a **2v2 A postplant**, a defender choosing Sign gives up immediate ground presence at Dye or the A rear. That is a worthwhile choice if Sign supplies a bounded approach or view, rather than an effortless overview. In a **2v2 B postplant**, the roof is not yet a credible emergency answer: its route is drawn, but travel, clearing, and remaining objective time have not been measured.

## Concrete proposals

### N9-S01-M1 — Give A's Clinic two consequential territories

**Type:** opportunity / concentration risk, not a demonstrated sightline defect. **Applies:** A, S3/S4/S6 and Clinic.

**Physical proposal:** Separate the S3-to-S6 service transfer from the Clinic forecourt receiving S4 and the Gallery/Tea circulation. Use an enclosed service bay and an opaque elbow between the two, retaining the existing connections. Do not add a new stair or an additional route. Reserve this separation in the next plan so the service transfer is not just another mouth in a single room.

**Before/after scenario:** After attackers win the well ascent in a 3v3, the current single Clinic territory potentially cuts the east B response and the middle exchange together. With two territories, winning the Clinic forecourt pressures that response but still requires taking a second threshold to sever the service transfer. A CT teammate can preserve the roof response while another contests the forecourt.

**Opponent response and cost:** Attackers can keep pressure on the forecourt and attack B via Laundry, forcing the CT to choose which territory to retain. The cost is a more protected CT transfer and additional enclosed depth; this could make the middle gain feel too weak. Compare the same lost-Clinic scenario in the developed plan, including whether attackers gain a real route or information advantage without also receiving the entire roof approach.

### N9-S01-M2 — Separate B's north and east loft arrivals

**Type:** opportunity / approach-equivalence risk. **Applies:** B, Boiler Gallery, East Landing, B Machine Loft.

**Physical proposal:** Reserve a northern receiving bay for the Boiler route and a southeastern production-floor arrival for the East route, divided by a full-height service or machine core. Replace the broad incidental contact between East Landing and the loft's east arm with a deliberate, offset entrance. Preserve reversible movement and an exit before each final threshold. This changes the interior relationship; it does not require another graph edge.

**Before/after scenario:** In a B 3v3 retake, Dye/S3 and A/S4/Sign currently both feed the east arm. One piece of east-arm information might settle both approaches. Separated receiving volumes let a teammate establish one front while the other group pressures another part of the planted space. The plant team must decide which area to retain and which information to sacrifice.

**Opponent response and cost:** Plant defenders can concede a receiving bay, hold the next threshold, or fall toward School while a teammate watches the other front. The cost is more clearing depth and potentially too many simultaneous angles on the plant. The next plan should demonstrate a plant/hold position with a deliberate exposure choice, not require the planter or a lone late defender to watch all fronts at once. The core must not merely create two doors a step apart around one obstacle.

### N9-S01-M3 — Make B's cross-level information a commitment

**Type:** opportunity; the dashed blue view is a hypothesis, not verified visibility. **Applies:** B loft, Lift Lightwell and ground underpass–Dye continuation.

**Physical proposal:** Keep the underpass ceiling and most of the loft/lightwell boundary opaque. Place one short, bounded observation opening toward the School-side shoulder, offset from the straight lower continuation to Dye. Give that opening its own approach within the loft, so watching below requires leaving the principal east-arrival watch. Retain the visible stacked volume; do not turn the entire loft edge into an overlook.

**Before/after scenario:** Following a failed School probe in a 3v3, attackers should be able to stay low and attempt a Boiler/Dye change of plan. If a B anchor sees the lower transit while holding the ordinary loft fight, that choice can disappear. A committed observation pocket instead lets a defender request a teammate's cover to gather lower-route information; attackers can occupy the corresponding upper front or threaten the pocket from the lower route before transferring.

**Opponent response and cost:** A defending pair can intentionally trade coverage for information, while the attackers can fake a lower presence and continue toward another site. The cost is reduced continuous visibility of the attractive stacked space and a possible isolated lookout. Review the pocket's return route and whether its information actually changes a decision. No audio, penetration, smoke or cross-level aiming behavior is established by this proposal.

## Questions for the selected layout

- Assign B's roof transfer a primary use: an early territorial flank, a delayed rotation, or a retake option. Measure that chosen scenario in the compiled blockout before making it shorter or adding more links. A route can be valuable without being a viable 40-second emergency retake.
- Draw the site frontiers and the places a team can safely change its plan after contact. For A this includes Repair/Low Well and Herb/Tea; for B it includes Butcher/School/Boiler and Tailor/East. The result should expose what a teammate must hold for the change to remain possible.
- Carry both objective states and the reduced-player cases into the next review. Do not accept B solely because its overlapping floors look more complex, or reject A because its upper route is easier to read.

No finished cover, utility lineup, spawn timing, physical stair fit, visibility or population balance is accepted at S01. Those are later evidence tasks; the current packet is sufficient to select and develop a direction.
