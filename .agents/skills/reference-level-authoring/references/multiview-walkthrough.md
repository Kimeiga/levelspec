# Cross-view structure and human-scale inspection

## Reconcile different original angles

Identify the same physical assemblies using distinctive neighboring features;
repeated circles, door shapes or tread lines alone may be ambiguous. Record the
relevant views for major levels, stairs, landings and wall planes. A second view
should test depth/ownership rather than repeat nearly identical pixels. When only
one view exists, retain the resulting limits instead of inventing depth certainty.

Test main-photo interpretations against other relevant originals before extensive
dependent detail. Separate focal-length/crop/camera uncertainty from geometry.
Check whole-image depth and overlap before refining local boundaries. Unobserved
continuations remain unknown; source-visible connections still need coherent
support even when their exact dimensions are estimated.

## Inspect actual candidate geometry

Use multiple rendered sides, plans, sections or cutaways to expose the whole modeled
volume. Label diagnostic unmatched views. For numerical overlays, fit cameras with
stable distributed landmarks, lock the fit and test held-out boundaries. Do not
warp images or tune the camera to conceal the feature being evaluated.

Follow the shared [human-scale review](../../level-authoring/references/structural-review.md#follow-the-person)
for actual collision-aware routes. Add recognizable source-linked stops where useful.
Separate valid starts for disconnected portions are route audits, not evidence of
an unseen connection. A clear sampled route cannot excuse source-contradicting
geometry elsewhere. Regenerate affected captures after material repairs and retain
the exact candidate identity, camera state, observed results and remaining limits.
