## Initial observations

- D1 is a broad, direct four-meter descent near the southwest arrival. Its lower mouth at approximately(−22,−20,−4) meets a usable west-side strip around H1. This can support a quick committed entry and a follower. Its straightness is not itself a defect.
- D2 climbs from the basin toward approximately(10,8,0). The two-meter interval to the hall wall at x=12 ends in the opening spanning y=6..14. The stair axis and opening are aligned: a hall-side hold may see deep into the ascent before the climber can choose how to enter the hall. That is a prediction from the drawing, not a verified ray result.
- A at(−12,18,−4) is beyond H1's northern tip. The hull can divide approaches from the south, but the objective's relationship to the northern rim remains unresolved. Rim guards and the now-stated hull height must be included before judging its exposure.
- The Rochefort photograph shows flights and landings embedded in substantial retaining masses. Those returns create successive reveals. It does not establish a need to turn both Tidebreak descents into switchbacks, nor does it establish dimensions or shooter balance.

## D-M1 — offset the D2-to-hall threshold

**Type:** design experiment; recommended selected change for R02.

**Physical delta:** retain the D2 flight and top mouth at(10,8,0) for the first comparison. Close the hall's west opening at x=12,y6..14 and provide a four-meter opening at x=12,y0..4, with ordinary standing clearance. Preserve the other southern hall entrance. This changes the wall/threshold relationship rather than adding a freestanding cover box. The exposed exterior apron still connects northward to the winch/dock-head territory.

**Before:** a player climbing D2 faces an approximately aligned view through the west hall opening. A hall defender can potentially threaten the ascent while also occupying the H2-side work apron. A following player is still on the same climbing axis.

**After:** the lead player completes the ascent into the exterior apron, then chooses whether to turn south toward the hall, move north toward the winch court, or return below. Entering the hall requires a second threshold at H2's west shoulder. A follower can occupy the upper landing/apron and support that turn once they reach grade; they do not automatically have a trading view from the lower flight. An opponent can hold the new threshold, pressure the exterior apron from the north, or threaten G1's lower access instead of watching the entire D2 stair from one hall position.

**Sacrifice/risk:** approximately 4–8m additional travel toward the hall, depending on body path; at 3.4 m/s this is roughly 1–2.5 s of movement before stopping/clearing costs. The turn can isolate the first player if they leave the follower behind. It may create a strong close corner at the new opening or an undesirable interaction with the relocated internal G1. This is not a universal safety improvement and should not erase upper-yard pressure.

**Comparison:** freeze the aligned R01 opening and the offset R02 opening as two local variants. With identical bodies, inspect D2 ascent, its upper mouth, the exterior apron, the new opening from both sides, and G1 lower/middle positions. Use a lead player plus a follower spaced roughly 2–3 m apart; observe when each can see the same opponent and when neither can cover the other's next step. Test retreat from hall contact to D2 as well as entry. Reject or reshape the offset if the only result is a compulsory close ambush with no meaningful information or teammate contribution.

**Shared-interface ownership:** dock reviewer checks ascent/landing/approach; spatial/gallery reviewer checks hall-side clearance, G1 interaction, opposing views and acceptance. Since this reviewer proposed the change, a different reviewer must assess its adopted gameplay consequence.

The separate suggestion to end D2 atx8, leaving a four-meter apron, is a useful follow-up if the compiled two-meter apron actually causes body/support congestion. Do not combine it automatically with this first experiment: keeping the flight fixed makes the threshold's effect easier to assess.

## D-M2 — move A onto the west bow shoulder

**Type:** optional experiment, deferred until compiled rim/hull views exist.

**Physical delta:** compare the current A center(−12,18,−4) with a provisional center near(−18,11,−4), on H1's west bow shoulder. Confirm the actual objective interaction footprint fits the clear floor; no such footprint is specified yet. Keep both existing descents and the rounded dock head.

**Before:** occupying A commits the player beyond the hull tip, where the north rim and east-side arrival may both matter. H1 separates the southern approaches but may not shelter the objective interaction.

**After:** the hull shoulder can separate an eastern D2 threat from the western approach. One teammate can contest the next bow turn while another holds the objective side. An opponent may take the rim, pressure D1's side, or wrap around the bow; simply standing at D2 should not reveal every action at A.

**Sacrifice/risk:** shorter access from the D1 side and less immediate influence from the eastern approach. The combination of a tall H1, a 1.2 m rim guard and the near-wall position could make A excessively concealed or hard to retake. This is why it should not be adopted merely because the plan looks exposed. It also changes macro objective access and needs that reviewer's assessment.

**Comparison:** first inspect actual standing/crouching bodies at current A against north, northwest and east rim positions and against both hull shoulders. Then inspect the proposed shoulder position with identical geometry. Keep the current location if its exposure is purposeful and a lower team can use the hull to contest the next action. Move it only if the observed interaction is dominated by unrelated rim sectors without a viable local response under the stated mechanics.

## D-M3 — a lower D1 retaining return

**Type:** optional architectural/combat experiment, deferred.

**Physical delta:** if compiled evidence shows an unbroken lower-floor shot from the bow zone into D1's mouth, compare a retaining return extending approximately x[−22,−18],y[−18,−17.2], from dock floor−4 to grade0. It attaches to the west retaining construction. Keep a clear route around its eastern tip and the hull shoulder; do not add another stair or tunnel.

**Before:** the lead player may emerge from D1 directly into a long lower-floor line toward A. The follower shares the descent but may be exposed to the same distant hold.

**After:** the return creates a brief lower landing decision before the west-hull approach. The leader can test the return tip while a follower watches the stern-side wrap or waits to support the same corner. An opponent can push the rim or move closer to contest the return instead of retaining the original long hold.

**Sacrifice/risk:** a predictable blind corner, reduced immediate follower fire through the return, and a tighter west-hull route. It may merely replace a long contest with a close ambush. Combined with D-M2 it could protect the western plan too much. Do not adopt both by default.

**Comparison:** verify full-body clearance around the return tip, standing/crouching exposure from the rim and bow, a two-player ascent/retreat, and whether the opponent can contest the landing without needing unknown utility. Keep D1's current direct form if it already supplies a legible, supported commitment.
