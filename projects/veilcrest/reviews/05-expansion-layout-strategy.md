## Macro fronts and five-player decisions

The original site relationship remains comprehensible, but neither side can treat the rear as permanently safe. West Garden→Orangery→Music supports an A-west hit or a longer Gallery→Carriage infiltration. East Drain→Pump→Wine→Cellar supports B-east pressure or a separate stair toward the Carriage Court. Each rear infiltration requires crossing a room and defended threshold; it does not provide a free public shortcut. CT can hold those forward wings, concede them and watch the court entrances, or use site-side doors to contest them after information. Each choice costs attention or players elsewhere.

With five CT, a 2 A / 2 B / 1 flex setup can retain site crossfires while the fifth probes a wing/library. It cannot assume persistent control of both outer wings. A 1 A / 1 Music / 2 B / 1 Cellar setup buys earlier rear warnings but gives T more interior space and leaves the A anchor vulnerable to a synchronized garden/library split. Those are plausible distinct choices. An absent wing defender is not automatically a design failure: attackers must still spend travel time and leave part of their team far from the plant. Without implemented sound or utility, a claim that a single observer can reliably detect every passing flank would be unsupported.

T can show garden pressure then choose the Music flank instead of immediately committing to A, or show B drain while a pair works through Pump/Wine. A team sending a player on both outer loops has only three left for the first site contest. That cost matters against two-anchor crossfires. A five-player rush through one wing trades the direct route for isolation and long reset travel; it need not be equally fast or equally useful as every other plan.

The library revision is consequential. Its north/east walls stay closed, so winning Hall no longer immediately yields a court bypass. South entry x52–58 leads around a 12×12 m opaque core to the west A door y70–76. The old Hall→A south portal is sealed, leaving four A frontiers rather than five. The east/north portion of the ring is an optional longer side-clear or lurk position; most direct Hall→A travel will use its west aisle. That asymmetry is acceptable, but the long ring should not be advertised as an equally quick rotation.

## Local fights and ownership changes

**A / west wing.** A now faces garden south, music west, library east and staff-court north. Two CT can allocate a southern/eastern crossfire and request Music information, but a lone anchor cannot watch all four cardinal arrivals. T earning Music can attack the west door or continue toward the court, forcing a defender response. The choice is meaningful because continuing north forfeits immediate plant support and requires another threshold. The existing central sculpture divides parts of the plant footprint; it is not evidence that all new west/east views are mutually screened. Compile opposing west/library/north views and check the surviving anchor's retreat through the north door.

**Library / hall.** T taking Hall gains the shorter interior site choice, but Library adds a real threshold and corner-clear before A. CT cannot claim the old direct hall arch for either initial pressure or retakes. With two T entering, one can hold the core's west shoulder while the other clears ahead; taking both sides of the ring separates the pair for longer. CT pushing back from A east can retreat through the same door, but prolonged library fighting consumes an A defender while Music/Garden remain live. The closed north wall prevents a library player simply turning into Staff Court behind both sites.

**B / cellar wing.** B now has service west, drain south, cellar east and old north stair. The cistern still affects central plant views, but its old south/west interaction does not establish separation against the new east door. CT can contest Cellar from the northern stair, or defend B while conceding the broad cellar floor and waiting for the east threshold. A T pair taking Cellar must choose whether to attack B or climb toward CT rear territory. A single cellar lurker can threaten a reinforcement but cannot simultaneously assist the plant through the wine route. Inspect the east entry, rack shoulders and north-stair exposure with both sites' ownership reversed.

**Courts / rear infiltration.** The Staff Court retains the relatively short A↔B route; Carriage Court is the expanded wing-distribution and spawn area. Rear Hall is the main direct spawn-to-site link, so it will have meaningful initial traffic. Wing attackers reaching the Carriage Court can threaten that link and cut subsequent rotations, but must earn those positions through Music/Gallery or Cellar/Stair. CT should have enough room to reverse their route around the 3 m gatehouse aisles; body blocking and team traffic are untested.

The offset gatehouses interrupt horizontal court rays, but a plan-level candidate full-length diagonal remains: approximately `[6.6,94.6,3]` to `[113.4,101.2,3]`. Its center ray passes just south of the west pavilion and north of the east pavilion. This is earned rear-territory exposure, not an initial T↔CT spawn line. The dedicated visibility scope should adjudicate actual supported positions, wall thickness and opposing views. Until then, qualify the claim that the pavilions remove every continuous 108 m optical channel. A focused pavilion/interface repair is preferable if compiled evidence finds an extreme bare lane; no strategy requirement favors preserving that particular diagonal.

## First-contact estimates and timing risk

These estimates sum specified XY waypoints and divide by 3.4 m/s; they are not executed movement and omit clearing/fighting. Source-backed candidate paths give:

- CT `[59,98]` through Rear Hall/Staff Court to A `[39,70]`: about 45.3 m / 13.3 s.
- T `[58,11]` through front foyer, Hall and Library to A: about 87.5 m / 25.7 s.
- CT `[59,98]` through Rear Hall and old north B route to `[83,55]`: about 68.0 m / 20.0 s.
- T `[58,11]` through Drain to B `[83,55]`: about 80.6 m / 23.7 s; from eastern spawn `[68,14]`, about 70.4 m / 20.7 s.
- CT west assignment `[55,98]` to Music near `[11,78]`: about 59.1 m / 17.4 s; east assignment `[71,98]` to Cellar near `[110,72]`: about 65.6 m / 19.3 s.

B's fastest opposing paths therefore reach their northern/southern thresholds at roughly the same early period (around 16 s in these estimates). Do not carry over the old assumption that every CT has a generous uncontested B setup. This is a valid initial contest hypothesis for production, but it must be measured from all spawn offsets. Test whether defenders can occupy a useful B position or deliberately concede its southern apron, and whether that concession still permits objective defense. A retains a clearer CT setup interval. The longer A library entry and optional outer loops mean a split requires synchronization rather than assumed equal arrival times.

The unchanged Staff Court site route was about 25.5 s in r05; it is only a prior-revision indication until remeasured. A distant outer-wing rotation can easily exceed the 40 s plant assumption. Such a route is useful for early flanks and pre-plant map control, not a credible late post-plant response.

## Post-plant / casualty scenarios

For 2v2 A, T can split garden/music and library/north depending on earned territory. CT already near north and library may attempt separated entries, but a CT still in the far east wing cannot be counted as timely support. If T took Carriage Court, a CT arriving via Rear Hall may first have to fight a cutoff before reaching the north site arch. The objective still forces T to expose to a defuse; an isolated 1v2 should consider a tap or one committed flank, not a tour of the full west wing.

For 2v2 B, T cistern/cellar or drain/cellar control gives post-plant support from two directions and can punish north-stair entrants. CT north/service pressure can split those tasks if already staged near the thresholds. A CT stranded in Music or the far Carriage west end may be unable to return and defuse inside 40 s, especially without a kit. Losing site-side control has a real clock cost. This is an intentional strategic risk, not evidence that every retake is feasible. Low-utility 1v2 tests should start from actual attainable positions and include both kit/no-kit outcomes.
