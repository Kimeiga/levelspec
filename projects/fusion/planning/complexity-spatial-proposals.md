# Fusion — independent spatial proposals for the expanded brief

Status: **two serious alternatives for drawing and independent comparison**, not an accepted production layout. The user now wants substantially more layout complexity, shortcuts and verticality. B04 is the starting geometry, not the extent to preserve. No AML was edited for these proposals.

I reread the current plan and actual vessel/assembly photographs before proposing these organizations. The references suggest temporary decks inside the vessel, service galleries around the pit, freight voids and transfers between occupied levels. Those relationships are stronger material for this request than adding many isolated rooms.

Coordinates remain meters, Y north, reactor centre (0,4). Preserve vessel body Ø19.4 × 11.4m, the inferred D section, 18 coil stations and each installed coil's 9 × 17m envelope. Different unfinished installation states are allowed; shrinking a coil to fit a walkway is not. Both alternatives retain 5v5 and two objectives. Proposed dimensions are design reservations, not compiled clearance claims.

## Alternative A — Three inhabited layers, ground objectives

**Promising, comparatively direct continuation of B04.** Keep A and B at Z0, but make the middle of the map and its approaches a woven three-level structure: a lower service transfer at −3.2, equatorial/wing routes at 0, and partial vessel/wing walks at +3.2. Retain the local −1.2 bus trench. These are connected occupied routes, not isolated sniper balconies.

### What this changes in play

- The **lower transfer** lets a team abandon a blocked Cask approach, reappear at the apron, or commit toward the east approach. It pays for that flexibility in descent, enclosed travel and re-ascent, and reaches the same contested front of each wing rather than creating protected rear access. A lower exit observer and a ground teammate can coordinate an exit; one player cannot watch all three exits.
- The **upper internal arc** changes how players cross the north vessel. It is slower than staying on the equatorial deck but permits a higher peek into an adjacent bend. The core and inner shell still divide the reactor; a north upper position should not be granted an unqualified view of the south work deck. Leave selected inner-edge openings for lower/upper support, with solid screens at the access landings where simultaneous port and stair threats would otherwise stack.
- The **freight void** allows a ground Cask pair, a lower scout and a gallery player to act in the same room. A small suspended crossing gives a quick ground-side change but exposes the player to a different level. Freight equipment should create two local windows across the void, not a 360° fighting balcony. Remove the gallery support player in the review and test whether the lower player has a usable retreat.
- The **east upper route** offers a new B entry elevation and a view toward the lower port/bypass junction. Its B landing should see only a bounded west portion of the site; the plinth and an overhead equipment screen separate the plant area. A teammate on the trench side can pressure the upper defender's attention. The east route is a long parallel walk, distinct from the west's crossing above a freight void.

This alternative adds several route decisions without requiring a completely new opening balance. Its main risk is **too many ways to bypass the same reactor contest**. In the first drawing, compare A10 present/absent: the upper west transfer is the easiest link to omit if the lower service route and two internal ascents already supply enough choice. Do not solve that risk by deleting the entire lower layer before seeing the organization.

A partial outer transfer gallery at +6.4 and a partial lower internal vessel walk at −3.2 create very different upper and lower ways to change sides. This alternative deserves its own drawing and strategy review.

Permit modest wing expansion to x≈±43 and south y≈−40 where necessary for real stairs, freight bays and preparation rooms. Do not merely enlarge the old rectangles; build the height relationships first.

### What this changes in play

The choice of objective becomes a choice of vertical commitment. A approaches climb into a shared freight volume; B approaches descend through equipment and floor openings. The ground reactor route remains the simplest cross-map connection but no longer delivers both objectives at its own elevation.

The lower vessel route creates a consequential west-to-B shortcut. From the western freight room, a team can switch toward B without first clearing the equatorial ports. It still has to obtain the lower west threshold and emerge through B's west work room. If that room is held, the lower team can reverse toward freight or use its intermediate ground exit; it does not have a direct ladder into defender rear travel. Losing this lower territory should therefore change the B defense's attention assignment.

The crown costs two rises from most ground starts. It earns bounded information about the outer port landings and freight arrivals, and a new A retake direction, but installed coils and full-height service cabinets should divide its views. At +6.4 it is **not** automatically above the 10m-tall coil tops; do not draw a panoramic firing line across the reactor. The crescent's long travel is part of its commitment. A suppressed or absent crown teammate must leave a practical ground or lower alternative rather than strand the whole plan.

B's open basin can support ground-to-lower teamwork, but exposed lower players cannot be asked to contest an entire upper perimeter. Reserve a substantial lower room under the retained ground shelf, connected to the open installation void by two local thresholds. A ground player may see a crossing or exit while being unable to watch the plant point directly. Preserve a real firing opportunity across the void; enclosing the whole basin would lose the purpose of the height change.

A's freight mezzanine should span part of a lower volume, with a break in its edge and a crossing toward the existing gallery. Its three stair arrivals can then be separated by cask tooling and structural projections rather than by three cloned corridor mouths. Inspect upper defenders against the lower stair leader and follower at each landing.

The main risk is **vertical travel making a mathematically available retake arrive too late**, especially crown → B. That is why B11 deliberately reconnects through an intermediate ground route and why the ordinary ground/ring network remains intact. This alternative needs fresh spawn and objective timing studies; B04's accepted opening distances cannot be carried over.

## Shared physical feasibility and next drawing questions

I independently sampled the current inferred inner section: at Z−3.2 its radial interior is approximately 3.80..7.55m; at Z+5.6, the top of a 2.4m clearance volume above a +3.2 floor, it is approximately 3.82..7.55m. These are section intersections, before body radius, rails, shell seams, slabs, supports or ports. They make the proposed R4.5..6.5 upper and R4.6..6.8 lower reservations plausible, not verified. Parent's separate height-feasibility sample is consistent with stacking being worth testing.

A lower floor at −3.2 beneath the existing .30m equatorial slab has nominal 2.9m clear height. A +3.2 upper slab leaves nominal 2.9m below it. Actual curved-shell and stair interference still govern the walkable width. Keep supports out of the reserved paths and show the section through every transfer.

- Draw the two organizations separately with floor openings visible at every elevation. Show actual stair runs and intermediate landings, not arrow-only level changes.
- For internal curved ascents, inspect outer ground passage, stair leader/follower views, upper arrival and reverse approach; do not assume the curve blocks or preserves support.
- For lower ports, test the full bridge through vessel, poloidal/TF coils, cryostat and bioshield. Cut plausible unfinished apertures in enclosures, not the magnet envelopes. A bridge that fits in plan may still strike a lower coil.
- For the crown, enlarge/raise the relevant occupied hall and wing roofs. Do not leave new +6.4 floors beneath existing +7.2 roofs.
- No route requires a jump, crouch, ladder, lift or drop. Optional versions can be studied later once engine mechanics exist; the complex layout must already work through stairs and floors.
- Compare initial teams, a suppressed support player, 3v3 after first contact and a 2v2 post-plant. Complexity should produce decisions, readable commitments and useful teammate contributions rather than ten simultaneous mandatory watches.

**Recommendation for comparison:** draw A as the stronger continuity option and B as the stronger transformation option. Do not merge all their links before review; that would erase their distinct costs and make the reactor a minor choice among too many protected bypasses.
