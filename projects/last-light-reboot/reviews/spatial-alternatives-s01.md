## Initial comparison and verdicts

**A / Department After Hours: promising.** The large department store has its own enclosure and four substantial entrances. A public promenade and a southern service belt continue past it, so players can choose a storefront approach or move around the occupied store before committing. The upper route is continuous across the north front and reaches a Returns balcony inside the store: this is a spatial change from the old isolated opposing lofts. The store core, western return and cinema threshold can create distinguishable frontages.

Its main risk is that most of the interesting mall identity currently sits outside a very large central fight room. If the developed interior is only an opaque center box plus low rectangles, the reboot would still feel like a large arena surrounded by route labels. The department-store floor needs actual divisions of depth and enclosure: sales floor, service threshold and mezzanine relationship. This need not become a maze of small shops. A connected retail hall can work if its frontage, bay structure and gallery are legible.

**B / Twin Atrium Exchange: promising; preferred spatial organization.** West and East atria are different volumes on opposite sides of a real low passage beneath the scoring floor. Grand and service ascents lead to different upper interfaces. A player below Control can cross under it, change which atrium they enter, and select a new ascent; that is more than a decorative bridge. The food-court floor is both an occupied destination and a connection between upper galleries. This is a stronger departure from the original's two similarly shaped wings around a small court.

The proposal has an architectural reason for its layering, but that reason must survive production. The lower passage should read as a mall transfer under a thick occupied floor, with enclosed service/store volumes beside it, while the atria remain tall roofed spaces. A slab ring with unrelated stair bars would lose the idea. The early diagram's four upward routes do not establish that a grounded retake has four useful choices; the upper holders' sightlines and the cost of changing ascent remain open.

## What remains open

The original overview is a cutaway-like view; I do not infer missing roofs from it. Both new proposals explicitly require modeled roof/skylight enclosure. Production review must see roof-on mass plus interior opposing views, not merely a top-down slab arrangement.

Before production, define actual wall/guard/opening boundaries, store depth, stair landing geometry, stair/upper guard sections and the lower transfer's visible exits. For B, the strategy pass must address grounded regroup/retake when the objective is upstairs, including 2v5 stagger and support separation. Both pass the **promising** early gate; neither has detailed layout, continuous movement, combat balance or compiled architectural acceptance.
