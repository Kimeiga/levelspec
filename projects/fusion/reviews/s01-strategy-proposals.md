# Fusion S01 — strategy and opposing-plan proposals

Date: 2026-11-11. Reviewer: `skill_forward_review`.
Inspected: `planning/brief.md`, `candidates-s01.png`, the drawing source's coordinate
definitions, and `references/assembly-0.webp`. Read before sibling verdicts or an
author preference. This pass covers strategic organization and useful local
opportunities at sketch stage; it is not detailed layout acceptance.

## Candidate comparison

**A, Port-hall weave, is the strongest strategic starting point.** Receiving feeds
the cleanlock/cask journey, cooling/bus-trench journey and the reactor's southern
port. The west and east vessel ports give the circular interior a consequential
job: change the side or direction of pressure after entering the machine. The
rear-services band can offer defenders a response path distinct from that contested
interior. The west gallery is confined to one interface instead of automatically
providing an overhead circuit around both sites. These relationships are visible
in the sketch, although the detailed doorways and boundaries remain unresolved.

The main risk is early convergence: if each vessel port and its neighboring outer
route join at the same final doorway, the reactor becomes a scenic detour rather
than a second attack relationship. A second risk is the rear band granting one
position immediate influence over both objective interactions. Neither is established
by this early drawing; both should shape the next plan.

**B, Crescent section, offers a distinct elevation experience but less resolved
strategic structure.** A player can rise around the reactor toward the eastern
gallery while another remains in port cells. That could support an unequal-level
attack. However, vessel entrances are not drawn, so the annulus currently supplies
no inspectable transfer into either route. The long crescent also risks making
height ownership the prerequisite for every useful eastern action. Develop a
ground-level alternative and explicit transfer mouths before treating this as an
equivalent strategic candidate. Its continuous ascent is worth retaining as an
optional focused study, not adding wholesale to A.

**C, Assembly nave, has the most direct shared-volume opposition.** The reactor
and detached sector can create different sides of a common battle space, with
the lift gallery changing the route between them. Yet both objectives currently
sit in the same broadly open nave and the gallery runs a long shared edge. Control
near the detached sector may influence the cross-map passage and B at once. No
vessel entrance is drawn here either. C would need substantial internal sightline
and staging development to prevent one front from resolving both objectives.

Recommendation: develop A while retaining C's detached-sector tooling as a possible
local spatial mass, if its actual shape improves an encounter. Do not import C's
entire broad nave or B's continuous gallery merely to accumulate features.

## Concrete proposals for A

All coordinates below refer to the provisional candidate-A drawing frame, in metres.
They locate the proposal; they are not approved collision boundaries.

### S01-S1 — Preserve different site entries from vessel and outer routes

- **Affected:** west port around `(-18, 3.5)`, Hot Cell site A near `(-30, 14)`,
  cask lane, east port around `(20, 4.5)`, Bus Trench site B near `(32, 16)`.
- **Physical arrangement:** let cask lane reach Hot Cell from its southern side,
  while the west port enters a distinct eastern work bay before the same site.
  Give the east port a western Bus Trench approach distinct from the southern
  Cooling approach. Keep an actual work volume or equipment mass between the
  two arrivals; do not merge them into a single short final hall.
- **Situation:** two attackers gaining the vessel can threaten a different face
  of a hold while an outer-route teammate maintains pressure. If that teammate is
  suppressed, the pair must clear independently, rejoin them, or switch ports.
  Defenders can trade which arrival to contest and whether to concede the forward bay.
- **Cost/risk:** multiple useful angles can overload site defenders if both arrive
  without warning or cross the same hold at once. The next drawing should bound
  the angle from each mouth and give nearby control a concrete information role.
- **Test:** trace the same hold against outer-only, vessel-only and coordinated
  attacks, then remove the outer supporting player. Compare meaningful decisions,
  not simply the number of doorways.

### S01-S2 — Keep the reactor's two arcs strategically different through access

- **Affected:** south vessel port `(0, -12)`, interior annulus, west/east ports.
- **Physical arrangement:** retain the three-port arrangement initially. The
  southern arc contains the receiving junction; the northern arc does not need a
  direct rear-services mouth. This preserves a meaningful difference without
  changing the accurate reactor radius or forcing artificial asymmetry into it.
  Use a real sector-work frame or scaffold termination to make one consequential
  preparation/peek position at the southern junction, leaving standing passage and
  follower view to be checked. Do not scatter screens around every sector.
- **Situation:** from the western port, a team can cross the northern arc to change
  objective pressure without re-entering the southern arrival junction. Taking the
  southern arc instead can contest new arrivals or enable support from Receiving.
  Which is valuable changes as the south junction changes ownership.
- **Cost/risk:** the northern loop can become a very cheap unseen flank. Its return
  must reach an objective in useful time, but it should not place a player directly
  behind both sites' defenders. Its final port bay should be locally contestable.
- **Test:** compare holding the south junction against bypassing it via the north
  arc. Inspect actual curved views: a wide annulus can expose much more of an arc
  than a centerline sketch suggests. Do not count curvature alone as occlusion.

### S01-S3 — Preserve a rear response while changing its arrival problem

- **Affected:** Rear Services at approximately `y=24..33`, Control, both rear site
  interfaces, and the vessel as an alternative transfer.
- **Physical arrangement:** keep the rear band as circulation, with its objective
  exits offset from direct plant sightlines. A player should leave the band to gain
  useful site influence. Avoid adding a central rear window that sees both sites
  or a fourth vessel mouth that makes this band an unchallenged shortcut through
  the entire reactor.
- **Situation:** before planting, the rear route supports a response to credible
  contact while the vessel offers a contested forward alternative. After planting,
  defenders can approach a rear entry, but attackers occupying that site's work bay
  can contest the arrival; the circulation itself need not become exposed end to end.
  A retake can also use a captured vessel port rather than funnel through one rear exit.
- **Cost/risk:** excessive rear speed may erase attacker destination choice;
  excessive delay may make site loss terminal. Neither can be decided from S01.
- **Test:** compare a real attack and a fake, then a plant at each site, using the
  same initial defender allocation and known resources. Time detection, movement,
  destination preparation and objective action separately. Recheck after the flex
  player is lost; two independent responders are not always available.

### S01-S4 — Make the west gallery support one transition, then require commitment

- **Affected:** Cask Lane to the `+3.2 m` west gallery and Hot Cell's eastern bay.
- **Physical arrangement:** bound the gallery's useful aperture to the port-bay
  arrival or one cask crossing. Keep most of its back as service enclosure. A gallery
  occupant should not simultaneously see Receiving, both vessel arcs and the plant.
  Show the ascending player's intermediate exposure and a follower's view around
  the turn; the stair top alone is insufficient.
- **Situation:** a teammate can watch a particular crossing while an entry player
  advances on current information. Once the entry goes beyond that view or an
  opponent contests the gallery, the team must perform a new handoff. After planting,
  test whether the same gallery becomes denial, a retake foothold, or irrelevant.
- **Cost/risk:** a support window can become a nearly compulsory hold, and opening
  it can expose a friendly fallback. Its information job and firing job should be
  explicit rather than assumed identical.
- **Test:** compare the entry with its gallery support present, suppressed and
  absent. Measure information age at commitment. If the observer has to traverse
  the whole stair to exploit their own sighting, a staged teammate or another
  observation may matter more than reducing every travel distance.

## Next packet and scenario priorities

The next plan should resolve the six site-arrival interfaces, the three vessel
mouths, west stair/landing, southern vessel junction and rear exits. Label temporary
decks, nonwalkable machine masses, ground/upper floors and optical apertures separately.
An accurate reactor envelope does not establish usable headroom through its D section.

Use a modest set of scenarios: an outer attack supported from a vessel port; a
vessel pair switching destination after contact; a defender conceding the forward
port bay; and a post-plant retake through rear plus captured-port access. In each,
remove or suppress the named support job and record whether a viable adjustment
still exists. Include a team deliberately bypassing an attractive gallery.

These are selected hypotheses and proposals, not measured timings, navigation passes
or competitive-balance findings. No additional engine, mechanic, universal route
count or forced symmetry is requested.
